NCKU-Tainan & STEAM from Seed

The STEAM from Seed team is a service-oriented group of students from various high schools and universities in Tainan City. Every year, this team conducts science education camps in rural elementary schools in Tainan City. Their contributions to education have earned them recognition for three consecutive years from the Taiwan Ministry of Education's Youth Volunteer Action Competition. This year, our NCKU-Tainan team collaborated with them to integrate life science lessons into the STEAM curriculum, allowing students in rural elementary schools to experience life science in their daily lives and inspiring the next generation of scientific talent.

Ten sessions, ten schools

STEAM? Not only Science, Technology, Engineering, Art, Mathematics, but also Sparking True Enthusiasm And Motivation

Across these 10 activities, we ran science experiments like DNA extraction, water rockets, elephant toothpaste, and a chemical garden—using everyday materials and carefully crafted lessons to show children that science is all around them.

To quantify the curriculum's effectiveness, we used pre/post questionnaires covering students' curiosity about science, interest in science, tolerance for experimental failure, willingness to ask questions, understanding of DNA, and grasp of experimental procedure. Statistical analysis showed significant improvement across all areas (p < 0.001, N=217).

HP safety

We consulted faculty on safety procedures and purchased materials only after school approval. Participants wore safety goggles and gloves, and a medical professional was present at every activity. Most experiments followed the 3R principle (Replace, Recycle, Reduce), reusing equipment and sorting waste properly.

Education, a process of two-way learning

Teaching the same lessons across ages and levels taught us, through trial and error, how to make complex science concrete enough for children to love. Their pure curiosity about the unknown reminded us that researchers, too, must hold onto curiosity in the face of setbacks. What moved us most were the thank-you cards children wrote at the end of each activity—proof they felt our care. For us, education is a two-way learning process, not just knowledge flowing outward.

What we achieve?

Across these 10 activities, we reached 266 children. The camp advanced SDG 3 (Health and Wellbeing), SDG 4 (Quality Education), SDG 5 (Gender Equality), and SDG 10 (Reduced Inequality), and followed the four principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC): non-discrimination, best interests of the child, right to survival and development, and respect for the child's opinion.

Detailed Explanation:

  1. Prohibition of Discrimination: No child is selected or excluded based on background; everyone is treated equally.
  2. Best Interests of the Child: Curriculum design, teaching methods, and communication are all built around the child's interests.
  3. Right to Survival and Development: Activities carry virtually no risk of harm, and the scientific knowledge taught supports children's future development.
  4. Respect children's opinions: We encourage children to speak up in class and patiently answer every question, so they feel heard.

NCKU Workshops

Date: August 2–3  |  Location: National Cheng Kung University, Tainan

Workshop: 我們一起追的科學

This two-day workshop introduced high school students to the spirit of iGEM, synthetic biology, and how science connects with creativity and sustainability through interactive, hands-on activities. Beyond our project and how our team works, we hoped to spark interest in scientific research and build skills in observation, critical thinking, teamwork, and communication.

Activities included ice-breaking games, a World Café session, the Agarose Enzyme Lab, an RPG-style adventure, The Green Cabinet board game, Micro Detective, and brainstorming. Day one opened with ice-breakers, then the World Café introduced iGEM, our team, and our project; the Agarose Enzyme Lab gave participants hands-on experimental work and direct observation of scientific phenomena, and the RPG added exploration and immersion. A highlight was The Green Cabinet, a board game our team built from scratch—idea, cards, production, and testing—that introduced participants to the 17 Sustainable Development Goals in an everyday, relatable way. Day two's Micro Detective explored the microscopic world beyond the naked eye, and a brainstorming session closed things out with open idea-sharing.

We also found room to improve our time management—imprecise estimates meant some sessions ran long while others left gaps to fill, making the pace harder to control. Going forward, we'll plan not just content but how activities flow together, how much time each truly needs, and how to adapt more flexibly to the unexpected.

Feedback reflected a highly positive experience: the workshop averaged 4.6 out of 5 in satisfaction, and students' self-reported understanding of synthetic biology rose substantially, from 2.39 before the workshop to 3.88 after.

Beyond the numerical results, many students also shared thoughtful reflections on their experience. One participant wrote:

“活動內容很創新、豐富，這兩天真的學習很多，也走出了自己的舒適圈，謝謝 iGEM 舉辦了這麼棒的工作坊🥰”
"The activities were innovative and diverse. I learned so much over these two days and even stepped outside my comfort zone. Thank you, iGEM, for organizing such a wonderful workshop! 🥰"

Another participant shared:

“有許多需要團隊合作、溝通協調的部分，我覺得自己的社交能力進步很多😭”
"There were many activities that required teamwork, communication, and coordination. I feel that my social skills improved a lot through this experience. 😭"

These responses reminded us the impact went beyond scientific knowledge—through experiments, games, discussions, and teamwork, students explored new ideas and learned through interaction, while their feedback showed us what worked and what we can still improve.

Thank you to all the high school students whose enthusiasm made these two days so meaningful, and to Future Hall for the venue and the STEAM From Seed initiative for their support. We hope to keep designing activities that let more students experience science through hands-on learning, discover the spirit of iGEM, and grow into people ready to face real-world challenges with curiosity and confidence.

National Xihu Senior High School

Date: May 24, 2026  |  Location: Changhua County

Workshop: 3D玩家出任務

"Sharing science might not transform their lives overnight, but it lights a beacon that guides them toward a broader world."

On May 24, 2026, we hosted an interactive Synthetic Biology and STEM camp at National Xihu Senior High School in Changhua County for 39 students from both the senior and junior high schools. With no gender quotas in registration, 26 of the 39 participants (66.7%) were female—a turnout that reflects how an open, approachable learning environment can naturally support Women in STEM.

To make synthetic biology tangible, our curriculum linked STEAM concepts to our 2026 project, "Capture," using an agar-protease assay built from household materials to model how Pseudomonas aeruginosa degrades epidermal cells' extracellular matrix. Watching the simulated tissue degradation in real time showed students that complex biology can be explored with simple tools.

Beyond the experiments, we ran an Innovation Workshop challenging students to tackle everyday problems with synthetic biology. Watching them sketch and debate ideas on whiteboards mirrored our own iGEM brainstorming—proof these young minds don't lack creativity, just a stage. Giving them that platform revealed real potential as future innovators.

Quantitatively, the camp showed real impact: students rated overall satisfaction at 4.82/5.00, and pre/post surveys showed a highly significant increase in comprehension of synthetic biology and STEM concepts (p < 0.001). The experience was a two-way learning journey for us too, pushing us to translate intricate science into language that resonates with everyone. A single camp won't transform lives overnight, but we believe "the seeds of curiosity planted here will serve as a beacon, guiding these students toward a broader world."

Beyond the numerical results, students also shared thoughtful reflections on their experience. One participant wrote:

"學習到了STEM和合成生物學的一些知識，因為我才高一，其實有一些之前沒聽過，但今天的營隊讓我了解了很多，吸收了很多新知識，我覺得很讚～～💯"
"I learned some things about STEM and synthetic biology. Since I'm only a high school freshman, there were some things I hadn't heard of before, but today's camp taught me a lot and I absorbed a lot of new knowledge. I think it's great!"

Another participant shared:

"了解關於合成生物學的相關知識，也和許多不同想法的人討論、碰撞，不僅可以向成大優秀的學長姐學習，也能和其他同學一起討論，我認為這是對我很大的收穫。"
"Learning about synthetic biology and discussing and exchanging ideas with many people who have different perspectives has been a great gain for me. I can learn from outstanding students at National Cheng Kung University School and also discuss with other students."

Puyan Elementary School

Date: May 25, 2026  |  Location: Changhua County

Workshop: The Ultimate Code of Life

"Science is not merely about finding answers; it is the key that unlocks the hidden blueprint of our world."

Building on our visit to Xihu Senior High School, we traveled to Puyan Elementary School in Changhua County on May 25, 2026, to host "The Ultimate Code of Life," an interactive STEM workshop grounded in SDG 4 (Quality Education) and STEAM principles, aimed at building scientific literacy through equal access and hands-on exploration.

To make genetics intuitive for young learners, our curriculum framed DNA as a "code of life." Gamified activities like "Lego Instruction Manuals" and "Trait Battle Royale" helped students understand how DNA and genes shape the human body and its traits, while a hands-on DNA Extraction Experiment let them isolate genetic material with simple, low-cost tools—demystifying complex biology through direct observation.

Beyond the experiments, we aimed to cultivate curious, active thinkers. Framing DNA as "life's hidden blueprint" encouraged students to question and imagine how genetics connects to healthcare and agriculture. Their enthusiastic debate mirrored our own iGEM brainstorming—proof that young minds simply need a stage to shine.

Designed for 16 students aged 10–11, the workshop showed strong measurable impact: overall satisfaction reached a perfect 5.00/5.00, and pre/post surveys showed comprehension of genetics and STEM concepts rising from 2.81 to 3.50 out of 4.00 (+0.69, t(15) = 3.91, p < 0.01). 93.8% of students said they wanted to join similar activities again.

This was a rewarding two-way exchange, pushing us to translate molecular biology into language that sparks childhood curiosity. A single workshop won't teach everything, but we believe the curiosity planted here will encourage these students to keep exploring.

Anping Junior High School

Date: September 13, 2026  |  Location: Anping District, Tainan

In this iGEM junior high school workshop, we guided students to understand the core concepts of the iGEM competition and synthetic biology, and through the simple hands-on activity of "DNA crude extraction," allowed them to experience firsthand the close connection between science and daily life.

This activity was specially designed for gifted students in the third year of Anping Junior High School. Facing the pressure of college entrance exams, we used a relaxed and humorous interactive approach to guide them in exploring the joy of science. When the experiment was successfully completed, the students excitedly shared their extracted results: "So the knowledge in textbooks can actually be seen with the naked eye!" Promoting science education is not only about guiding students to discover knowledge, but more importantly, inspiring them to apply theory to real life—this is the vision that our team continues to strive for.

Inside Tainan Girls’ High School: From Capture to “What Is Medical School Really Like?”

Date: September 30, 2026  |  Location: Tainan City

On September 30, 2026, we were invited by the Medical Research Club of National Tainan Girls’ Senior High School to meet with 50 club members. The session covered two topics: our 2026 iGEM project, Capture, and the question the students were most curious about: “What is medical school actually like?”

Part 1: Unpacking a Synthetic Biology Project

Each team member introduced the knowledge behind their own subgroup’s work: what plasmids are, how genes are designed and expressed, the core ideas of synthetic biology, and how our hardware was built from concept to prototype. The students were shy at first and hesitant to raise their hands, so we opened an anonymous Google Form where anyone could ask questions freely. We received 31 questions, the most common being details of the gene circuit.

Part 2: Why a Medical Student Joined iGEM

In the second half, our team leader Hank, currently a second-year medical student, shared why he chose medicine, what daily life in medical school looks like, and why a medical student would take part in a synthetic biology competition like iGEM. This part sparked an enthusiastic response: students eagerly asked questions to learn more about the perspective about health insurance and the reality of studying medicine.

Audience

What is iGEM?

Hank

iGEM is short for the International Genetically Engineered Machine Competition — the world’s largest synthetic biology competition, aimed at using synthetic biology to solve problems we actually face in daily life. NCKU-Tainan’s iGEM team has competed since 2016, and has won 8 gold medals and 1 world championship.

Audience

Is high school chemistry important?

Hank

I think chemistry is an extremely important subject — it’s part of basic science, and a lot of physiological reactions and related mechanisms are much easier to understand once you have a handle on the relevant chemistry concepts.

Audience

Would you recommend studying medicine?

Hank

I think as long as you’re passionate about medicine, not afraid of hard work, and enjoy helping people, you’re actually quite well-suited for medical school. And a medical degree opens up a really wide range of paths — you don’t have to become a physician; you could also go into basic science research, for example.

Audience

Are there any fun clubs at NCKU?

Hank

The most fun one is definitely our Synthetic Biology Research Club — haha, just kidding! NCKU actually has a really diverse mix of clubs, from academic ones to sports clubs to service-oriented ones. I’d highly recommend coming to study at NCKU.

Reflection

Our previous education activities mostly guided students through hands-on experiments. This time, we chose a different format: sharing and open dialogue. Through first-hand stories and two-way conversation, students did not just receive information; they could picture their own future studies and careers. We also learned that an anonymous question channel greatly lowers the barrier to participation.

Little Sun Project

Date: August 5  |  Location: Nanxi District, Tainan

Some forms of education focus on guiding children outward to explore a broader world, while others choose to remain rooted in one community, accompanying them day by day as they grow.

This year, our team visited the Little Sun Care Association in Nanxi District, Tainan. Situated in a mountainous region about an hour and a half's drive from the city center, Nanxi has experienced a severe outmigration of its young working-age population, leaving behind a demographic largely composed of children and the elderly.

At the invitation of a supervisor from the Tainan City Education Bureau, we organized a science program tailored for the disadvantaged children at the Association. Through hands-on experiments, we introduced concepts in life sciences, DNA, and photochemistry—transforming complex, abstract knowledge into tangible experiences they could relate to in their daily lives.

To evaluate our impact, we conducted pre- and post-event surveys. As shown in the attached data, this single afternoon of science education led to a statistically significant increase in the children's scientific cognition, curiosity, and confidence (p < 0.05).

If we can introduce a novel experience and open a new window to the world for these children—complementing the long-term, stable support provided by Little Sun—then our encounter takes on profound meaning.

Education is never a short sprint, but a long-term relay race passed between many hands. Little Sun provides the fertile soil for these children to grow safely, and we are honored to serve as one of the bridges connecting them to the fascinating world of life sciences.
