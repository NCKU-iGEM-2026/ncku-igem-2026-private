NCKU-Tainan & STEAM from Seed

The STEAM from Seed team is a service-oriented group of students from various high schools and universities in Tainan City. Every year, this team conducts science education camps in rural elementary schools in Tainan City. Their contributions to education have earned them recognition for three consecutive years from the Taiwan Ministry of Education's Youth Volunteer Action Competition. This year, our NCKU-Tainan team collaborated with them to integrate life science lessons into the STEAM curriculum, allowing students in rural elementary schools to experience life science in their daily lives and inspiring the next generation of scientific talent.

STEAM? Not only Science, Technology, Engineering, Art, Mathematics, but also Sparking True Enthusiasm And Motivation

In these 10 activities, we arranged many science-related experiments, such as DNA extraction, water rockets, elephant toothpaste, and a chemical garden. We used common everyday materials for children to practice, and paired these with carefully crafted course presentations to show them that science is all around us.

To quantify the effectiveness of the overall curriculum, our team designed pre- and post-test questionnaires to assess students' curiosity about science, their level of interest in science, their tolerance for experimental failures, their willingness to ask questions, their understanding of DNA, and their knowledge of following experimental procedures. Our statistical analysis showed significant effectiveness in all areas (p-value < 0.001, N=217), demonstrating the success and effectiveness of our science education program.

HP safety

Regarding course arrangements, we first consulted with relevant faculty members about safety procedures, and only purchased materials after receiving approval from the school. During experiments, we required participants to wear safety goggles and gloves as per course requirements, and a medical professional was present during each activity to ensure safety and address any medical issues. Finally, most of our experiments adhered to the 3R principle (Replace, Recycle, Reduce), reusing equipment whenever possible, and implementing proper waste sorting to ensure environmental sustainability.

Education, a process of two-way learning

Although we taught the same lessons in each activity, we also learned how to communicate with students of different ages and levels, and how to make complex scientific knowledge more concrete so that children could understand and develop a love for science. These were lessons we learned through countless trials and failures. In our interactions with the children, we witnessed their pure curiosity and fascination with the mysterious. This mindset is an indispensable quality for us scientific researchers; it reminds us that even in the face of setbacks, we must not lose our curiosity and pursuit of science. What moved us most was the card-writing session at the end of each activity. Seeing the thank-you cards written by the children let us know that they genuinely felt our efforts and care. For our team, education is not just about disseminating scientific knowledge around the world, but also a two-way learning process.

What we achieve?

In these 10 activities, we reached a total of 266 children. The overall STEAM science camp not only achieved SDGs-3 Health and Wellbeing, SDGs-4 Quality Education, SDGs-5 Gender Equality, and SDGs-10 Reduction of Inequality; it also conformed to the four principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC): prohibition of discrimination, best interests of the child, the right to survival and development, and respect for the child's opinion.

Detailed Explanation:

  1. Prohibition of Discrimination: This camp does not select children based on their background; every child is treated equally and fairly.
  2. Best Interests of the Child: The entire curriculum is designed with the child in mind, including curriculum design, teaching methods, and communication methods.
  3. Right to Survival and Development: The curriculum ensures that children are virtually unharmed during the activities and contributes to their future development through the teaching of scientific knowledge.
  4. Respect children's opinions: In the classroom, we encourage children to speak up and patiently answer every curious question, so that they feel cared for.

NCKU Workshops

Date: August 2–3  |  Location: National Cheng Kung University, Tainan

Workshop: 我們一起追的科學

This science workshop was designed to introduce high school students to the spirit of iGEM, the applications of synthetic biology, and the ways science can be connected with creativity and sustainability through interactive, hands-on activities. Beyond helping participants learn about our project and how our team works, we also hoped to spark their interest in scientific research and encourage them to develop skills in observation, critical thinking, teamwork, and communication throughout the two-day program.

The workshop included a variety of activities, such as ice-breaking games, a World Café session, the Agarose Enzyme Lab, an RPG-style adventure, The Green Cabinet board game, Micro Detective, and brainstorming. On the first day, the ice-breaking game helped everyone loosen up and get to know one another, while the World Café introduced iGEM, our team structure, and our project in an engaging and approachable way. The Agarose Enzyme Lab then gave participants a chance to experience basic experimental work firsthand and understand scientific phenomena through direct observation. The RPG game added a sense of exploration and interaction, making the learning process even more immersive. One of the highlights of this year's workshop was The Green Cabinet, a board game entirely developed by our team, from the initial idea to card design, production, and testing. Through this game, participants gained a deeper understanding of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals, and sustainability became something they could connect with in a more familiar and everyday context. On the second day, Micro Detective guided students to explore a microscopic world beyond what the naked eye can see, while the brainstorming session encouraged idea sharing and creative thinking, allowing participants to generate even more possibilities through discussion.

During the implementation of the workshop, we also realized that there was still room for improvement in our time management. Because our initial estimation for each activity was not precise enough, some sessions ran longer than expected, while others left us with too much time to fill. This made the overall pace of the workshop somewhat difficult to control at times. Through this experience, we learned that future event planning should not only focus on the content itself, but also pay closer attention to how each activity flows into the next, how much time each session truly requires, and how we can respond more flexibly to unexpected situations. These lessons will help us make future workshops smoother and more well-balanced.

The feedback from our participants reflected a highly positive learning experience. The workshop received an average satisfaction score of 4.6 out of 5, showing that most students were highly satisfied with the overall activities. More importantly, the students' self-reported understanding of synthetic biology improved substantially after the workshop, with the average score increasing from 2.39 before the workshop to 3.88 afterward.

Beyond the numerical results, many students also shared thoughtful reflections on their experience. One participant wrote:

“活動內容很創新、豐富，這兩天真的學習很多，也走出了自己的舒適圈，謝謝 iGEM 舉辦了這麼棒的工作坊🥰”
"The activities were innovative and diverse. I learned so much over these two days and even stepped outside my comfort zone. Thank you, iGEM, for organizing such a wonderful workshop! 🥰"

Another participant shared:

“有許多需要團隊合作、溝通協調的部分，我覺得自己的社交能力進步很多😭”
"There were many activities that required teamwork, communication, and coordination. I feel that my social skills improved a lot through this experience. 😭"

These responses reminded us that the impact of the workshop extended beyond scientific knowledge. Through hands-on experiments, games, discussions, and teamwork, students were able to explore new ideas, challenge themselves, and learn through interaction. At the same time, their feedback gave us valuable insight into how our activities were experienced from the participants' perspective, helping us identify what worked well and what we can continue to improve.

Finally, we would like to sincerely thank all of the high school students who participated in this workshop. Their enthusiasm and thoughtful responses made these two days even more meaningful. We also want to thank Future Hall for providing the venue and making the event possible, as well as the support from the STEAM From Seed initiative. Looking ahead, we hope to continue designing science activities that are both educational and interactive, so that more students can experience science through hands-on learning and meaningful exchange, discover the spirit of iGEM, and grow into people who are ready to face real-world challenges with curiosity and confidence.

National Xihu Senior High School

Date: May 24, 2026  |  Location: Changhua County

Workshop: 3D玩家出任務

"Sharing science might not transform their lives overnight, but it lights a beacon that guides them toward a broader world."

On May 24, 2026, our team traveled to National Xihu Senior High School in Changhua County to host an interactive Synthetic Biology and STEM science camp for 39 students from both National Xihu Senior and Junior High Schools. We believe meaningful science education begins with open, equal access. Without imposing any gender quotas during registration, 26 of the 39 participants (66.7 percent) were female. This encouraging turnout demonstrated that our outreach naturally empowered young women, actively supporting the vision of Women in STEM by fostering an approachable learning environment.

To make synthetic biology tangible, our curriculum integrated STEAM concepts with our 2026 NCKU-iGEM project, "Capture." Using everyday household materials, we designed a hands-on agar-protease assay to model how Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections degrade the extracellular matrix of epidermal cells. By observing simulated tissue degradation in real time, students discovered that complex biological mechanisms could be demystified and explored using simple, accessible tools.

Beyond foundational experiments, we wanted to inspire active problem solvers. We hosted an Innovation Workshop that challenged students to tackle everyday problems using synthetic biology. As they sketched ideas on whiteboards and passionately debated solutions, their enthusiasm mirrored our own iGEM brainstorming journey. We realized these young minds do not lack creativity or bold imagination—they simply need a stage. Providing them with a platform to present their ideas revealed their immense potential as future innovators.

Our quantitative evaluations confirmed the camp's educational impact: students rated their overall satisfaction at 4.82 out of 5.00, and pre- and post-camp surveys showed a highly significant increase in their comprehension of synthetic biology and STEM concepts (p < 0.001). More importantly, this experience became a profound two-way learning journey for our team. It challenged us to translate intricate science into a language that resonates with everyone. While a single camp may not transform lives overnight, we believe "the seeds of curiosity planted here will serve as a beacon, guiding these students toward a broader world."

Beyond the numerical results, students also shared thoughtful reflections on their experience. One participant wrote:

"學習到了STEM和合成生物學的一些知識，因為我才高一，其實有一些之前沒聽過，但今天的營隊讓我了解了很多，吸收了很多新知識，我覺得很讚～～💯"
"I learned some things about STEM and synthetic biology. Since I'm only a high school freshman, there were some things I hadn't heard of before, but today's camp taught me a lot and I absorbed a lot of new knowledge. I think it's great!"

Another participant shared:

"了解關於合成生物學的相關知識，也和許多不同想法的人討論、碰撞，不僅可以向成大優秀的學長姐學習，也能和其他同學一起討論，我認為這是對我很大的收穫。"
"Learning about synthetic biology and discussing and exchanging ideas with many people who have different perspectives has been a great gain for me. I can learn from outstanding students at National Cheng Kung University School and also discuss with other students."

Puyan Elementary School

Date: May 25, 2026  |  Location: Changhua County

Workshop: The Ultimate Code of Life

"Science is not merely about finding answers; it is the key that unlocks the hidden blueprint of our world."

Building upon our outreach journey at Xihu Senior High School, our team traveled to Puyan Elementary School in Changhua County on May 25, 2026, to host an interactive STEM science workshop titled "The Ultimate Code of Life." Grounded in the core values of SDG 4 (Quality Education) and STEAM, we believe that meaningful science education stems from equal access and interactive exploration, establishing a solid foundation for scientific literacy during early childhood education.

To render abstract genetic concepts accessible and intuitive for elementary students, our curriculum adopted the perspective of a "code of life." Through gamified activities such as "Lego Instruction Manuals" and "Trait Battle Royale," students gained a clear understanding of the roles DNA and genes play in the human body while appreciating the diversity of their own physical traits. Furthermore, through a hands-on "DNA Extraction Experiment", students isolated genetic material using simple, cost-effective tools. By observing the extraction process in real time, they discovered that complex biological mechanisms could be demystified, learning firsthand about the vital roles that genes and DNA play within our bodies and the surrounding ecosystem.

Beyond conducting experiments, our goal was to cultivate active thinkers and problem solvers. By guiding students to view DNA as "life's hidden blueprint," we encouraged them to ask questions and unleash their imagination, exploring how genetics connects to real-world applications in healthcare and agriculture. As they passionately debated concepts and shared their observations, their enthusiasm mirrored our own iGEM brainstorming journey—reaffirming that young minds do not lack bold imagination; they simply need a stage to shine.

Designed primarily for 16 elementary students aged 10 to 11, our quantitative evaluations confirmed the workshop's exceptional educational impact. All 16 participants demonstrated remarkable enthusiasm, yielding a 100% overall satisfaction score of 5.00 out of 5.00. Moreover, pre- and post-activity surveys revealed a highly significant improvement in students' comprehension of genetics and STEM concepts. Average comprehension scores rose from 2.81 to 3.50 out of 4.00—a net growth of +0.69 (t(15) = 3.91, p < 0.01). Reflecting high levels of engagement, 93.8% of students explicitly expressed a desire to participate in similar science activities in the future.

Ultimately, this experience proved to be a profoundly rewarding, two-way learning journey for our team, challenging us to translate complex molecular biology into an intuitive language that sparks childhood curiosity. While a single short workshop may not instantly transform the world or convey all scientific knowledge, we firmly believe the seeds of curiosity planted here will serve as a catalyst, empowering these young minds to fearlessly explore a broader world.
