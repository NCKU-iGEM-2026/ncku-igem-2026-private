Part 1

Inhibition model

Our inhibition strategy uses published single-stranded DNA aptamers that bind the acyl-homoserine lactone (AHL) signals of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The model represents these aptamers as molecular sponges that reduce the free signal available to receptors.

We calibrated the model using ALS-5 and A16-2 swarming data, tested it against the held-out Cb-2 treatment — which inhibited more than our baseline dosing assumption predicted, in all three assays, and less than an equal-molar one would — and estimated a higher A16-2 dose for future testing.

Code: GitLab (MIT; results and report CC BY 4.0). Model outputs and re-analysis results are traceable to files in the repository's results/ directory, cited beneath the relevant tables and figures. The measurements themselves are the wet lab's, and are reported on the Results page.

1.1 The question

Three aptamers were tested, targeting the las and rhl AHL signals.[1] Their preparation differed, which matters when comparing the treatments.

Cb-2 was tested as a crude extract, not a purified oligonucleotide. Its aptamer concentration was not quantified, so its comparison with model predictions depends on dosing assumptions (1.4).

All three treatments reduced swarming and pyocyanin. A16-2 and Cb-2 also reduced biofilm; ALS-5's biofilm result was borderline (1.2). These measurements show partial inhibition, but do not establish the mechanism limiting the response. The model therefore addresses two questions:

  1. Prediction: Can the model predict the held-out Cb-2 treatment? See 1.4 →
  2. Next experiment: What higher A16-2 dose should be tested for 50 % swarming inhibition? See 1.5 →

We assess the held-out predictions first, then present the dose estimate with the limitations identified by that comparison. The estimate remains a hypothesis for experimental testing.

Plate photographs and the wet lab's original analysis are on the Results page. Section 1.2 explains this model's separate re-analysis of the same measurements.

Assay-by-assay interpretation and supporting analyses

All three aptamers inhibit swarming and pyocyanin. All six of those intervals exclude no effect (p between 2.4×10−6 and 0.019), and the effects agree in direction, with overlapping magnitudes, across two independent phenotypes.

Biofilm resolves for Cb-2 and for A16-2, and not for ALS-5. Cb-2 removes 36.7 % of the biofilm [28.8, 43.7] on twelve replicates (p = 3×10−6) and A16-2 12.6 % [2.7, 21.5] on six (p = 0.023); ALS-5's 11.5 % does not clear the line (p = 0.057).

The ordering reverses between assays. A16-2 is the strongest in swarming, Cb-2 in pyocyanin and biofilm. The model is calibrated on the swarming measurements alone (1.3), and nothing on this page explains that reversal.

Growth effects have not been excluded. These phenotype measurements alone cannot distinguish AHL signalling inhibition from changes in growth or viability. Matched growth and viability measurements are needed alongside the phenotype assays (1.6).

The BL21 extract control showed no statistically significant difference from untreated controls in any of the three assays. It used E. coli BL21(DE3) without the retron plasmid, processed through the same culture, harvest and TRIzol extraction as Cb-2 (Results). This checks the extraction background, but does not establish equivalence or exclude growth effects specific to the Cb-2 sample. The control was not used for model calibration.

Supporting analyses: contrasts.csv, equivalence_tests.csv, plate_split.csv, power_requirements.csv and prism_crosscheck.csv; see also model_report.md, section 1.

{# ========================= 1.3 the model ========================= #}

1.3 The model

The model asks whether AHL sequestration can account for the observed swarming inhibition. It links aptamer binding, signal feedback and receptor occupancy to relative swarm radius. These links describe an assumed mechanism; the phenotype data alone do not prove it.

From free signal to swarming

The model represents two signals: 3-oxo-C12-HSL (las, signal 1) and C4-HSL (rhl, signal 2). Its swarming output is:

R / Rcontrol = ( θ2 / θ2,control )β

R is swarm radius and θ2 is modelled rhl receptor occupancy. We fix β = 1, so a 50 % reduction in occupancy gives a 50 % reduction in predicted radius. This mapping is assumed, not independently measured; it does not describe bacterial survival or colony area.

Full equations and symbol definitions

Receptor occupancy depends on free signal concentration, with Ki the effective activation constant:

θi = {{ frac('Li,free', 'Ki + Li,free') }}

Signal production and removal, with autoinduction and an assumed las-to-rhl gate:

dL1/dt = v1 ( e1 + (1 − e1) θ1h ) − d1 L1
dL2/dt = v2 ( e2 + (1 − e2) θ2h ) · g(θ1) − d2 L2
g(θ1) = ( θlas + θ1 ) / ( 1 + θlas ) L1 is 3-oxo-C12-HSL and L2 is C4-HSL. ei is the leaky fraction made without activation, h the cooperativity of the loop, vi the maximum synthesis rate and di the removal rate. The dimensionless parameter θlas sets how far las occupancy can move rhl synthesis, and larger values make the gate less responsive: as θ1 falls to zero, g falls only to θlas / (1 + θlas). It is distinct from receptor occupancy θ1.

Binding is represented by rapid equilibrium with one competitive site per aptamer. Aj is the total concentration of aptamer type j; Kd,ji is its dissociation constant for signal i, and the subscript f denotes free signal:

Dj = 1 + L1f/Kd,j1 + L2f/Kd,j2
L1,total = L1f + Σj Aj ( L1f/Kd,j1 ) / Dj
L2,total = L2f + Σj Aj ( L2f/Kd,j2 ) / Dj The shared denominator makes the two signals compete for the same binding capacity. This is a model assumption for Cb-2, not a measured binding stoichiometry.

What is fitted, and what is tested?

Two parameters are fitted to two swarming targets: the las-to-rhl gating parameter to ALS-5, and the effective A16-2 affinity to A16-2. Cb-2 is excluded from calibration and used for the held-out comparison in 1.4. Matching the two calibration targets is therefore not an independent validation.

Assumptions that affect the predictions

  • One well-mixed compartment. The baseline model does not resolve spatial gradients across a plate. Section 1.4 compares alternative spatial structure.
  • Rapid, competitive binding. Binding is treated as faster than signal synthesis, with one competitive binding site per aptamer.
  • Assumed Cb-2 affinities. Each Cb-2 affinity is set equal to the corresponding specialist's affinity. This is not a measurement of Cb-2 in our extract.
  • A nominal dose shared across treatments. Equal mass concentration is the baseline convention; the concentrations actually applied to the plates were not recorded.

Only swarming is used for calibration. Pyocyanin and biofilm comparisons in 1.4 are additional checks under the stated output assumptions, not separately calibrated mechanistic models of those phenotypes.

Dose convention, other inputs and literature limitations

The nominal mass concentration is 7.797 ng/µL, from a Nanodrop reading of TRIzol-extracted retron material (2026-07-31). The model treats this as ssDNA mass concentration and applies it to all three treatments. It is not a recorded plate dose or a sequence-specific measurement of Cb-2; ALS-5 and A16-2 were chemically synthesised reference oligonucleotides.

Using design lengths of 35, 44 and 93 nt gives nominal concentrations of 675, 537 and 254 nM for ALS-5, A16-2 and Cb-2, respectively. These conversions assume the assigned DNA mass is entirely the intended aptamer. The joint uncertainty sweep varies the mass concentration over 1–30 ng/µL, but does not resolve the unknown Cb-2 fraction or actual plate dilution.

Model records: params/parameters.csv and model_calibration.csv. Reference [3] is a verified quantitative AHL study used here for context; it does not establish the exact concentration bounds used in the existing sweep. Those bounds remain assumptions (see 1.7).

How uncertainty reaches the predictions

Each of 400 joint draws combines a plate-level bootstrap resample with uncertain model inputs and refits the two parameters. 296 draws calibrate successfully; 104 do not. Of the failed draws, 102 cannot reproduce the ALS-5 target under their sampled inputs and two give multiple solver roots.

All reported draw-based medians and ranges are conditional on the 296 successful draws and exclude incompatible or ambiguous ones. The sweep varies the occupancy-to-radius exponent β over 0.5–2.0 and the ALS-5 affinity over 20–35 nM along with the other inputs; β is the strongest single influence on the dose required for 90 % inhibition (PRCC −0.54), and it remains assumed rather than measured. What the sweep does not cover is the one-site competitive binding, the assumption that Cb-2 matches each specialist's affinity, and the single well-mixed compartment. The fitted-parameter medians are 1.9 for the gate and 1117 nM for A16-2 affinity, illustrating their dependence on the input assumptions.

Use the model to propose experiments. The fitted parameters are effective values, not measured biochemical constants. 1.4 checks them first, against the held-out Cb-2 treatment.

Sources: uncertainty_summary.csv for the draw counts and quantiles, model_report.md for how the 104 failures split, and calibration_draws.csv for the resampled targets.

How the dosing convention changes the prediction

At equal mass concentration, the 93 nt Cb-2 construct supplies about 47 % as many molecules as the 44 nt A16-2 construct, using the model's length-based conversion. The decomposition below separates the effect of dual specificity from this change in molecular concentration.

This explains the baseline prediction's sensitivity to dosing, but does not establish the dose used in the experiment or the biological value of either construct. Neither the applied plate concentration nor the Cb-2 fraction of the crude extract was quantified. Future comparisons should report both mass and molar dose for quantified aptamer preparations (1.6).

What spatial smoothing can explain

The one-compartment model has a sharp switching region: its swarming ratio changes from 0.991 at 8.69 ng/µL to 0.658 at 8.70 ng/µL, skipping the measured point estimate of 0.820. An alternative model averages patches with different local doses to test sensitivity to this abrupt response.

Source: model_report.md, section 2.6.

Across the tested log-dose spreads of 0.2–0.7, the smoothed swarming prediction falls inside the measured interval. Pyocyanin and biofilm predictions remain outside their intervals. Spatial heterogeneity is therefore a possible explanation for the swarming mismatch, not an experimentally established cause. Local dose variation was not measured, and this comparison does not validate the other phenotype-output mappings.

Spatial sensitivity: numerical comparisons and limits
  • Remaining phenotype gaps: at the widest tested spread, pyocyanin reaches 0.857 (measured interval 0.641–0.799) and biofilm 0.908 (0.563–0.712).
  • Calibration sensitivity: the gating parameter changes from 14.8 to 13.6 and the A16-2 effective affinity changes by less than 1.5 % in the reported comparison.
  • Dose-estimate sensitivity: the point-fit A16-2 dose multiplier for 50 % swarming inhibition changes from 1.55× in one compartment to 1.65× at a log-dose spread of 0.5. This is a structural sensitivity comparison, not a new estimate of the full uncertainty range.

The spread was varied as an assumption, not estimated from measured plate heterogeneity. Agreement over the tested range shows that this model variant can accommodate the swarming observation; it does not identify the true spatial structure. Unless explicitly labelled as spatial comparisons, the results in this part use the baseline one-compartment model.

Source: structure_comparison.csv.

What remains unresolved

  • Affinity and binding assumptions. Cb-2's effective affinities and binding behaviour were not measured in the tested extract. Changing these inputs can change predictions.
  • Plate differences. Cb-2 swarming was measured on five plates not shared with ALS-5 or A16-2, limiting the separation of treatment effects from plate differences.
  • Extract composition and growth. Cb-2 was applied as a crude extract. The BL21 extract control checks a shared processing background, but does not identify the active component or exclude treatment-specific growth effects (1.2).
Why an inverse affinity estimate is not a measurement

One inverse calculation gives a Cb-2 / C4-HSL effective Kd of about 158 nM, roughly 35 times tighter than the fitted A16-2 value of 5571 nM. This is a conditional model solution, not an independently measured affinity or evidence that stronger binding caused the observed inhibition.

The in-vitro binding assays reported by Wu et al. [1] provide a separate biochemical comparison. Neither their absolute values nor affinity ratios can be assumed to transfer unchanged to this crude-extract plate assay. Dose, sample composition, plate effects and model structure remain unresolved here; the inverse estimate cannot distinguish their contributions.

Source: cb2_inversion.csv.

The baseline does not reproduce the held-out Cb-2 results. The sensitivity analyses identify assumptions to test, but do not establish why Cb-2 differs from the baseline prediction. Quantified preparations, shared plates and matched growth measurements are needed to distinguish the alternatives (1.6).

{# ========================= 1.5 how much more aptamer ========================= #}

1.5 A higher dose to test

The A16-2 dose sweep estimates 50 % swarming inhibition near 1.6× the nominal dose, with a 90 % draw range of 1.0–2.6×. This is a hypothesis for a dose-response experiment, not an experimentally demonstrated concentration requirement.

Here, 50 % inhibition means a swarm radius half that of the untreated control. The estimate extrapolates beyond the single-dose calibration described in 1.3. The Cb-2 mismatch in 1.4 does not directly test this A16-2 dose response, but it limits confidence in the model's broader predictive performance.

Predicted dose-response shapes

In the model's dose analysis, ALS-5 does not reach 50 % swarming inhibition in 269 of the 296 successful draws (91 %). Its effect reaches swarming only through the assumed las-to-rhl gate, and at the fitted θlas = 14.8 that gate is nearly closed: removing las occupancy altogether changes rhl synthesis by about 6 %, so the predicted ALS-5 curve flattens. This is a property of the assumed gate, not evidence of a biological inhibition ceiling. A single-dose measurement cannot distinguish a saturating response from lower potency; testing that distinction needs a separate ALS-5 dose series.

What the range does and does not cover

The median and 90 % range summarise the 296 successful calibrations out of 400 joint draws. They are conditional on the sampled inputs and on calibration success, rather than a guaranteed range for a future experiment. Failed or ambiguous calibrations are excluded, as described in 1.3.

The sweep varies the nominal dose, the occupancy-to-radius exponent β and the ALS-5 affinity along with the other uncertain inputs. For the 50 % dose quoted here the uninhibited C4-HSL level leads the ranking (PRCC −0.35), ahead of β (−0.16); β leads only for the 90 % dose. What the sweep keeps fixed is the one-site competitive binding, the assumed Cb-2 affinities and the single compartment. The point-fit A16-2 multiplier also changes from 1.55× to 1.65× in the spatial comparison in 1.4. That comparison tests one structural assumption; it does not establish robustness to all model choices.

Test a dose series around the proposed range. Quantify the A16-2 preparation and record the concentration actually applied, in both mass and molar units. Measure swarm radius with matched controls, growth and viability measurements; rhamnolipid measurements would also help assess the assumed link to swarming (1.6). The 1.6× estimate should guide that experiment, not be reported as a validated effective dose or a required production yield.

1.6 From model gaps to next experiments

The model identifies measurements needed to test the proposed mechanism and the A16-2 dose hypothesis. The work below is proposed follow-up, not completed validation.

The multiplier must be tied to a quantified experimental reference; 7.797 ng/µL is the model's nominal convention, not a measured A16-2 plate dose. The dose series should estimate the response around the proposed target rather than treat 1.6× as a known effective dose.

Tests of the model's mechanism

  • Assess the signalling-to-radius link. Pair swarm radius with rhamnolipid and, where feasible, AHL or receptor-activity measurements. Rhamnolipid is a supporting readout; measuring it alongside radius does not by itself identify the occupancy-to-radius exponent β.
  • Test the ALS-5 plateau separately. An ALS-5 dose series is needed to distinguish saturation from lower potency. The single-dose comparison with A16-2 cannot establish a biological ceiling.
  • Check the binding inputs. Measure aptamer affinities under relevant conditions and rerun the model with alternative literature values. Treat the assumed Cb-2 affinities and competitive binding structure as hypotheses to test.
{# ========================= 1.7 limits ========================= #}

1.7 Limits

Four limitations define how the model's predictions should be interpreted.

  1. The inhibition mechanism is not confirmed

    Evidence gap
    Matched growth and viability measurements are missing. The BL21 extract control does not identify the active component in Cb-2.
    Implication
    Reduced swarming, pyocyanin or biofilm cannot be attributed specifically to AHL sequestration (1.2).

  2. Applied dose and sample composition are uncertain

    Evidence gap
    Plate concentrations were not recorded. Cb-2 was a crude extract, and its aptamer concentration was not quantified.
    Implication
    Comparisons depend on the nominal dosing convention. The 1.6× estimate is not a verified plate concentration or production requirement (1.3, 1.5).

  3. Calibration does not validate predictions

    Evidence gap
    Two parameters fit two single-dose swarming targets. The occupancy-to-radius exponent β, Cb-2 affinities and spatial structure remain assumptions.
    Implication
    Fitted values are effective model parameters. The baseline Cb-2 predictions miss the measured intervals; sensitivity analyses do not establish the cause (1.3, 1.4).

  4. The uncertainty range is incomplete

    Analysis scope
    Summaries use 296 successful calibrations out of 400 draws. Some parameters remain fixed, and statistical conclusions depend on plate structure and replicate definitions.
    Implication
    The reported ranges are conditional on these choices. They do not cover all model uncertainty or guarantee the response in a future experiment (1.3, 1.5).

The model guides the next experiments; it does not establish an effective dose or confirm the inhibition mechanism.

Part 2

Imaging model: from photograph to millimetres

This model now runs inside our software, as Plate Imaging · Agar Vision in LasReader.

2.1 The question

Colony sizes are measured by hand, one colony at a time. The work is slow and it has to be repeated for every plate. This part of the project is a tool built for the wet lab: it takes one photograph of a plate and returns every colony's size in millimetres, with each figure drawn across the colony it belongs to, so a reading can always be checked against what it was taken from.

It is not a model of the biology. What it models is the measurement — how a count of pixels becomes a length in millimetres, what the picture itself has to supply for that conversion to hold, and which of the bright things on a plate are colonies and which are not.

2.2 The model

  1. 1 Find the dish Circle transform on a downscaled copy; if that returns nothing, the largest thresholded shape instead. Gives the scale and the boundary in one step. dish ø ÷ diameter in px → mm/px
  2. 2 Flatten the lighting A heavily blurred copy is taken as the background and subtracted, removing the gradient across the plate.
  3. Which light is this?
    • White Brightness Otsu on the flattened image.
    • UV Colour Green-to-blue ratio: fluorescence is a question about hue, not about being bright.
  4. 3 Keep only what has a core A loose outline and a much stricter core. A region survives only if it holds enough core pixels — a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through.
  5. Convex enough to be one colony?
    • Yes Leave it alone Kept whole and counted as one colony.
    • No Watershed Split apart, and each piece counted as its own colony.
  6. 4 Measure and annotate Longest span, equivalent diameter, area — drawn onto the photograph with a scale bar.

The pipeline is OpenCV throughout. A Hough circle transform[2] finds the dish, run on a downscaled copy — the dish is the largest thing in the frame, so the search does not need full resolution — with Otsu thresholding[3] as the fallback. Subtracting a large-σ Gaussian flattens the lighting. White light and UV are then two problems rather than one with a switch: brightness against the agar is a question of intensity, answered by Otsu; fluorescence is a question of hue, answered by the green-to-blue channel ratio, with the choice between them made from the blue-violet fraction of the frame in HSV.

The difficult part was never finding bright things. It was telling a colony from a reflection off the agar or the lid, which can be just as bright. Thresholding twice — a loose outline and a stricter core — and keeping only the connected components that hold enough core pixels settles it: a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through. Solidity and aspect ratio remove what is left. Touching colonies are split only where solidity says they need it, by a marker-controlled watershed seeded from the distance transform, because breaking a clean colony apart is the worse error. The figure drawn on the photograph is the Feret diameter.

2.6 What it changed

Three things this changed about how the wet lab measures a plate.

  1. A plate is read in one pass, and the reading comes back drawn on the photograph. Every colony on the plate is measured together rather than one at a time, and the output is the picture itself with each span, its figure and a scale bar marked on it. The number never has to be copied from an instrument into a notebook, and any reading can be checked against the colony it came from without going back to the plate.
  2. The reading is good enough to use. Against hand measurement it agrees to within 0.71 mm on average, every colony inside 2 mm, with no bias the data can separate from zero (2.5). That is finer than the differences the assays are built to compare.
  3. It is small enough to run anywhere. The pipeline is classical computer vision — thresholds, morphology, a watershed, a convex hull — so there is no learned model, no weights to load and no inference step. It needs no GPU and runs on a free hosting tier, which is what let it go to the people doing the measuring rather than staying on one machine in the lab.

2.7 Limits

  • A strong enough reflection can still be read as a colony. The two-threshold test rejects the glare we have photographed — a reflection is bright without being bright all the way through, so it clears the loose outline and fails the core (2.4 shows one being dropped). What it cannot reject is a reflection that is both bright and even enough to have a dense centre of its own, which a fluorescent tube close to the plate, or a lid left on, can produce. The plates here were photographed without a lid and away from direct overhead light; that is a handling rule rather than something the program enforces.
  • The plate has to be photographed square-on. Every millimetre on the page comes from one number: the dish diameter the operator enters, divided by the dish diameter the program measures in pixels. That division assumes the dish appears as a circle. Photographed off-axis it projects as an ellipse, the circle fit lands somewhere between its long and short axis, and the scale is wrong by however much the tilt was. The error is systematic rather than random — one scale factor is shared by the whole plate, so every colony on it is out by the same proportion. A comparison made within a single plate, which is how the assays read, should therefore survive a small tilt even though the absolute millimetres would not; we have not measured how large a tilt that holds for.

{# ========================= the questions ========================= #}

3.0 Two questions

CAPTURE-Screen was built for our AHL biosensor and rehearsed on GFP cultures: we expected an AHL-induced culture to be about as bright as a GFP one (Hardware). Sections 3.1 to 3.4 were built on version 1 of the 3D-printed build, which was later replaced (Hardware); 3.5 checks what carried over to version 2. None of this part applies to the laser-cut build. What it describes is that instrument's measurement behaviour — how a count relates to the culture in the cuvette — not the biosensor's response to AHL, which has not been resolved.

Within that scope, the model had two questions put to it.

Is the reading proportional? Within one session, where the signal is clear of the floor, it is: double the GFP culture and the reading becomes 2.04×.

Why did the same recipe read six times higher on one day than another? Because a volume fraction is not an amount — a volume of thin culture holds less GFP than the same volume of dense culture. Dividing by the density of that day's stock brings the three slopes to within 8 % of each other.

The instrument's own log sets the scale of the noise. Within one insertion the three cycles differ by about 0.69 counts (root mean square), so a tube mean carries 0.27 to 0.43 counts of it. What counts as one measurement Show Hide

Each cycle reads the sensor three times: dark, lit, dark. A cycle whose dark read before the flash is not zero on all twelve channels is void, because that is what room light reaching the detector looks like. Each tube is read for three cycles and enters every model as the mean of its valid ones. The cycles are three looks at one tube, not three samples, so the tube is the unit throughout. A reading of zero is a legitimate value and is kept.

None of the tables below was typed in by hand. Each is generated by a script from the instrument's raw serial logs and the bench record, then recomputed by a second script, written separately, that compares the two cell by cell. Every tube mean the models use is listed, including the readings of zero and the tubes that were re-prepared, and the figures are drawn from those same means. The instrument itself is described on the Hardware page, and the software that turns a reading into a concentration on the Software page.

3.3 What shape the relationship has

Is the line of 3.2 enough, or does the relationship bend? Fit cannot answer that. Add parameters and the fit always improves, even when the extra ones mean nothing. That is overfitting. R² cannot detect it, because overfitting raises R² as well. So we scored each candidate on tubes it had never seen. We did that on the readings already taken; nothing was measured again. The rule is leave one recipe out. Set aside every reading from one recipe, fit the model to the rest, then ask it to predict the readings set aside. Every recipe takes its turn, and those errors become the Left-out recipe error column.

Every model below is fitted to every tube of its session — 14, 18 and 18, the full 50 across the three sessions. That takes in the twenty pure-GFP tubes of 3.2, the mixed tubes and the non-GFP tubes alike. This matters because a model candidate below is a surface in G and d. A pure-GFP tube has d = G, so that surface cannot be fitted on the twenty pure-GFP tubes alone like 3.2 did.

3.5 After the rebuild: what carried over

The models above describe the first reader. Its noise did not come mostly from the detector. In four of seven replicate groups, seating and preparation outweighed the cycle noise (3.1). That was one consideration in the rebuild that followed, alongside the parts that had broken or made loading slow.

The reader was rebuilt on 2026-10-03: five printed parts redrawn around a one-step cuvette seat, and one resistor to bring the LED to its design current. Putting the same tube back now repeats to 0.76 %, against 3.6 to 6.3 % before (Hardware).

Three sessions on the rebuilt reader — a dilution series, a session on AHL plates and one on how dense a culture can be — show what carries over. All three are on the Hardware page, and everything in this section was worked out after their readings were seen.

The inverse models did not carry over

Three models were also fitted on the first reader to read G back from the sensor's channels, none of them needing the stock OD: F4 alone, a ridge regression on four channels, and a random forest. All three were applied unchanged to the twelve tubes of the dilution series, whose share of GFP culture is known. None did better than always guessing the mean of the training tubes: the best was off by 27.1 points on average against 27.8, and that only after the counts were scaled down for the higher LED current. The stock of that session gave more signal for the same share of culture than any stock the models had seen. G, a share of one day's stock, is not a scale that carries from one stock to the next.

A background that rises with scatter

Every tube in 3.1 that held no GFP read exactly zero on the green channel. The plate suspensions were far denser than any of those tubes, and in them the green channel no longer read zero without GFP. That has to be described before any reading from those plates can be used.

What the extra reading follows is scatter. The denser the suspension, the higher a scatter channel, F2, and the higher the green channel reads. So the description is the signal from GFP plus one term in proportion to F2, put through the curve R that the dilution series already traced at its faint end:

F4 = R(SGFP + c · F2),   R(S) = S / (1 + (S0 / S)n)

Only one number in it is new. S0 = 6.51 counts and n = 5.20 are the shape of R, taken from the dilution series and not refitted. c, the size of the scatter term, was fitted here and comes to 0.0629. It rests on thirteen captures from the two sessions that held no GFP or were judged not induced, and only three of those thirteen read above zero at all. That is a thin fit, so everything below is a check against readings it was not fitted to.

Why the numbers are weaker than they look

  • The slope s contains both how much GFP each cell makes and how sensitive the instrument is. Without a fluorescence standard the two cannot be separated.[6]
  • ρ rests on very few OD readings: one in the first session, two in the third.
  • Tubes sharing a cuvette, a time slot or a side of a firmware reset are not fully independent, so every interval and p value on this page is nominal.
  • Three sessions, each with a different recipe design, are a small basis for a model. Every analysis here looked at the same three.
  • The cross-session conversion is a post hoc analysis (3.4). How ρ should be read was chosen after the three sessions had been seen, there are only three of them, and no prediction made in advance has tested it yet. The formal comparison agrees with the figures — a shared slope is not rejected after conversion, p = 0.897, and is rejected before it, p < 0.001 — but for the same reason those p values describe the data and validate nothing.
  • Mixing session 1 supports no lack-of-fit test (3.3). It repeated one recipe, which gives two degrees of freedom of pure error — too few for the test to detect anything — so that column is left blank for it rather than filled with p values that say nothing either way.
  • Neither background curve is a calibration (3.5), and neither may be used to subtract a background from other readings. Both were worked out after the readings were seen. The fitted curve rests on one parameter that only three readings above zero could constrain. The measured curve is one culture on one day, and it covers F3 from 5 to 89 and no further. Neither says whether the background is scattered light or the cells' own fluorescence. A reading that sits on the background means the reader read nothing above it there, not that the plate was not induced.

Part 1 · 1.4 Whether the sponge picture holds for a treatment it was never fitted to. It does not. The held-out Cb-2 prediction of 0.992 sits above a measured 0.820, and the miss repeats in pyocyanin and biofilm; one shared dose correction cannot pull all three inside their intervals. That ruled out the simplest reading — that dose alone explains the gap — and put the preparation itself on the list of things to measure.
Part 1 · 1.5, 1.6 How much more A16-2 the next swarming experiment should carry. A dose series around 1.6× the nominal convention, spanning 1.0–2.6×, written down before the experiment rather than read off afterwards. It also fixed what gets recorded beside it: the applied concentration in both mass and molar units, and matched growth and viability controls, since the three assays on their own cannot separate signal sequestration from a growth effect.
Part 2 · 2.5 Whether a photograph could stand in for measuring colonies by hand. It can, and the wet lab stopped measuring them one at a time. Agreement with hand measurement is 0.71 mm on average, every colony inside 2 mm, with no bias separable from zero — finer than the differences the assays are built to compare.
Part 2 · 2.6 How to get that reading to the people doing the measuring. Classical vision rather than a learned model, so there are no weights to load, no inference step and no GPU: it runs on a free hosting tier instead of one machine in the lab. Each plate comes back as the photograph itself, with every span and a scale bar drawn on it, so no number is copied by hand.
Part 3 · 3.1 Where the reader's noise actually came from. Not from the detector. In four of seven replicate groups, seating and preparation outweighed the cycle noise, which was one consideration in the rebuild that followed. Putting the same tube back now repeats to 0.76 %, against 3.6 to 6.3 % before.
Part 3 · 3.4 How to turn the cross-session model into evidence. A prediction for a new stock was written into the closing report, with its verdict rule fixed in advance. The reader was rebuilt before it could be tested, so it stands unresolved.
Part 3 · 3.5 Whether the earlier models could read the rebuilt reader's sessions. They could not, so the AHL plate session was read with a one-parameter background fitted to its own controls, then against a background measured two days later on a non-GFP culture. Both are reported as descriptions.
