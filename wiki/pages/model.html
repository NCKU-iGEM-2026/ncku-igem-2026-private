CAPTURE-Screen reports counts from a spectral sensor. A count is not yet a statement about a sample: it depends on how much reporter culture is in the cuvette, on how dense that culture happened to be on the day, and on the scatter that pipetting, cuvettes and the sensor add on top.

The model in this part connects the two. It was built from our own bench data and tested against it.

What this part does not claim. Every reading here is a relative fluorescence on one instrument. There is no fluorescence standard behind it, so there is no absolute unit, no limit of detection and no calibration curve. None of these cultures was induced with AHL: the samples are cultures reported to carry the sfGFP plasmid, cultures without it, and medium. A dose–response model of the biosensor itself is not part of this model, because we have not measured one.

Three questions

The reader model had three questions put to it. Each card turns over to show what came back.

Understand
Within one session, how does the green channel relate to the amount of GFP culture? Where the signal is clear of the floor, it is proportional: double the GFP culture and the reading becomes 2.04×.
Predict
Why do the slopes of different sessions differ by a factor of six, and can one session be converted into another? Dividing by the density of that day's stock brings the three slopes to within 8 % of each other.
Design
How should the next session be laid out, and is it worth running? A power calculation chose six pairs beforehand, and afterwards showed that a repeat would resolve a 10 % difference only one time in four.

3.1 The data behind it

Everything below comes from one instrument, P1-PROTO-01, read under an opaque shield with the UVette in the same orientation and the detector at the same settings throughout: gain 512×, ATIME 255, ASTEP 280. The models rest on three mixing sessions, in which a GFP stock, a non-GFP stock and fresh medium were combined at set volume fractions into 200 µL tubes.

That is 50 tubes, 38 containing GFP culture and 12 containing none. A pure-GFP tube holds GFP stock and medium only; those twenty are the ones the cross-session model uses.

What counts as one measurement 657 rows · 631 valid Show Hide

Each cycle reads the sensor three times: dark, lit, dark. A cycle whose dark read before the flash is not zero on all twelve channels is void, because that is what room light reaching the detector looks like. Each tube is read for three cycles and enters every model as the mean of its valid ones. The cycles are three looks at one tube, not three samples, so the tube is the unit throughout. A reading of zero is a legitimate value and is kept.

Across nine sets of logs from eight days the instrument recorded 657 rows, of which 631 are valid (96.0 %). The other 26 are accounted for: 18 are deliberate gate checks, run with the LED not armed or disabled, and 8 are cycles the firmware voided for a non-zero dark read. No row saturated.

The same record sets the scale of the noise. Within one insertion the three cycles differ by about 0.69 counts (root mean square), so a tube mean carries 0.27 to 0.43 counts of it. In four of seven replicate groups the spread left over between tubes is larger than that: there, seating, sampling, preparation or the cuvette contribute more than the detector's own cycle noise.

The simplest thing the reading could do is rise in proportion to the amount of GFP culture, with nothing left over at zero:

Whether that line is enough was not decided by how well it fits. A model with more terms always fits the tubes it was given better. What matters is how well it predicts tubes it was not given, so each candidate was scored by leaving one recipe out: every tube of one recipe is removed, the model is fitted to the rest, and it is asked to predict the ones removed.

Notes on the line 4 notes Show Hide
Doubling
Going from G = 0.5 to 1.0 multiplied the reading by 2.04 in mixing session 3, by 1.51 in session 1 with a dense stock, and by 9.25 in session 2 near the floor. Proportional would be 2.
Low end
The lowest-concentration recipe reads below the line in all three sessions, by 1.2 to 1.9 counts. One straight line is a first-order description.
Design
The six-parameter surface cannot be estimated from mixing session 3 at all. Its five recipes all lie on (d − G)(d − 1) = 0, so the design matrix has rank 5. A session meant to test curvature has to place recipes off that curve.
Caveat
The lack-of-fit test in mixing session 1 has two degrees of freedom of pure error, from one replicated recipe. It has almost no power, and its p values say little either way.

3.3 Across sessions, most of the difference is the stock

The three slopes above are 30.93, 5.17 and 18.69, a spread of 6.0×. If the instrument behaved the same way each day, most of that should come from the stock itself: G is a volume fraction, and a volume of thin culture holds less GFP than the same volume of dense culture. So G is replaced by a density index x that carries how dense that day's stock was.

Testing it: predict a session from the other two

A conversion that only tidies the numbers it was fitted to proves nothing. The test is to hold a whole session out, fit the slope on the other two, and predict the session that was never seen.

Predicting the same tubes from inside their own session, leaving one recipe out, gives 2.94, 1.90 and 1.09.

With conversion, a session predicted from two others lands about as close as a session predicted from itself. Without it, the error reaches 13.82 counts in mixing session 2, a session whose highest reading was 7.

This is a post hoc analysis. How ρ should be read was chosen after the three sessions had been seen, there are only three of them, and no prediction made in advance has tested it yet. The formal comparison agrees with the figures (a shared slope is not rejected after conversion, p = 0.897, and is rejected before it, p < 0.001), but for the same reason those p values describe the data and validate nothing.

3.4 Deciding what to measure, and when to stop

One question the instrument has to answer is whether other cells in the sample change the green reading. The comparison is a pair of tubes with the same GFP culture in them: A is 100 µL of GFP stock with 100 µL of medium, C is 100 µL of GFP stock with 100 µL of non-GFP culture. The quantity of interest is C − A.

Before the session, a simulation with the noise we had seen so far settled how many pairs to make. After the session, the same calculation was redone with the noise the session actually showed.

The six pairs gave a mean C − A of −0.39 counts, with a nominal 95 % interval from −1.88 to +1.10, which is −20.1 % to +11.8 % of A. The session did not resolve a difference. That is not the same as showing there is none: the honest statement is that if non-GFP culture shifts the reading, this session places the shift somewhere in that range.

At the observed noise a true difference would have to be about 21.8 % of A before six more pairs had an 80 % chance of resolving it, and resolving a 10 % difference would take 21 pairs. On those numbers the analysis recommended against repeating the comparison and for spending the remaining bench time elsewhere.

Lessons for the next design 3 notes Show Hide
More tubes
Because tube-to-tube spread exceeds cycle noise, precision comes from more tubes or more pairs, not from more cycles per tube.
Confound
The cuvettes were washed and reused, so which use of a cuvette it was, and whether A or C was read first, coincide exactly in that session. One cuvette per tube, or a cross-tabulation of the factors at the design stage, would have kept them apart.
Attribution
C receives culture, cells together with their spent medium, where A receives fresh medium. C − A is the difference between two recipes and cannot be assigned to the cells alone.

3.5 The other direction: from four channels back to G

The models above predict a reading from a recipe. In use the question runs the other way: given what the sensor reports, how much GFP culture is in the tube? A second study asked whether the other channels help with that. It predicts G from four channels (F2, F3, F4 and Clear) and from the displayed OD of that day's GFP stock, over all 50 tubes.

The pipeline was fixed before any score was looked at: a ridge regression on the four channels. It is always scored on a session it never saw, and its regularisation strength is chosen inside the training sessions by leaving recipes out.

3.7 How the numbers were produced

The tables behind sections 3.2 to 3.4 were not typed in by hand. Each is generated by a script that reads the instrument's raw serial logs and the bench record, and is then recomputed by a second script, written separately, that compares the two cell by cell. The least-squares fits are solved in exact rational arithmetic and rounded only for display. Every tube mean the models use is listed in those tables, including the readings of zero and the tubes that were re-prepared. The four-channel study keeps a record of every model it fitted and refits its held-out predictions as a check. The three figures in 3.2 and 3.3 are drawn from the twenty tube means themselves, with their slopes and held-out errors computed in the page.

The scripts and the raw logs are held by the team and are not published. The instrument that produced the readings is described on the Hardware page, and the software that turns a reading into a concentration on the Software page.
