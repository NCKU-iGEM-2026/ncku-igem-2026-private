Module 2 releases single-stranded DNA aptamers that bind the acyl-homoserine lactone (AHL) signals of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The idea is a molecular sponge: hold the signal below the threshold and the quorum-sensing switch never turns on. What the wet lab measured was a partial effect — swarming and pyocyanin both down by roughly a fifth to a third. This model was fitted to two of those numbers and then asked to predict a treatment and two phenotypes it had never seen. It misses them, in the same direction every time, and that failure is the most informative thing on this page.

{# 四張翻卡＝Part 1 的四個主要結果。和 Part 3 的 Three questions 同一個元件。 #}
At the switch
Binding alone removes only 4 % of the rhl output. The autoinduction loop turns that into the 36 % that was measured — an amplification of about nine. That is why the inhibition is partial, and why it varies.
1.6× more
About 1.6× the aptamer already made should cross 50 % swarming inhibition. This is the most robust number here: 1.0× to 2.6× across every source of uncertainty at once. Full suppression is not a target — the same sweep puts it anywhere up to 49×.
Length, not specificity
Cb-2 is predicted to lose to A16-2 because it is 93 nt long, not because it binds two ligands. Per molecule the dual binder is the better sponge; per nanogram it supplies 2.1× fewer molecules.
It fails its own test
Cb-2 was held out of every fit and the model misses it in all three assays, the same way each time. Two readings survive and this design cannot separate them. The experiment that would is the first thing 1.8 asks for.

1.1 The question

Three aptamers were tested: ALS-5 (35 nt, binds 3-oxo-C12-HSL), A16-2 (44 nt, binds C4-HSL) and Cb-2 (93 nt, binds both).[1] All three lowered swarming and pyocyanin, and none of them lowered either by as much as the sponge picture suggests they should. Four questions followed that no further repeat of the same experiment would answer:

  1. Why partial, and how much more aptamer?
  2. Why does the dual binder lose?
  3. One suppression, three phenotypes?
  4. Which unknown is in the way?

A sponge is a one-to-one binder, and P. aeruginosa makes AHL continuously under positive feedback. So the question is not only whether the affinity is good enough. It is whether the molar budget can hold down a signal the cell keeps regenerating — and that is a quantitative question, which is why it needed a model rather than another plate.

1.2 What the data show

Every treated well is normalised to a control on the same plate, so the response is a ratio and is analysed on the log scale, where ratios are symmetric and additive. Plates enter the model as a random intercept, so replicate wells on one plate are not counted as independent experiments. The plate count is printed beside every row: it is the number that sets how much the interval beside it is worth.

A plate is recovered from its control reading. The workbook records no dates and no plate numbers. Readings that are equal are taken to be the same plate. That is what re-pairs ALS-5 and A16-2 across nine swarming plates, which is what makes the key comparison below a within-plate one; it also merges two pyocyanin rows that both read 0.102, costing that assay a plate.

All three aptamers inhibit swarming and pyocyanin. All six of those intervals exclude no effect (p between 2 × 10−6 and 0.019), and the effects agree in direction and in size across two phenotypes measured in different ways.

Biofilm is not resolvable as run. Its point estimates sit with the others at 21–25 % inhibition, but each treatment was measured on two plates: nothing reaches significance (p = 0.16–0.22) and no plate-level interval is reportable. Against a 0.80–1.25 equivalence margin, no treatment in any assay is demonstrated equivalent to no effect either; for two of them — swarming A16-2 and pyocyanin Cb-2 — the effect lies beyond that margin, and the remaining seven are inconclusive (equivalence_tests.csv).

A biofilm row is a well, not a plate. Whether a numbered row is one or the other is an assay-level judgement, and it is the single choice that most changes what this assay appears to show. A swarming row is one colony and is read here as a plate; a biofilm row is one well of a 96-well plate — which is how the Engineering page describes the method — and is read as a replicate. That is why biofilm has two plates per treatment here rather than six or twelve, and why this part reports it as unresolved while the Results page reports the well-level statistics for the same measurements. The reading taken here is the one that claims less.

There is no quorum-sensing-independent control

All three phenotypes measured here are quorum-sensing-dependent, and all three also depend on growth. Showing that a QS-dependent phenotype is inhibited is therefore not the same as showing that the mechanism is quorum sensing: a treatment that simply slowed the bacteria down would reduce swarming, pyocyanin and biofilm together, exactly as observed.

Separating the two needs a phenotype that quorum sensing does not control — a flagellar motility assay is the usual choice — inhibited in the one case and untouched in the other. No such phenotype was measured, so nothing here excludes a growth effect. This is the single largest limitation of the analysis, the model cannot argue it away, and it is the first recommendation in 1.8.

Which comparisons are paired

A contrast between treatments measured on the same plates is a within-plate comparison and the plate effect cancels. A contrast across different plates carries the difference between plates with it. Both can return p ≈ 0.03 and they are not the same evidence, so every contrast is reported with the number of plates it rests on.

1.3 The model

Two signals run the system: L1 = 3-oxo-C12-HSL, which drives the Las circuit, and L2 = C4-HSL, which drives rhl. Each is made by its own synthase, each activates its own receptor, and each receptor raises the synthesis of its own signal. Las additionally gates rhl. The aptamer sits in the same compartment and takes signal out of it.

Assumptions

One compartment
The swarm plate is treated as a single well-mixed volume shared by the bacteria, the signals and the aptamer. A real swarm is a spreading front with gradients.
Fast binding
Receptor occupancy and aptamer binding are in rapid equilibrium relative to synthesis, so both are solved algebraically at every step.
One site per aptamer
Each aptamer molecule binds one ligand at a time. A dual binder does not get two sites; it gets one site that either ligand can occupy.
Mass to moles
An aptamer applied at mass concentration c supplies c / (n × 330 g/mol) moles, with n the length in nucleotides. The 93-nt Cb-2 therefore delivers 2.1× fewer molecules per nanogram than the 44-nt A16-2. This is arithmetic, not a fitted result.
Phenotype
Swarm radius follows rhl receptor occupancy through a power law. The model is calibrated on swarming alone.
Branch
Steady states are reached by integrating forward in time from a signal-free start, which is what a freshly inoculated plate is.

Equations

Receptor occupancy, with Ki the activation constant:

Synthesis with autoinduction, and the gate by which Las sets the scale of rhl:

dL1/dt = v1 ( e1 + (1 − e1) θ1h ) − d1 L1
dL2/dt = v2 ( e2 + (1 − e2) θ2h ) · g(θ1) − d2 L2
g(θ1) = ( θlas + θ1 ) / ( 1 + θlas ) ei is the leaky fraction made without activation, h the cooperativity of the loop, vi the maximum synthesis rate and di the removal rate.

Sequestration, in rapid equilibrium. Writing Dj for aptamer j's occupancy denominator makes the budget visible:

Dj = 1 + L1f/Kd,j1 + L2f/Kd,j2
Afree,j = Aj / Dj
L1,total = L1f + Σj Aj ( L1f/Kd,j1 ) / Dj
L2,total = L2f + Σj Aj ( L2f/Kd,j2 ) / Dj The shared Dj is the budget constraint: one site, two possible occupants. It is the whole reason a dual binder is not simply two specialists in one molecule, and it is the argument of 1.5, Result 3.

And the phenotype that is actually measured:

R / Rcontrol = ( θ2 / θ2,control )β

The model is calibrated on swarming only. It is the one phenotype with a documented mechanistic route from rhl output to the thing that gets measured. Pyocyanin and biofilm are regulated by las, rhl and pqs together, and mapping them directly would mean inventing parameters that no measurement here could fix. 1.6 tests them a different way instead.

The model contains no mechanism by which an aptamer could alter a phenotype that quorum sensing does not control, so for such a phenotype it would predict a ratio of exactly 1 with no fitted parameter. That is a structural property of the model, not a result — nothing here measures such a phenotype, so it is untested, which is the gap 1.2 describes.

One numerical choice is worth naming. Autoinduction makes the system bistable over part of the parameter range, so there is more than one steady state to choose from. Rather than solve for a steady state directly, the model integrates forward from a signal-free start, which picks the branch a freshly inoculated plate actually samples. The integration horizon is not fixed but extended until a drift criterion is met: a sponge buffers the signal and multiplies the relaxation time by roughly (1 + A/Kd), which at a large dose is far beyond any horizon chosen in advance.

1.5 What the model says

Result 1 · The inhibition is partial because the system sits at the switch

At the nominal yield the sponge is weak in pure binding terms. It moves RhlR's half-activation point by only about 9 % (Keff = K2 (1 + Afree/Kd)), which on its own would take about 4 % off the rhl output. The autoinduction loop turns that into the 36 % that was measured — an amplification of about nine.

The same amplification cuts the other way. It puts the system right at the edge of the switch, where a small change in dose produces a large change in outcome — which is the model's explanation both for why the measured effects are partial and for why they are not reproducible to the percent.

Full suppression is a different matter: the median is 11× and the range reaches 49×. We do not treat it as a target.

Result 2 · A C12-only aptamer is predicted not to do this job

ALS-5 reaches swarming only indirectly, through the Las → rhl gate, and in the model that route saturates: no dose of a 3-oxo-C12-HSL-only aptamer reaches 50 % inhibition of swarming, in 269 of 296 calibrated draws (91 %). That is the flat blue curve in the figure above.

This is a prediction, not a result. The data do not have the resolution to test it: it would be tested by a dose series, and what exists is one dose. The swarming difference between ALS-5 and A16-2 is consistent with it (1.2, p = 0.028 on nine shared plates), but consistency at a single dose does not distinguish “ALS-5 saturates” from “ALS-5 is simply weaker here”. 1.8 says what would settle it.

Result 3 · Length, not dual specificity, is what costs Cb-2

This one reverses the obvious guess. A dual binder divides one site between two ligands, so dual specificity looks like the thing that should cost it. Decomposing the A16-2 → Cb-2 gap says otherwise, in two steps that point in opposite directions.

Held out again: the other two phenotypes

The model is calibrated on swarming. Pyocyanin and biofilm are not independent of it — all three are supposed to be downstream of the same quorum-sensing suppression — so one set of receptor occupancies ought to account for all three at once. That is a test the model can fail. Each phenotype is written as a power law in the two occupancies:

P / Pcontrol = ( θ1 / θ1,0 )w1 · ( θ2 / θ2,0 )w2

ALS-5 and A16-2 determine the two exponents exactly. A16-2 binds C4-HSL only and las is upstream of rhl, so it leaves θ1 untouched and isolates w2; ALS-5 then gives w1. Cb-2 is left out of all three fits and becomes a held-out prediction in each assay.

1.8 What it changed

Seven things went back to the wet lab, in the order they can be acted on. None of them had been carried out when this page was written; where one is completed before the wiki freeze, this list should be rewritten to say what happened.

  1. Measure a phenotype that quorum sensing does not control, on the same plates as swarming. A flagellar motility assay is the usual choice. This is the single highest-value measurement missing here: without it nothing in this part excludes the possibility that the aptamers simply slow the bacteria down, and every conclusion inherits that caveat. One plate set carrying both settles it.
  2. Raise the aptamer yield by about 1.6×, and expect to cross 50 % swarming inhibition. The most robust dose prediction here (90 % range 1.0–2.6× across all three sources of uncertainty). Full suppression is a different matter: median 11×, reaching 49×.
  3. Measure β: rhamnolipid quantification alongside swarm radius. It is the most influential parameter in the model (PRCC −0.54) and it is currently assumed, not measured. 1.7 shows that the parameters, not the measurements, are what hold the predictions back, and this is the parameter at the top of that list.
  4. Run a dose series of A16-2 on swarm plates (0, 0.25×, 1×, 4× the current yield, same plate). The model predicts a steep transition between 1× and 2× — a falsifiable shape, not just a number — and it is what would test the claim in Result 2 that a C12-only aptamer saturates.
  5. Record and report the applied aptamer concentration in mol/L, not ng/µL, and compare constructs per mole. Cb-2's predicted deficit against A16-2 is a molar-budget effect of being 93 nt long; per molecule it is the better sponge. This is also the one missing number that would turn the failed Cb-2 prediction into a real test. All the model can currently say is that the dose was above about 3 ng/µL — and a bound is not a record.
  6. Put Cb-2 on the same plates as the other two. Every Cb-2 comparison in swarming is currently across different plates, which costs more statistical power than any extra replicate would buy back — and 1.6 leaves a residual that is either a real difference in Cb-2's affinities or an artefact of exactly that isolation. One shared-plate run separates them.
  7. Biofilm needs more plates, not more wells. Three plates is the minimum for any plate-level interval and each treatment has two; detecting a 20 % effect at this assay's plate-to-plate spread needs about nine. The wells within a plate are already plentiful and are not what the assay is short of. Source: power_requirements.csv.

1.9 Assumptions and limits

  • No phenotype outside quorum-sensing control was measured, so a growth effect cannot be excluded. This is the largest limitation here; 1.2 states it in full and 1.8 opens with it.
  • The applied aptamer concentration on a plate was never recorded. The model uses the one measured ssDNA concentration (7.797 ng/µL) as the nominal dose and sweeps it over 1–30 ng/µL. Under equal-mass dosing the Cb-2 prediction fails; under equal-molar dosing it fails in the other direction. For the two synthesised aptamers this is a record-keeping gap; for Cb-2, a crude extract, the quantity was never measured at all.
  • No dose–response data exist, so the A16-2 affinity is inferred from a single effect size rather than measured. Its fitted value (Kd ≈ 5.6 µM) is weak for an aptamer and should be read as an effective parameter that also absorbs any error in the assumed dose.
  • β is assumed, not measured, and is the most influential parameter in the model. It is fixed at 1 purely so that the number of free parameters equals the number of calibration points.
  • Cb-2's affinities are assumed equal to the specialists'. No measurement exists. This is the optimistic case, so the predicted length penalty is a lower bound on the real one.
  • Every Cb-2 comparison in swarming is unpaired. Cb-2 shares no plate with ALS-5 or A16-2, so those contrasts carry plate-to-plate variation that the paired contrasts do not.
  • Biofilm has two plates per treatment and supports no interval. Its point estimates are consistent with the other assays and that is all they are.
  • The two calibration targets are resampled independently. ALS-5 and A16-2 sit on nine of the same plates, so their errors are really positively correlated; pairing independent draws makes the difference between them — which is what pins the fit — more variable than it is. The data uncertainty reported here is therefore an upper bound, and 1.7 shows it is not the dominant source in any case.
  • The reconciling dose in 1.6 sits on a discontinuity. The least-squares optimum lands at 8.6932 ng/µL, the first dose past the switch; at 8.6930 the model predicts 0.991 for swarming instead of 0.659. The fold is the edge of a jump, not an interior optimum, and should not be quoted to more than the two decimals given.
  • The workbook records no dates, so a plate is identified by its control reading. That recovers which treatments were measured side by side, which is what the paired contrasts rest on, but it cannot tell whether two plates were run on the same day. Two pyocyanin rows quote the same control reading and are read as one plate rather than two.
  • Whether a numbered row is a plate or a well is an assay-level judgement. Read the other way, biofilm would clear the three-plate floor and report intervals; the reading taken here is the one that claims less (1.2).
  • Two Cb-2 biofilm percentages have no raw reading anywhere in the workbook and are excluded by a traceability rule fixed before they were looked at. They sit above the values that can be traced, so excluding them makes the Cb-2 biofilm result look stronger; in the event it moves the well-level mean only from 64.3 % to 65.6 % of control, and changes nothing that is significant.
  • The swarm plate is treated as one well-mixed compartment. A real swarm is a spreading front with gradients in cell density and signal; the model replaces that with a single effective concentration, and 1.6 shows where that bites.
  • Whether the aptamer acts inside or outside the cell is not resolved. The model places the sponge in the same compartment as the signal. If sequestration happens in the engineered E. coli cytoplasm, the two AHLs would have to cross a membrane first and would be affected differently. That term is absent here.

Part 2

Imaging model: from photograph to millimetres

2.1 The question

Colony sizes are measured by hand, one colony at a time. The work is slow and it has to be repeated for every plate. This part of the project is a tool built for the wet lab: it takes one photograph of a plate and returns every colony's size in millimetres, with each figure drawn across the colony it belongs to, so a reading can always be checked against what it was taken from.

It is not a model of the biology. What it models is the measurement — how a count of pixels becomes a length in millimetres, what the picture itself has to supply for that conversion to hold, and which of the bright things on a plate are colonies and which are not.

2.2 The model

  1. 1 Find the dish Circle transform on a downscaled copy; if that returns nothing, the largest thresholded shape instead. Gives the scale and the boundary in one step. dish ø ÷ diameter in px → mm/px
  2. 2 Flatten the lighting A heavily blurred copy is taken as the background and subtracted, removing the gradient across the plate.
  3. Which light is this?
    • White Brightness Otsu on the flattened image.
    • UV Colour Green-to-blue ratio: fluorescence is a question about hue, not about being bright.
  4. 3 Keep only what has a core A loose outline and a much stricter core. A region survives only if it holds enough core pixels — a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through.
  5. Convex enough to be one colony?
    • Yes Leave it alone Splitting a clean colony is the worse mistake.
    • No Watershed Seeded from the deepest point of each lobe.
  6. 4 Measure and annotate Longest span, equivalent diameter, area — drawn onto the photograph with a scale bar.
{# 只留流程圖沒帶到的理由，並點名用到的 CV 技術。流程本身不要在這裡重述一次。 #}

The pipeline is OpenCV throughout. A Hough circle transform[2] finds the dish, run on a downscaled copy — the dish is the largest thing in the frame, so the search does not need full resolution — with Otsu thresholding[3] as the fallback. Subtracting a large-σ Gaussian flattens the lighting. White light and UV are then two problems rather than one with a switch: brightness against the agar is a question of intensity, answered by Otsu; fluorescence is a question of hue, answered by the green-to-blue channel ratio, with the choice between them made from the blue-violet fraction of the frame in HSV.

The hard part was never finding bright things. It was telling a colony from a reflection off the agar or the lid, which can be just as bright. Thresholding twice — a loose outline and a stricter core — and keeping only the connected components that hold enough core pixels settles it: a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through. Solidity and aspect ratio remove what is left. Touching colonies are split only where solidity says they need it, by a marker-controlled watershed seeded from the distance transform, because breaking a clean colony apart is the worse error. The figure drawn on the photograph is the Feret diameter.

2.6 What it changed

{# 三點都是團隊給的。第 1、3 點描述的是程式實際的行為與架構（標註輸出、 無學習模型），第 2 點指回 2.5 的數字而不另外下形容詞。 「免費方案的主機」是團隊告知的部署方式——站內其他頁目前沒有寫到部署環境， 之後 Software 頁若補上部署說明，這裡要跟它一致。 #}

Three things this changed about how the wet lab measures a plate.

  1. A plate is read in one pass, and the reading comes back drawn on the photograph. Every colony on the plate is measured together rather than one at a time, and the output is the picture itself with each span, its figure and a scale bar marked on it. The number never has to be copied from an instrument into a notebook, and any reading can be checked against the colony it came from without going back to the plate.
  2. The reading is good enough to use. Against hand measurement it agrees to within 0.71 mm on average, every colony inside 2 mm, with no bias the data can separate from zero (2.5). That is finer than the differences the assays are built to compare.
  3. It is small enough to run anywhere. The pipeline is classical computer vision — thresholds, morphology, a watershed, a convex hull — so there is no learned model, no weights to load and no inference step. It needs no GPU and runs on a free hosting tier, which is what let it go to the people doing the measuring rather than staying on one machine in the lab.

2.7 Limits

{# 兩點都是團隊給的。第 2 點末尾那句「同一盤內的比較不受影響」是從 「比例尺是全盤共用的一個係數」推出來的：係數錯了，盤上每顆都錯同樣的 倍率，所以盤內的相對比較不變。這是推論，不是量測結果，寫法上沒有 當成已驗證的結論。 #}
  • A strong enough reflection can still be read as a colony. The two-threshold test rejects the glare we have photographed — a reflection is bright without being bright all the way through, so it clears the loose outline and fails the core (2.4 shows one being dropped). What it cannot reject is a reflection that is both bright and even enough to have a dense centre of its own, which a fluorescent tube close to the plate, or a lid left on, can produce. The plates here were photographed without a lid and away from direct overhead light; that is a handling rule rather than something the program enforces.
  • The plate has to be photographed square-on. Every millimetre on the page comes from one number: the dish diameter the operator enters, divided by the dish diameter the program measures in pixels. That division assumes the dish appears as a circle. Photographed off-axis it projects as an ellipse, the circle fit lands somewhere between its long and short axis, and the scale is wrong by however much the tilt was. The error is systematic rather than random — one scale factor is shared by the whole plate, so every colony on it is out by the same proportion. A comparison made within a single plate, which is how the assays read, should therefore survive a small tilt even though the absolute millimetres would not; we have not measured how large a tilt that holds for.

Each cycle reads the sensor three times: dark, lit, dark. A cycle whose dark read before the flash is not zero on all twelve channels is void, because that is what room light reaching the detector looks like. Each tube is read for three cycles and enters every model as the mean of its valid ones. The cycles are three looks at one tube, not three samples, so the tube is the unit throughout. Across nine sets of logs from eight days the instrument recorded 657 rows, of which 631 are valid (96.0 %). The other 26 are accounted for: 18 are deliberate gate checks, run with the LED not armed or disabled, and 8 are cycles the firmware voided for a non-zero dark read. No row saturated.

The same record sets the scale of the noise. Within one insertion the three cycles differ by about 0.69 counts (root mean square), so a tube mean carries 0.27 to 0.43 counts of it. In four of seven replicate groups the spread left over between tubes is larger than that: there, seating, sampling, preparation or the cuvette contribute more than the detector's own cycle noise.

3.4 Deciding what to measure, and when to stop

One question the instrument has to answer is whether other cells in the sample change the green reading. The comparison is a pair of tubes with the same GFP culture in them: A is 100 µL of GFP stock with 100 µL of medium, C is 100 µL of GFP stock with 100 µL of non-GFP culture. The quantity of interest is C − A.

Before the session, a simulation with the noise we had seen so far settled how many pairs to make. After the session, the same calculation was redone with the noise the session actually showed.

The six pairs gave a mean C − A of −0.39 counts, with a nominal 95 % interval from −1.88 to +1.10, which is −20.1 % to +11.8 % of A. The session did not resolve a difference. That is not the same as showing there is none: the honest statement is that if non-GFP culture shifts the reading, this session places the shift somewhere in that range.

At the observed noise a true difference would have to be about 21.8 % of A before six more pairs had an 80 % chance of resolving it, and resolving a 10 % difference would take 21 pairs. On those numbers the analysis recommended against repeating the comparison and for spending the remaining bench time elsewhere.

Lessons for the next design 3 notes Show Hide
More tubes
Because tube-to-tube spread exceeds cycle noise, precision comes from more tubes or more pairs, not from more cycles per tube.
Confound
The cuvettes were washed and reused, so which use of a cuvette it was, and whether A or C was read first, coincide exactly in that session. One cuvette per tube, or a cross-tabulation of the factors at the design stage, would have kept them apart.
Attribution
C receives culture, cells together with their spent medium, where A receives fresh medium. C − A is the difference between two recipes and cannot be assigned to the cells alone.

3.6 After the rebuild: what carried over

The models above describe the first reader. It was rebuilt on 2026-10-03, and three sessions on the rebuilt reader, a dilution series, a session on AHL plates and one on how dense a culture can be, show what carries over. The inverse models did not carry over

The three models that need no OD, F4 alone, the four-channel ridge and a random forest, were applied unchanged to the twelve tubes of the dilution series, whose share of GFP culture is known. None did better than always guessing the mean of the training tubes: the best was off by 27.1 points on average against 27.8, and that only after the counts were scaled down for the higher LED current. 3.8 How the numbers were produced

The tables behind sections 3.2 to 3.4 were not typed in by hand. Each is generated by a script that reads the instrument's raw serial logs and the bench record, and is then recomputed by a second script, written separately, that compares the two cell by cell. The least-squares fits are solved in exact rational arithmetic and rounded only for display. Every tube mean the models use is listed in those tables, including the readings of zero and the tubes that were re-prepared. The four-channel study keeps a record of every model it fitted and refits its held-out predictions as a check. The three figures in 3.2 and 3.3 are drawn from the twenty tube means themselves, with their slopes and held-out errors computed in the page. The numbers in 3.6 are computed by scripts from the raw logs of the two later sessions.

The scripts and the raw logs are held by the team and are not published. The instrument that produced the readings is described on the Hardware page, and the software that turns a reading into a concentration on the Software page.
