Part 1

Inhibition model

Partial inhibition, a dose hypothesis, and a Cb-2 prediction the model could not explain.

Our inhibition module releases single-stranded DNA aptamers that bind the acyl-homoserine lactone (AHL) signals of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The idea is a molecular sponge: reduce the free signal available to receptors. The experiments show partial inhibition. We fitted a model to ALS-5 and A16-2 swarming data, used it to estimate a higher A16-2 dose to test, and checked its predictions against Cb-2, which was excluded from calibration.

  • Cb-2 Inhibition underestimated in all three assays One held-out treatment, checked across three phenotypes. The baseline predictions miss each measured interval; these are not three independent validation experiments. 1.4
  • 1.6× Model-estimated dose to test About 1.6× the nominal A16-2 dose for 50 % swarming inhibition (90 % range 1.0–2.6×). This is an unvalidated estimate: applied plate concentration was not recorded and β is assumed. 1.5

Code: GitLab (MIT). Every number in this part comes from a file in that repository's results/, named under the table or figure that uses it. The measurements themselves are the wet lab's, and are reported on the Results page.

{# ========================= 1.1 the question ========================= #}

1.1 The question

Three aptamers were tested: ALS-5 (35 nt, binds 3-oxo-C12-HSL, the las signal), A16-2 (44 nt, binds C4-HSL, the rhl signal) and Cb-2 (93 nt, binds both).[1] They did not arrive the same way. ALS-5 and A16-2 are chemically synthesised reference oligonucleotides; Cb-2 is the construct this project built, applied as a TRIzol extract of the retron culture rather than as a purified oligonucleotide. That asymmetry runs through everything below, and 1.4 is where it bites.

Every treatment reduced swarming and pyocyanin, and two of the three reduced biofilm — ALS-5's biofilm effect is borderline (1.2). None of them came close to switching the system off. The wet lab's own account of these assays, with the plate photographs and its own statistics, is on the Results page; this part is a separate re-analysis of the same wells (1.2).

A measurement says that much. It does not say why the effect stops where it does, or what to change next. Those are the two questions this model exists to answer:

  1. Does the model predict a treatment it was never fitted to? 1.4
  2. What higher A16-2 dose should the next experiment test? 1.5

They are answered in that order on purpose. A model that has not been put against a measurement it did not see has not earned the right to extrapolate, so the held-out test comes first and the dose estimate afterwards — carrying whatever the test leaves of it.

Replicate units, statistical methods, and how these numbers relate to the Results page

Every treated well is normalised to a control on the same plate, so each reading is a ratio and is analysed on the log scale. What counts as one experiment differs between the assays: for swarming and pyocyanin the plate is the unit and plates enter the model as a random intercept, so repeated readings on one plate are not counted as independent experiments; for biofilm every row is a separately grown biological replicate, which is the wet lab's own reading of its own assay (2026-10-15), and the replicates are the units.

These are not the same numbers the Results page reports, and they should not be. Results gives each treatment's arithmetic mean against its untreated control with a one-sample t test, which is the wet lab's own analysis. This part re-reads the same wells on the log scale with the plate as a random intercept, which is what the model needs as a calibration target. The two readings were recomputed side by side and agree in direction and significance for all nine treatment–assay pairs; the largest disagreement in inhibition is 2.5 percentage points (Cb-2 biofilm, 34.2 % there against 36.7 % here), and the one verdict that is close enough to move is ALS-5's biofilm result (p = 0.053 there, 0.057 here — borderline under either). Source: prism_crosscheck.csv.

Two row counts differ as well, and the reason is traceability rather than analysis. Cb-2's biofilm series has fourteen wells on the wet lab's sheet; two of them could not be traced to a control, so this part uses twelve. Cb-2's swarming series has ten readings, of which seven are read against the first control on their plate rather than their own row and one is ambiguous. Those decisions are recorded cell by cell in prism_crosscheck.csv rather than left to the reader to infer.

Assay-by-assay interpretation and supporting analyses

All three aptamers inhibit swarming and pyocyanin. All six of those intervals exclude no effect (p between 0.000002 and 0.019), and the effects agree in direction and size across two independent phenotypes.

Biofilm resolves for Cb-2 and for A16-2, and not for ALS-5. Cb-2 removes 36.7 % of the biofilm [28.8, 43.7] on twelve replicates (p = 3×10−6) and A16-2 12.6 % [2.7, 21.5] on six (p = 0.023); ALS-5's 11.5 % does not clear the line (p = 0.057).

The ordering reverses between assays. A16-2 is the strongest in swarming, Cb-2 in pyocyanin and biofilm. The model is calibrated on the swarming measurements alone (1.3), and nothing on this page explains that reversal.

There is no quorum-sensing-independent control, and that limits everything below. All three phenotypes depend on quorum sensing, and all three also depend on growth. A treatment that simply slowed the bacteria down would reduce swarming, pyocyanin and biofilm together, exactly as observed. Separating the two needs a phenotype quorum sensing does not control — a flagellar motility assay is the usual choice — inhibited in the one case and untouched in the other. No such phenotype was measured, so nothing here excludes a growth effect. The model cannot argue this away, and it is the first thing 1.6 asks for.

One control the assays did carry is worth stating, because it narrows the alternatives even though it does not close this gap. Every assay included a BL21 extract control — E. coli BL21(DE3) without the retron plasmid, taken through the same culture, harvest and TRIzol extraction as Cb-2 — and it was not significantly different from the untreated control in any of the three phenotypes (−2.3 % swarming, +1.4 % pyocyanin, −6.9 % biofilm, all not significant; Results). That makes the extraction background an unlikely explanation for the effects. It says nothing about growth, because the extract control is not a quorum-sensing-independent phenotype, so the paragraph above stands. The model does not use this control: it enters nowhere in the calibration.

The analysis behind this section also reports which comparisons are paired and which are not, what each assay could have detected, whether the plates within a treatment agree, and the wet lab's own one-sample test recomputed beside these effects. Those are in the repository: contrasts.csv, equivalence_tests.csv, plate_split.csv, power_requirements.csv and prism_crosscheck.csv, with section 1 of model_report.md.

{# ========================= 1.3 the model ========================= #}

1.3 The model

Two signals, one aptamer pool, one feedback loop. The aptamer takes signal out of circulation; the bacteria make more of it, and how much more depends on how much of it the receptors can still see. Everything the model does follows from writing those two sentences down.

The core link from signalling to the measured output is:

R / Rcontrol = ( θ2 / θ2,control )β

Here R is swarm radius and θ2 is rhl receptor occupancy. We assume β = 1; this mapping is not independently measured.

Full equations: receptor occupancy, feedback and binding balance

Receptor occupancy, with Ki the activation constant:

θi = {{ frac('Li,free', 'Ki + Li,free') }}

Synthesis with autoinduction, and the gate by which las sets the scale of rhl:

dL1/dt = v1 ( e1 + (1 − e1) θ1h ) − d1 L1
dL2/dt = v2 ( e2 + (1 − e2) θ2h ) · g(θ1) − d2 L2
g(θ1) = ( θlas + θ1 ) / ( 1 + θlas ) L1 is 3-oxo-C12-HSL and L2 is C4-HSL. ei is the leaky fraction made without activation, h the cooperativity of the loop, vi the maximum synthesis rate and di the removal rate.

Sequestration, in rapid equilibrium. Each aptamer carries one site that both ligands compete for, so writing Dj for its occupancy denominator makes the budget visible:

Dj = 1 + L1f/Kd,j1 + L2f/Kd,j2
L1,total = L1f + Σj Aj ( L1f/Kd,j1 ) / Dj
L2,total = L2f + Σj Aj ( L2f/Kd,j2 ) / Dj The shared Dj is the budget constraint: one site, two possible occupants. It is the whole reason a dual binder is not simply two specialists in one molecule.

The model is calibrated on swarming only. It is the one phenotype with a documented mechanistic route from rhl output to the thing that gets measured. Pyocyanin and biofilm are regulated by las, rhl and pqs together, and mapping them directly would mean inventing parameters no measurement here could fix. 1.4 tests them a different way instead.

Four assumptions carry the rest, and each of them is a place the model can be wrong: the plate is one well-mixed compartment (1.4 measures what that costs); binding is fast compared with synthesis; β = 1, so a halving of rhl occupancy halves the swarm radius; and Cb-2's affinities equal the specialists', which is the optimistic case for Cb-2 and therefore makes any predicted shortfall a lower bound.

Calibration fits two parameters to two swarming observations, ALS-5 and A16-2. Cb-2 is excluded from that fit. Matching the calibration points therefore does not independently validate the model.

The two calibration targets are not the two ratios in 1.2, and the difference is deliberate. Calibration takes the plate-resampled estimates (0.766 and 0.639) rather than the mixed model's (0.768 and 0.642), because the fit runs once per bootstrap resample and has to take its target from the same resampling that generates the draws. The two readings differ by less than a third of a percentage point and nothing in this part turns on which is used.

The rest are literature or stated assumption, and each is swept over its full range rather than trusted at its nominal value: the LasR and RhlR activation constants (50 nM, reported across three orders of magnitude;[7] and 1000 nM, with no published equilibrium constant to fix it[8]), the cooperativity of the autoinduction loop (h = 2, standard in AHL models[6]), the uninhibited signal levels that the synthesis rates are back-calculated from rather than guessed (1000 nM each, over 60–2600 and 56–5000[4][5]), the two AHL removal rates, set from the lactonolysis half-lives and the faster loss of the shorter acyl chain (0.014 and 0.087 h−1, which cancel at steady state once synthesis is calibrated to those signal levels[9]), and the applied dose.

That applied dose is one number, measured on one of the three treatments, and used for all of them. It is 7.797 ng/µL by Nanodrop of TRIzol-extracted retron ssDNA (2026-07-31), the only aptamer quantity ever measured in this project. It is applied in the model to ALS-5 and A16-2 as well, which were chemically synthesised and never quantified here, and for Cb-2 it is total extracted single-stranded DNA rather than Cb-2 aptamer, so it is an upper bound on the Cb-2 aptamer actually present. The concentration that reached a plate was never recorded for any treatment, which is why the dose is swept over 1–30 ng/µL and carried as its own source of uncertainty rather than fixed. The three design lengths, 35, 44 and 93 nt, turn the nominal dose into 675, 537 and 254 nM. 1.4 is where this assumption is tested, and 1.7 is where it is booked as a limit.

Source: params/parameters.csv (every parameter, with its units, range and citation) and model_calibration.csv. ALS-5's provenance is settled: Wu et al. attribute the 3-oxo-C12-HSL aptamer to Zhao et al. 2013 and the C4-HSL one to Zhao et al. 2019, truncate the second to A16-2, and fuse the two into Cb-2.[1] What is unresolved is not which paper but which measurement: the value fixed here is 6.5× tighter than Wu et al.'s own SYBR Green I figure for the same sequence, and the model has not been re-run on it. Booked as a limit in 1.7 rather than settled here.

Calibration is not run once. It is run on 400 plate-level bootstrap resamples of those two swarming ratios, one per uncertainty draw, so the uncertainty in the data reaches the predictions instead of stopping at a point estimate. The two parameters are pinned by the difference between ALS-5 and A16-2, and that difference is resolvable on nine shared plates, which is what makes the rest of this part worth reading.

104 of those 400 draws do not calibrate, and every quantile on this page is taken over the 296 that do. Almost all of them (102) are draws where the literature parameters came out wide enough that the observed ALS-5 effect is unreachable at any gating strength; the other two left the solver with more than one root. Those draws carry no prediction, so excluding them is the only option — but it is a selection, and it runs in one direction: the discarded draws are the ones the model fits worst. The counts are reported rather than smoothed, here and in uncertainty_summary.csv. Source: calibration_draws.csv.

{# ========================= 1.4 testing it ========================= #}

1.4 Cb-2: where the predictions miss

Cb-2 was excluded from calibration and is the only measured held-out treatment. Under the stated dosing and affinity assumptions, the baseline prediction underestimates inhibition in all three assays. These are checks across three phenotypes of one treatment, rather than three independent validation experiments. The applied plate concentration was not recorded, so the check is conditional on the model's dosing convention.

Cb-2 is the weakest of the three as a held-out test, and it is the one we have. It is the only treatment applied as a crude extract: its nominal dose is total TRIzol-extracted ssDNA, not quantified Cb-2 aptamer (1.3). Its affinities are assumed rather than measured here, though Wu et al. report them and What is left below reads the miss against those numbers.[1] And it is the treatment whose swarming plates it shares with nothing else. A held-out test is only as strong as the number fed into it, and this one is conditional on three assumptions at once — which is why the paragraphs below work through what each of them would have to be for the miss to go away, instead of reading the miss as a finding about Cb-2's biology.

Why the model expects so little from Cb-2

A prediction of 0.992 is the model saying Cb-2 should do essentially nothing, and it is worth seeing where that comes from, because the obvious guess is wrong. Binding two ligands is not what costs Cb-2. Taking A16-2 apart into the two things that distinguish it from Cb-2 separates them:

Two things follow. The first is a design statement that does not depend on the size of any measured effect, because it is arithmetic on two lengths: Cb-2 is worth keeping only if it is dosed per mole, not per mass — which for the retron route means the quantity to compare is molecules produced per cell. 1.6 asks for exactly that measurement.

The second is a caution about this whole section. The predicted deficit is a consequence of the equal-mass convention, and that convention rests on a nominal concentration nobody recorded (1.3). The figure explains the prediction; it does not defend it.

Part of the miss is the model's own fault

Swarming's miss has a second reading. One well-mixed compartment crosses the autoinduction switch all at once: at 8.69 ng/µL the model predicts 0.991 and at 8.70 ng/µL it predicts 0.658, jumping straight over the measured 0.820. No real plate can be discontinuous in dose, so the whole analysis was run again over a plate of patches — a spread of local dose, swept rather than fitted. Every spread in a plausible range puts the held-out swarming prediction back inside its measured interval, so that one miss is a structural artefact rather than a result. The other two are not: pyocyanin and biofilm sit outside at every spread.

What the smoothing does not move matters as much. The fit barely notices it — θlas goes from 14.8 to 13.6 and the A16-2 affinity stays inside 1.5 % — and neither does the dose ratio: at the single calibration, 1.55× for half suppression under one compartment against 1.65× at a spread of 0.5. That matters for what comes next. The one quantity this part carries forward out of a failed test is the dose estimate of 1.5, and it does not rest on the compartment assumption that the swarming miss turned out to be about. The model still has no held-out test it passes in all three phenotypes.

What the one-compartment assumption costs

The swarming miss is structural, and not by tuning. Every spread from 0.2 to 0.7 in log standard deviation puts the held-out swarming prediction inside its measured interval, while the spread itself moves over a factor of three and a half; the residual crosses zero near 0.5. A prediction that landed on the measurement at one spread and nowhere else would be a fitted parameter wearing a structural hat, and this is not that.

The other two misses are not structural. At the widest spread pyocyanin reaches only 0.857 against a measured 0.716, and biofilm 0.908 against 0.633. Both sit outside at every spread. Swarming is linear in rhl occupancy and inherits the whole jump, so smoothing is worth 0.21 in ratio there; the sub-linear exponents of the other two had already compressed it. So the model still has no held-out test it passes in all three phenotypes.

What does not move is worth as much as what does. The fit is nearly indifferent to the structure — θlas goes from 14.8 to 13.6, the A16-2 affinity stays inside 1.5 % — and so is the headline dose claim: 1.55× for half suppression under one compartment against 1.65× at a spread of 0.5. Nothing here measures plate heterogeneity, so the spread is an assumption swept over a plausible range, not a parameter with a value; the swarming prediction landing inside its interval under that assumption says what the structure can accommodate, and does not withdraw the failed test above. Every published output in results/ is the one-compartment model. Source: structure_comparison.csv.

The simplest explanation, and why it does not work

Before any claim about Cb-2's binding, there is a duller candidate that has to be ruled out first: the applied Cb-2 dose was simply higher than the model assumes. It is the explanation the experiment most invites, because Cb-2's nominal dose is total extracted ssDNA rather than quantified aptamer (1.3), and because a single wrong dose would push all three predictions the same way — which is exactly what the residuals do.

That makes it a test rather than an excuse: one free parameter against three observations. Fitting the dose that best reconciles all three gives 1.11× the assumption, and at that dose the three phenotypes do not come into line together.

This does not mean the dose is right; it means the dose alone cannot be what is wrong.

What is left

Two explanations survive, and this experiment cannot separate them. Either Cb-2 really binds C4-HSL more tightly than A16-2 — inverting the model on the swarming measurement asks for Kd ≈ 158 nM, 35× tighter than A16-2's fitted value, and that solution is real — or Cb-2's swarming estimate is displaced by having been measured on five plates it shares with nothing else. Both are testable, and 1.6 says how.

The published affinities argue against the first. Wu et al. measured Cb-2 and the specialists side by side. Against C4-HSL, Cb-2's Kd is 2.21 ± 1.05 µM to A16-2's 2.66 ± 1.96 µM by ITC, and by SYBR Green I 85.72 ± 13.15 nM to the parent A16's 105.8 ± 15.14 nM.[1] Both assays put Cb-2 about 1.2× tighter than the C4-HSL specialist, not 35×. Only the ratio carries over — the fitted 5571 nM is an effective plate parameter and those are in-vitro constants — but a 35-fold gap is not something either assay sees, which leaves the plate-isolation explanation and the one below carrying more of the weight.

A third possibility sits outside the model entirely and the data cannot exclude it. Cb-2 was applied as a crude cellular extract. The BL21 extract control makes a generic extraction artefact unlikely (1.2), but it does not establish that the aptamer is what acts, nor that the 93 nt species on the gel is the whole of what was applied. Resolving that is a purification and quantification question for the wet lab, not something a sequestration model can settle.

So the model fails the only held-out test it has. That is the honest headline of this part, and the next section has to be read with it in hand.

{# ========================= 1.5 how much more aptamer ========================= #}

1.5 A higher dose to test

A model that has just missed its held-out treatment is not entitled to much. What it is still entitled to is the comparison it was actually calibrated on: ALS-5 against A16-2, on nine shared swarming plates, with Cb-2 nowhere in the fit. That comparison is untouched by the Cb-2 miss, and it is the only thing the estimate below rests on.

At the yield the wet lab reached, A16-2 took about a third off swarming. Sweeping the applied dose across the calibrated draws says how much further that yield would have to go.

Half suppression is a hypothesis for the next dose experiment. Across 296 draws spanning three orders of magnitude in the LasR constant, more than an order of magnitude in both signal levels and 1–30 ng/µL of applied dose, 95 % of draws put the requirement for half suppression at or below 2.6× — a 95th percentile, not a bound: the remaining draws run higher. The same percentile for 90 % inhibition is 49×, which is not an engineering target but a statement that the model cannot say.

That flat ALS-5 curve is a prediction, not a result. ALS-5 reaches swarming only indirectly, through the las-to-rhl gate, and in the model that route saturates: no dose reaches 50 % inhibition in 269 of 296 calibrated draws (91 %). The data cannot test it. Testing it needs a dose series, and what exists is one dose: the measured ALS-5–A16-2 difference is consistent with saturation, but consistency at a single dose does not separate “ALS-5 saturates” from “ALS-5 is simply weaker here”. 1.6 says what would settle it.

This dose estimate has not been experimentally validated. It depends on the assumed occupancy-to-radius mapping and a nominal ssDNA concentration rather than a recorded applied plate dose. Use it to choose a dose series, not as a demonstrated yield requirement.

Read beside 1.4, that is the whole of what this model offers: one dose series worth running, and a held-out test it did not pass. 1.6 turns both into experiments.

Full recommendations and proposed experimental comparisons
  1. Measure a phenotype quorum sensing does not control, on the same plates as swarming. A flagellar motility assay is the usual choice. This is the highest-value measurement missing from the project: without it, nothing here excludes the aptamers simply slowing the bacteria down, and every item below inherits that caveat. One plate set carrying both settles it.
  2. Test the model's estimate of about 1.6× the nominal A16-2 dose for 50 % swarming inhibition (90 % range 1.0–2.6×). The estimate is conditional on the model's assumptions. The 90 % inhibition estimate ranges up to 49×, so it does not provide a precise yield target.
  3. Measure β — rhamnolipid quantification alongside swarm radius. It is the most influential parameter in the model and it is currently assumed.
  4. Run a dose series of A16-2 on swarm plates (0, 0.25×, 1×, 4× the current yield, same plate). The model predicts a steep transition between 1× and 2×: a falsifiable shape rather than a number, and the test of 1.5's claim that a C12-only aptamer saturates.
  5. Record the applied concentration in mol/L, and compare constructs per mole. Cb-2's entire predicted deficit is the molar budget of being 93 nt long, and none of it is the cost of binding two ligands (1.4, F5). This is also the one missing number that would turn the failed Cb-2 prediction into a real test.
  6. Put Cb-2 on the same plates as the other two. Every Cb-2 swarming comparison is currently across different plates, which costs more than any extra replicate would buy back — and 1.4 leaves a residual that is either a real difference in Cb-2's affinities or an artefact of exactly that isolation.
  7. Biofilm needs its treatments on shared plates, not more replicates. The assay already has 24 independent replicates and resolves two of its three treatments against their controls. What it cannot do is compare the treatments with each other: ten of Cb-2's twelve replicates sit on a plate carrying nothing else. One run with all three treatments on every plate fixes this and item 6 together.
{# ========================= 1.7 limits ========================= #}

1.7 Limits

Stated plainly, because a model's limits are part of its result. The first is the one that matters most.

  • No quorum-sensing-independent control was measured, so a growth effect cannot be excluded for any of the three phenotypes. Everything above inherits this.
  • The three treatments are not comparable in the way the model treats them. ALS-5 and A16-2 are chemically synthesised oligonucleotides; Cb-2 is a crude TRIzol extract whose nominal dose is total ssDNA rather than quantified aptamer. The model applies one measured concentration to all three. A model that compares constructs per unit mass is only as good as that assumption, and here it is an assumption about two treatments that were never quantified and one whose quantity is an upper bound.
  • The applied aptamer concentration on a swarm plate was never recorded. The model uses the one measured ssDNA concentration as its nominal dose and sweeps it over 1–30 ng/µL. This is a record-keeping gap, not a modelling choice, and under equal-mass dosing the Cb-2 prediction fails one way while under equal-molar dosing it fails the other.
  • No dose-response data exist, so A16-2's affinity is inferred from a single effect size rather than measured. Its fitted value (≈ 5.6 µM) should be read as an effective parameter that also absorbs any error in the assumed dose. It is about twice Wu et al.'s ITC figure for the same sequence (2.66 ± 1.96 µM), and their ITC values for every aptamer in this family fall between 1.7 and 18.3 µM,[1] so a micromolar fit is not the anomaly it looks like. It is still not a measurement of anything: across the calibrated draws it ranges over more than two orders of magnitude (1.3).
  • ALS-5's fixed affinity has not been reconciled with the paper the rest of this project works from. The two citations turn out not to conflict: Wu et al. attribute the 3-oxo-C12-HSL aptamer to Zhao et al. 2013 and the C4-HSL one to Zhao et al. 2019, then truncate the second to A16-2 and fuse the two into Cb-2,[1] so ALS-5 is Zhao et al.'s aptamer carried over unchanged.[2] What is unreconciled is the number. The table fixes 20–35 nM from the screening paper, while Wu et al. measure the same sequence at 179.8 ± 81.31 nM by SYBR Green I and 7.26 ± 6.35 µM by ITC — and those two assays disagree by 19 to 62 times on every sequence the study reports both ways, so there is no single right value to swap in. The model has not been re-run on any of them, and this input is fixed and never swept, so the cost of the choice does not appear in the 90 % column.
  • β is assumed, not measured, and is the most influential parameter in the model. It is fixed at 1 purely so that the number of free parameters equals the number of calibration points.
  • Biofilm rests on the wet lab's statement that its wells are independent biological replicates (2026-10-15). Read as plates instead, the assay has two units per treatment and resolves nothing (p = 0.13–0.45); every biofilm number here would change, and only those.

Three more are stated where they bite rather than repeated here: Cb-2's affinities are assumed equal to the specialists' (1.3, which leaves 1.4 an affinity explanation the data cannot rule out), every Cb-2 swarming comparison is unpaired (1.4), and the plate is one well-mixed compartment, which is what makes the model's switch sharper than any real plate's (1.4). Eleven further limitations on the data handling and the statistics are in section 4 of model_report.md, and METHOD_CHANGES.md records the methods that were withdrawn during this work and why. An earlier version of this page rested on two of them.

Imaging model: from photograph to millimetres

This model now runs inside our software, as Plate Imaging · Agar Vision in LasReader.

2.1 The question

Colony sizes are measured by hand, one colony at a time. The work is slow and it has to be repeated for every plate. This part of the project is a tool built for the wet lab: it takes one photograph of a plate and returns every colony's size in millimetres, with each figure drawn across the colony it belongs to, so a reading can always be checked against what it was taken from.

It is not a model of the biology. What it models is the measurement — how a count of pixels becomes a length in millimetres, what the picture itself has to supply for that conversion to hold, and which of the bright things on a plate are colonies and which are not.

2.2 The model

  1. 1 Find the dish Circle transform on a downscaled copy; if that returns nothing, the largest thresholded shape instead. Gives the scale and the boundary in one step. dish ø ÷ diameter in px → mm/px
  2. 2 Flatten the lighting A heavily blurred copy is taken as the background and subtracted, removing the gradient across the plate.
  3. Which light is this?
    • White Brightness Otsu on the flattened image.
    • UV Colour Green-to-blue ratio: fluorescence is a question about hue, not about being bright.
  4. 3 Keep only what has a core A loose outline and a much stricter core. A region survives only if it holds enough core pixels — a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through.
  5. Convex enough to be one colony?
    • Yes Leave it alone Kept whole and counted as one colony.
    • No Watershed Split apart, and each piece counted as its own colony.
  6. 4 Measure and annotate Longest span, equivalent diameter, area — drawn onto the photograph with a scale bar.

The pipeline is OpenCV throughout. A Hough circle transform[2] finds the dish, run on a downscaled copy — the dish is the largest thing in the frame, so the search does not need full resolution — with Otsu thresholding[3] as the fallback. Subtracting a large-σ Gaussian flattens the lighting. White light and UV are then two problems rather than one with a switch: brightness against the agar is a question of intensity, answered by Otsu; fluorescence is a question of hue, answered by the green-to-blue channel ratio, with the choice between them made from the blue-violet fraction of the frame in HSV.

The difficult part was never finding bright things. It was telling a colony from a reflection off the agar or the lid, which can be just as bright. Thresholding twice — a loose outline and a stricter core — and keeping only the connected components that hold enough core pixels settles it: a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through. Solidity and aspect ratio remove what is left. Touching colonies are split only where solidity says they need it, by a marker-controlled watershed seeded from the distance transform, because breaking a clean colony apart is the worse error. The figure drawn on the photograph is the Feret diameter.

ThresholdWhat it decides

Not a tuning knob but a switch between two routes, so it only has to separate two cases that look nothing alike, and anything in the middle of that range would mean the photograph is lit in some third way the pipeline was not built for.
Detection region at 0.90 of the dish radius How much of the plate is looked at. The outer tenth is given up on purpose: the rim and the meniscus are where reflections collect, and the colonies lost there cost less than the false ones gained.

2.6 What it changed

Three things this changed about how the wet lab measures a plate.

  1. A plate is read in one pass, and the reading comes back drawn on the photograph. Every colony on the plate is measured together rather than one at a time, and the output is the picture itself with each span, its figure and a scale bar marked on it. The number never has to be copied from an instrument into a notebook, and any reading can be checked against the colony it came from without going back to the plate.
  2. The reading is good enough to use. Against hand measurement it agrees to within 0.71 mm on average, every colony inside 2 mm, with no bias the data can separate from zero (2.5). That is finer than the differences the assays are built to compare.
  3. It is small enough to run anywhere. The pipeline is classical computer vision — thresholds, morphology, a watershed, a convex hull — so there is no learned model, no weights to load and no inference step. It needs no GPU and runs on a free hosting tier, which is what let it go to the people doing the measuring rather than staying on one machine in the lab.

2.7 Limits

  • A strong enough reflection can still be read as a colony. The two-threshold test rejects the glare we have photographed — a reflection is bright without being bright all the way through, so it clears the loose outline and fails the core (2.4 shows one being dropped). What it cannot reject is a reflection that is both bright and even enough to have a dense centre of its own, which a fluorescent tube close to the plate, or a lid left on, can produce. The plates here were photographed without a lid and away from direct overhead light; that is a handling rule rather than something the program enforces.
  • The plate has to be photographed square-on. Every millimetre on the page comes from one number: the dish diameter the operator enters, divided by the dish diameter the program measures in pixels. That division assumes the dish appears as a circle. Photographed off-axis it projects as an ellipse, the circle fit lands somewhere between its long and short axis, and the scale is wrong by however much the tilt was. The error is systematic rather than random — one scale factor is shared by the whole plate, so every colony on it is out by the same proportion. A comparison made within a single plate, which is how the assays read, should therefore survive a small tilt even though the absolute millimetres would not; we have not measured how large a tilt that holds for.

Is the reading proportional? Within one session, where the signal is clear of the floor, it is: double the GFP culture and the reading becomes 2.04×.

Why did the same recipe read six times higher on one day than another? Because a volume fraction is not an amount — a volume of thin culture holds less GFP than the same volume of dense culture. Dividing by the density of that day's stock brings the three slopes to within 8 % of each other.

3.5 After the rebuild: what carried over

The models above describe the first reader. Its noise did not come mostly from the detector. In four of seven replicate groups, seating and preparation outweighed the cycle noise (3.1). That was one consideration in the rebuild that followed, alongside the parts that had broken or made loading slow.

The reader was rebuilt on 2026-10-03: five printed parts redrawn around a one-step cuvette seat, and one resistor to bring the LED to its design current. Putting the same tube back now repeats to 0.76 %, against 3.6 to 6.3 % before (Hardware).

Three sessions on the rebuilt reader — a dilution series, a session on AHL plates and one on how dense a culture can be — show what carries over. All three are on the Hardware page, and everything in this section was worked out after their readings were seen.

The inverse models did not carry over

Three models were also fitted on the first reader to read G back from the sensor's channels, none of them needing the stock OD: F4 alone, a ridge regression on four channels, and a random forest. All three were applied unchanged to the twelve tubes of the dilution series, whose share of GFP culture is known. None did better than always guessing the mean of the training tubes: the best was off by 27.1 points on average against 27.8, and that only after the counts were scaled down for the higher LED current. The stock of that session gave more signal for the same share of culture than any stock the models had seen. G, a share of one day's stock, is not a scale that carries from one stock to the next.

A background that rises with scatter

Every tube in 3.1 that held no GFP read exactly zero on the green channel. The plate suspensions were far denser than any of those tubes, and in them the green channel no longer read zero without GFP. That has to be described before any reading from those plates can be used.

What the extra reading follows is scatter. The denser the suspension, the higher a scatter channel, F2, and the higher the green channel reads. So the description is the signal from GFP plus one term in proportion to F2, put through the curve R that the dilution series already traced at its faint end:

F4 = R(SGFP + c · F2),   R(S) = S / (1 + (S0 / S)n)

Only one number in it is new. S0 = 6.51 counts and n = 5.20 are the shape of R, taken from the dilution series and not refitted. c, the size of the scatter term, was fitted here and comes to 0.0629. It rests on thirteen captures from the two sessions that held no GFP or were judged not induced, and only three of those thirteen read above zero at all. That is a thin fit, so everything below is a check against readings it was not fitted to.

One parameter fitted to three readings of the session it is meant to describe is thin evidence. So the background was measured on its own instead. On 2026-10-11 a non-GFP culture, one with no sfGFP gene to make any green of its own, was diluted and read 21 times, covering F3 from 5 to 89 (Hardware).

Why the numbers are weaker than they look

  • The slope s contains both how much GFP each cell makes and how sensitive the instrument is. Without a fluorescence standard the two cannot be separated.[6]
  • ρ rests on very few OD readings: one in the first session, two in the third.
  • Tubes sharing a cuvette, a time slot or a side of a firmware reset are not fully independent, so every interval and p value on this page is nominal.
  • Three sessions, each with a different recipe design, are a small basis for a model. Every analysis here looked at the same three.
  • The cross-session conversion is a post hoc analysis (3.4). How ρ should be read was chosen after the three sessions had been seen, there are only three of them, and no prediction made in advance has tested it yet. The formal comparison agrees with the figures — a shared slope is not rejected after conversion, p = 0.897, and is rejected before it, p < 0.001 — but for the same reason those p values describe the data and validate nothing.
  • Mixing session 1 supports no lack-of-fit test (3.3). It repeated one recipe, which gives two degrees of freedom of pure error — too few for the test to detect anything — so that column is left blank for it rather than filled with p values that say nothing either way.
  • Neither background curve is a calibration (3.5), and neither may be used to subtract a background from other readings. Both were worked out after the readings were seen. The fitted curve rests on one parameter that only three readings above zero could constrain. The measured curve is one culture on one day, and it covers F3 from 5 to 89 and no further. Neither says whether the background is scattered light or the cells' own fluorescence. A reading that sits on the background means the reader read nothing above it there, not that the plate was not induced.
