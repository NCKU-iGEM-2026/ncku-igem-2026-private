Inhibition model: a molar budget against a signal that regenerates

Module 2 releases single-stranded DNA aptamers that bind the acyl-homoserine lactone (AHL) signals of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The idea is a molecular sponge: hold the signal below the threshold and the quorum-sensing switch never turns on. What the wet lab measured was a partial effect — swarming down by a quarter to two fifths, nothing resolvable in biofilm or pyocyanin.

This model was fitted to two of those numbers and then asked to predict two treatments it had never seen.

{# 四張翻卡＝Part 1 的四個主要結果。和 Part 3 的 Three questions 同一個元件。 #}
At the switch
Binding alone removes only 4 % of the rhl output. The autoinduction loop turns that into the 40 % that was measured — an amplification of about nine. That is why the inhibition is partial, and why it varies.
1.4× more
About 1.4× the aptamer already made should cross 50 % swarming inhibition. This is the most robust number here: it stays between 1.0× and 1.7× across the full literature range of every uncertain parameter.
The C4 arm is not optional
A 3-oxo-C12-HSL-only aptamer saturates below 50 % inhibition of swarming at any dose, in 158 of 169 parameter draws. Its only route to swarming is the las → rhl gate, and that route runs out.
Length, not specificity
Cb-2 loses to A16-2 because it is 93 nt long, not because it binds two ligands. Per molecule the dual binder is the better sponge; per nanogram it supplies 2.1× fewer molecules.

A fifth result came from modelling the measurement rather than the biology. The biofilm assay was being read outside its usable range; correcting for that removed an artefactual result and revealed a real one (1.5, Result 3).

1.1 The question

Three aptamers were tested: ALS-5 (35 nt, binds 3-oxo-C12-HSL), A16-2 (44 nt, binds C4-HSL) and Cb-2 (93 nt, binds both). All three lowered swarming, and none of them lowered it by as much as the sponge picture suggests they should. Three questions followed that no further repeat of the same experiment would answer:

  1. Why partial, and how much more aptamer?
  2. Why does the dual binder lose?
  3. Biology, or measurement?

A sponge is a one-to-one binder, and P. aeruginosa makes AHL continuously under positive feedback. So the question is not only whether the affinity is good enough. It is whether the molar budget can hold down a signal the cell keeps regenerating — and that is a quantitative question, which is why it needed a model rather than another plate.

Equivalent is not the same as inconclusive. Swimming carries a positive claim — the effect is inside a stated margin. Pyocyanin and biofilm carry no claim at all: they were underpowered as run. Resolving a 20 % effect at 80 % power would need 14 and 30 independent plates respectively, and 6 and 9 were available; the smallest effects they could have detected were 28 % and 33 %. We report them as inconclusive, not as negative. Section 1.5 explains why the biofilm case is worse than underpowered — and what is recoverable from it.

1.3 The model

Two signals run the system: L1 = 3-oxo-C12-HSL, which drives the las circuit, and L2 = C4-HSL, which drives rhl. Each is made by its own synthase, each activates its own receptor, and each receptor raises the synthesis of its own signal. las additionally gates rhl. The aptamer sits in the same compartment and takes signal out of it.

Assumptions

One compartment
The swarm plate is treated as a single well-mixed volume shared by the bacteria, the signals and the aptamer. A real swarm is a spreading front with gradients.
Fast binding
Receptor occupancy and aptamer binding are in rapid equilibrium relative to synthesis, so both are solved algebraically at every step.
One site per aptamer
Each aptamer molecule binds one ligand at a time. A dual binder does not get two sites; it gets one site that either ligand can occupy.
Mass to moles
An aptamer applied at mass concentration c supplies c / (n × 330 g/mol) moles, with n the length in nucleotides.
Phenotype
Swarm radius follows rhl receptor occupancy through a power law. Swimming does not depend on quorum sensing at all.
Branch
Steady states are reached by integrating forward in time from a signal-free start, which is what a freshly inoculated plate is.

Equations

Receptor occupancy, with Ki the activation constant:

θi = {{ frac('Li,free', 'Ki + Li,free') }}

Synthesis with autoinduction, and the gate by which las sets the scale of rhl:

dL1/dt = v1 ( e1 + (1 − e1) θ1h ) − d1 L1
dL2/dt = v2 ( e2 + (1 − e2) θ2h ) · g(θ1) − d2 L2
g(θ1) = ( θlas + θ1 ) / ( 1 + θlas ) ei is the leaky fraction made without activation, h the cooperativity of the loop, vi the maximum synthesis rate and di the removal rate.

Sequestration, in rapid equilibrium. Writing Dj for aptamer j's occupancy denominator makes the budget visible:

Dj = 1 + L1f/Kd,j1 + L2f/Kd,j2
Afree,j = Aj / Dj
L1,total = L1f + Σj Aj ( L1f/Kd,j1 ) / Dj
L2,total = L2f + Σj Aj ( L2f/Kd,j2 ) / Dj The shared Dj is the budget constraint: one site, two possible occupants. It is the whole reason a dual binder is not simply two specialists in one molecule.

And the phenotype that is actually measured:

R / Rcontrol = ( θ2 / θ2,control )β

Swimming is quorum-sensing-independent, so the model predicts a ratio of exactly 1 for it with no fitted parameter. That is not a free pass: it is a prediction the data in 1.2 confirm, and it is the anchor that tells us the swarming effect is not general toxicity.

One modelling choice is worth naming. Autoinduction makes the system bistable over part of the parameter range, so there is more than one steady state to choose from. Rather than solve for a steady state directly, the model integrates forward from a signal-free start. That picks the branch a freshly inoculated plate actually samples, and it lets “the switch never turns on” appear as a distinct outcome rather than as a numerical accident.

1.5 Results

Result 1 · The inhibition is partial because the system sits at the switch

At the measured retron yield the sponge is weak in pure binding terms. It moves RhlR's half-activation point by only 11 % (Keff = K2 (1 + Afree/Kd)), which on its own would take about 4 % off the rhl output. The autoinduction loop turns that into the 40 % that was measured — an amplification of about nine.

The same amplification cuts the other way. It puts the system right at the edge of the switch, where a small change in dose produces a large change in outcome — which is the model's explanation for why the measured effects are partial, why they differ so much between aptamers, and why they are not reproducible to the percent.

The 50 % figure is the robust one. Across 169 successfully calibrated parameter draws — spanning three orders of magnitude in the LasR constant and more than an order of magnitude in both AHL levels — the requirement for half suppression never leaves 1.0×–1.7× the yield already achieved. The 90 % figure is not robust, and we do not treat it as a target.

A 3-oxo-C12-HSL-only aptamer cannot do this job. ALS-5 reaches swarming only indirectly, through the las → rhl gate, and that route saturates: raising the ALS-5 dose a thousandfold moves the predicted swarm ratio from 0.75 only as far as 0.73. No dose of it reaches 50 % inhibition, in 158 of 169 parameter draws. For anti-swarming activity, the C4-HSL arm is not optional.

Result 2 · Length, not dual specificity, is what costs Cb-2

The obvious explanation for A16-2 beating Cb-2 is that a dual binder divides its budget between two ligands. The model says that explanation is wrong, and the decomposition says so in two steps.

The parameter that matters most, β, is the phenotype mapping rather than the biochemistry: how a change in rhl output becomes a change in swarm radius. It was fixed at 1 so that two observations could determine two parameters. Measuring it — rhamnolipid quantification alongside swarm radius — is the cheapest single way to tighten every dose prediction on this page.

1.7 What it changed

Six things went back to the wet lab, in the order they can be acted on.

  1. Raise the aptamer yield by about 1.5× and expect to cross 50 % swarming inhibition. The most robust prediction here (90 % range 1.0–1.7×). Full suppression is a different problem: median 10×, reaching 32×.
  2. Use the C4-HSL arm for anti-swarming work. A 3-oxo-C12-HSL-only aptamer saturates below 50 % at any dose in 158 of 169 parameter draws.
  3. Record and compare aptamer dose in mol/L, not ng/µL. Cb-2's entire deficit is a molar-budget artefact of its length — and this is also the one missing number that would turn our failed prediction into a real test.
  4. Re-run biofilm with control wells above OD590 1.1. At OD 0.9 the assay needs 9 wells per group to see a 20 % effect; at OD 2.8 it needs 1.
  5. Pyocyanin needs about 14 independent batches, or a protocol that puts the control OD520 clearly above 0.1.
  6. The experiment that would most improve the model is an A16-2 dose series on swarm plates (0, 0.25×, 1×, 4×, same plate). The model predicts a steep transition between 1× and 2× — a falsifiable shape, not just a number — and it would independently fix β and the A16-2 affinity, currently the two weakest links.

1.8 Assumptions and limits

  • The applied aptamer concentration on a swarm plate was never recorded. We use the one measured ssDNA concentration (7.797 ng/µL) as the nominal dose. This is the dominant uncertainty in everything above, and it is a record-keeping gap rather than a modelling choice.
  • No dose–response data exist, so the A16-2 affinity is inferred from a single effect size rather than measured. Its fitted value (Kd ≈ 5 µM) is weak for an aptamer and should be read as an effective parameter that also absorbs error in the assumed dose.
  • β is assumed, not measured — and it is the most influential parameter in the model.
  • The swarm plate is treated as one well-mixed compartment. A real swarm is a spreading front with gradients in cell density and signal; the model replaces that with a single effective concentration.
  • Whether the aptamer acts inside or outside the cell is not resolved. The model places the sponge in the same compartment as the signal. If sequestration happens in the engineered E. coli cytoplasm, the two AHLs would have to cross a membrane first and would be affected differently. That term is absent here.
  • Cb-2's affinities are assumed equal to the specialists'. No measurement exists. This is the optimistic case, so the predicted length penalty is a lower bound on the real one.
  • θlas = 14 places the system close to the switching threshold. The qualitative consequences — partial inhibition, steep dose–response, amplified response — are robust across the sweep. Individual point predictions near the threshold are not, and are not presented as such.
  • ALS-5-1 appears in the early batches as a construct distinct from ALS-5 and our records do not say what it is. It is reported in the statistics and excluded from the mechanistic calibration.
  • Two outlying plates are retained, not removed. The 2026-09-04 biofilm plate (control OD590 0.036) and the 2026-08-27 pyocyanin plate (A16-2 ratio 2.18) stay in the data, where the precision weighting handles them automatically, rather than being excluded by a rule chosen after seeing them.

Part 2

Imaging model: from photograph to millimetres

2.1 The question

Colony sizes are measured by hand, one colony at a time. The work is slow and it has to be repeated for every plate. This part of the project is a tool built for the wet lab: it takes one photograph of a plate and returns every colony's size in millimetres, with each figure drawn across the colony it belongs to, so a reading can always be checked against what it was taken from.

It is not a model of the biology. What it models is the measurement — how a count of pixels becomes a length in millimetres, what the picture itself has to supply for that conversion to hold, and which of the bright things on a plate are colonies and which are not.

2.2 The model

  1. 1 Find the dish Circle transform on a downscaled copy; if that returns nothing, the largest thresholded shape instead. Gives the scale and the boundary in one step. dish ø ÷ diameter in px → mm/px
  2. 2 Flatten the lighting A heavily blurred copy is taken as the background and subtracted, removing the gradient across the plate.
  3. Which light is this?
    • White Brightness Otsu on the flattened image.
    • UV Colour Green-to-blue ratio: fluorescence is a question about hue, not about being bright.
  4. 3 Keep only what has a core A loose outline and a much stricter core. A region survives only if it holds enough core pixels — a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through.
  5. Convex enough to be one colony?
    • Yes Leave it alone Splitting a clean colony is the worse mistake.
    • No Watershed Seeded from the deepest point of each lobe.
  6. 4 Measure and annotate Longest span, equivalent diameter, area — drawn onto the photograph with a scale bar.
{# 只留流程圖沒帶到的理由，並點名用到的 CV 技術。流程本身不要在這裡重述一次。 #}

The pipeline is OpenCV throughout. A Hough circle transform finds the dish, run on a downscaled copy — the dish is the largest thing in the frame, so the search does not need full resolution — with Otsu thresholding as the fallback. Subtracting a large-σ Gaussian flattens the lighting. White light and UV are then two problems rather than one with a switch: brightness against the agar is a question of intensity, answered by Otsu; fluorescence is a question of hue, answered by the green-to-blue channel ratio, with the choice between them made from the blue-violet fraction of the frame in HSV.

The hard part was never finding bright things. It was telling a colony from a reflection off the agar or the lid, which can be just as bright. Thresholding twice — a loose outline and a stricter core — and keeping only the connected components that hold enough core pixels settles it: a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through. Solidity and aspect ratio remove what is left. Touching colonies are split only where solidity says they need it, by a marker-controlled watershed seeded from the distance transform, because breaking a clean colony apart is the worse error. The figure drawn on the photograph is the Feret diameter.

Each cycle reads the sensor three times: dark, lit, dark. A cycle whose dark read before the flash is not zero on all twelve channels is void, because that is what room light reaching the detector looks like. Each tube is read for three cycles and enters every model as the mean of its valid ones. The cycles are three looks at one tube, not three samples, so the tube is the unit throughout. A reading of zero is a legitimate value and is kept.

Across nine sets of logs from eight days the instrument recorded 657 rows, of which 631 are valid (96.0 %). The other 26 are accounted for: 18 are deliberate gate checks, run with the LED not armed or disabled, and 8 are cycles the firmware voided for a non-zero dark read. No row saturated.

The same record sets the scale of the noise. Within one insertion the three cycles differ by about 0.69 counts (root mean square), so a tube mean carries 0.27 to 0.43 counts of it. In four of seven replicate groups the spread left over between tubes is larger than that: there, seating, sampling, preparation or the cuvette contribute more than the detector's own cycle noise.

3.3 Across sessions, most of the difference is the stock

The three slopes above are 30.93, 5.17 and 18.69, a spread of 6.0×. 3.4 Deciding what to measure, and when to stop

One question the instrument has to answer is whether other cells in the sample change the green reading. The comparison is a pair of tubes with the same GFP culture in them: A is 100 µL of GFP stock with 100 µL of medium, C is 100 µL of GFP stock with 100 µL of non-GFP culture. The quantity of interest is C − A.

Before the session, a simulation with the noise we had seen so far settled how many pairs to make. After the session, the same calculation was redone with the noise the session actually showed.

The six pairs gave a mean C − A of −0.39 counts, with a nominal 95 % interval from −1.88 to +1.10, which is −20.1 % to +11.8 % of A. The session did not resolve a difference. That is not the same as showing there is none: the honest statement is that if non-GFP culture shifts the reading, this session places the shift somewhere in that range.

At the observed noise a true difference would have to be about 21.8 % of A before six more pairs had an 80 % chance of resolving it, and resolving a 10 % difference would take 21 pairs. On those numbers the analysis recommended against repeating the comparison and for spending the remaining bench time elsewhere.

Lessons for the next design 3 notes Show Hide
More tubes
Because tube-to-tube spread exceeds cycle noise, precision comes from more tubes or more pairs, not from more cycles per tube.
Confound
The cuvettes were washed and reused, so which use of a cuvette it was, and whether A or C was read first, coincide exactly in that session. One cuvette per tube, or a cross-tabulation of the factors at the design stage, would have kept them apart.
Attribution
C receives culture, cells together with their spent medium, where A receives fresh medium. C − A is the difference between two recipes and cannot be assigned to the cells alone.

3.6 After the rebuild: what carried over

The models above describe the first reader. It was rebuilt on 2026-10-03, and two sessions on the rebuilt reader, a dilution series and a session on AHL plates, show what carries over. Both are on the Hardware page, and everything in this section was worked out after their readings were seen.

The inverse models did not carry over

The three models that need no OD, F4 alone, the four-channel ridge and a random forest, were applied unchanged to the twelve tubes of the dilution series, whose share of GFP culture is known. None did better than always guessing the mean of the training tubes: the best was off by 27.1 points on average against 27.8, and that only after the counts were scaled down for the higher LED current. The stock of that session gave more signal for the same share of culture than any stock the models had seen. G, a share of one day's stock, is not a scale that carries from one stock to the next.

A background that rises with scatter

The plate suspensions were far denser than any tube in 3.1, and in them the emission channel was no longer zero without GFP. We described that with the curve R that follows the dilution series down at its faint end, and one new parameter: a background in proportion to the scatter channel F2.

F4 = R(SGFP + c · F2),   R(S) = S / (1 + (S0 / S)n)

S0 = 6.51 counts and n = 5.20 come from the dilution series and were not refitted. c = 0.0629 was fitted to thirteen captures from the two sessions that held no GFP or were judged not induced, of which three read above zero.

3.8 How the numbers were produced

The tables behind sections 3.2 to 3.4 were not typed in by hand. Each is generated by a script that reads the instrument's raw serial logs and the bench record, and is then recomputed by a second script, written separately, that compares the two cell by cell. The least-squares fits are solved in exact rational arithmetic and rounded only for display. Every tube mean the models use is listed in those tables, including the readings of zero and the tubes that were re-prepared. The four-channel study keeps a record of every model it fitted and refits its held-out predictions as a check. The three figures in 3.2 and 3.3 are drawn from the twenty tube means themselves, with their slopes and held-out errors computed in the page. The numbers in 3.6 are computed by scripts from the raw logs of the two later sessions.

The scripts and the raw logs are held by the team and are not published. The instrument that produced the readings is described on the Hardware page, and the software that turns a reading into a concentration on the Software page.
