Inhibition releases single-stranded DNA aptamers that bind the acyl-homoserine lactone (AHL) signals of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The idea is a molecular sponge: hold the signal below the threshold and the quorum-sensing switch never turns on. What the wet lab measured was a partial effect — swarming, pyocyanin and biofilm all down, by a tenth to a third. This model was fitted to two of those numbers and then asked to predict a treatment and two phenotypes it had never seen. It misses them, in the same direction every time, and that failure is the most informative thing on this page.

At the switch
Binding alone removes only 4 % of the rhl output. The autoinduction loop turns that into the 36 % that was measured — an amplification of about nine. That is why the inhibition is partial, and why it varies.
1.6× more
About 1.6× the aptamer already made should cross 50 % swarming inhibition. This is the most robust number here: 1.0× to 2.6× across every source of uncertainty at once. Full suppression is not a target — the same sweep puts it anywhere up to 49×.
Length, not specificity
Cb-2 is predicted to lose to A16-2 because it is 93 nt long, not because it binds two ligands. Per molecule the dual binder is the better sponge; per nanogram it supplies 2.1× fewer molecules.
It fails its own test
Cb-2 was held out of every fit and the model misses it in all three assays, the same way each time and by the most in biofilm. Three readings survive and this design cannot separate them. The experiment that would is the first thing 1.8 asks for.

1.1 The question

Three aptamers were tested: ALS-5 (35 nt, binds 3-oxo-C12-HSL), A16-2 (44 nt, binds C4-HSL) and Cb-2 (93 nt, binds both).[1] All three lowered swarming and pyocyanin, and none of them lowered either by as much as the sponge picture suggests they should. Four questions followed that no further repeat of the same experiment would answer:

  1. Why partial, and how much more aptamer?
  2. Why does the dual binder lose?
  3. One suppression, three phenotypes?
  4. Which unknown is in the way?

A sponge is a one-to-one binder, and P. aeruginosa makes AHL continuously under positive feedback. So the question is not only whether the affinity is good enough. It is whether the molar budget can hold down a signal the cell keeps regenerating — and that is a quantitative question, which is why it needed a model rather than another plate.

1.2 What the data show

Every treated well is normalised to a control on the same plate, so the response is a ratio and is analysed on the log scale, where ratios are symmetric and additive. What counts as one experiment differs between the assays. For swarming and pyocyanin the plate is the unit and plates enter as a random intercept, so repeated readings on one plate are not counted as independent experiments. For biofilm every row is a separately grown biological replicate and the replicates are the units. That unit is printed beside every row, because it is what sets how much the interval beside it is worth.

A plate is recovered from its control reading. The workbook records no dates and no plate numbers. Readings that are equal are taken to be the same plate. That is what re-pairs ALS-5 and A16-2 across nine swarming plates, which is what lets the key comparison below be made within plates at all; it also merges two pyocyanin rows that both read 0.102, costing that assay a plate.

All three aptamers inhibit swarming and pyocyanin. All six of those intervals exclude no effect (p between 2 × 10−6 and 0.019), and the effects agree in direction and in size across two phenotypes measured in different ways.

Biofilm resolves for Cb-2 and for A16-2, and not for ALS-5. Cb-2 removes 36.7 % of the biofilm [28.8, 43.7] on twelve replicates (p = 3 × 10−6) and A16-2 12.6 % [2.7, 21.5] on six (p = 0.023); ALS-5's 11.5 % does not clear the line (p = 0.057). Those three readings agree with the wet lab's own summary on the Results page, which reports the same assay as reductions of 34.2 % for Cb-2, 12.2 % for A16-2 and 11.0 % for ALS-5, and likewise leaves ALS-5 just short at p = 0.053. The two analyses are compared treatment by treatment at the end of this section. Cb-2's row carries a condition the other two do not: its two plates disagree with each other, which is the caution below.

Equivalence, and what the assays could have detected

Failing to reject “no effect” is not the same as showing there is none, so every treatment was tested the other way round as well, against a 0.80–1.25 equivalence margin. The margin is symmetric on the log scale, where the analysis is done; in ratio terms that reads as −20 % / +25 % and must not be quoted as ±20 %.

Of the nine rows, three are larger than the margin allows — swarming A16-2, pyocyanin Cb-2 and biofilm Cb-2 — four are inconclusive, and two come back equivalent to no effect, both in biofilm: ALS-5 (p = 0.049) and A16-2 (p = 0.043). A16-2's is the awkward one: the same six replicates give p = 0.023 against no effect and p = 0.043 against the margin. Read together those say the effect is resolvable and small — a real difference from the control that a 20 % criterion would not call biologically interesting.

Both verdicts sit on the line, and one number says how close. Every biofilm well is divided by the mean of its block's control wells, so one number divides all of them and its own sampling error cancels out of the spread of the ratios. Carry that error through and both verdicts turn over — ALS-5 to 0.055, A16-2 to 0.050, neither equivalent any more. Until 2026-10-15 this analysis read a biofilm row as one well of a shared 96-well plate — a technical replicate, with the plate as the experimental unit — which is how the Engineering page describes the method, and it was reported here as the reading that claims less. There is no quorum-sensing-independent control

All three phenotypes measured here are quorum-sensing-dependent, and all three also depend on growth. Showing that a QS-dependent phenotype is inhibited is therefore not the same as showing that the mechanism is quorum sensing: a treatment that simply slowed the bacteria down would reduce swarming, pyocyanin and biofilm together, exactly as observed.

Separating the two needs a phenotype that quorum sensing does not control — a flagellar motility assay is the usual choice — inhibited in the one case and untouched in the other. No such phenotype was measured, so nothing here excludes a growth effect. This is the single largest limitation of the analysis, the model cannot argue it away, and it is the first recommendation in 1.8.

1.3 The model

Two signals run the system: L1 = 3-oxo-C12-HSL, which drives the Las circuit, and L2 = C4-HSL, which drives rhl. Each is made by its own synthase, each activates its own receptor, and each receptor raises the synthesis of its own signal. Las additionally gates rhl. The aptamer sits in the same compartment and takes signal out of it.

Assumptions

One compartment
The swarm plate is treated as a single well-mixed volume shared by the bacteria, the signals and the aptamer. A real swarm is a spreading front with gradients. 1.6 runs the whole analysis again over a plate of patches and reports what this assumption costs.
Fast binding
Receptor occupancy and aptamer binding are in rapid equilibrium relative to synthesis, so both are solved algebraically at every step.
One site per aptamer
Each aptamer molecule binds one ligand at a time. A dual binder does not get two sites; it gets one site that either ligand can occupy.
Mass to moles
An aptamer applied at mass concentration c supplies c / (n × 330 g/mol) moles, with n the length in nucleotides. The 93-nt Cb-2 therefore delivers 2.1× fewer molecules per nanogram than the 44-nt A16-2. This is arithmetic, not a fitted result.
Phenotype
Swarm radius follows rhl receptor occupancy through a power law. The model is calibrated on swarming alone.
Branch
Steady states are reached by integrating forward in time from a signal-free start, which is what a freshly inoculated plate is.

Equations

Receptor occupancy, with Ki the activation constant:

θi = {{ frac('Li,free', 'Ki + Li,free') }}

Synthesis with autoinduction, and the gate by which Las sets the scale of rhl:

dL1/dt = v1 ( e1 + (1 − e1) θ1h ) − d1 L1
dL2/dt = v2 ( e2 + (1 − e2) θ2h ) · g(θ1) − d2 L2
g(θ1) = ( θlas + θ1 ) / ( 1 + θlas ) ei is the leaky fraction made without activation, h the cooperativity of the loop, vi the maximum synthesis rate and di the removal rate.

Sequestration, in rapid equilibrium. Writing Dj for aptamer j's occupancy denominator makes the budget visible:

Dj = 1 + L1f/Kd,j1 + L2f/Kd,j2
Afree,j = Aj / Dj
L1,total = L1f + Σj Aj ( L1f/Kd,j1 ) / Dj
L2,total = L2f + Σj Aj ( L2f/Kd,j2 ) / Dj The shared Dj is the budget constraint: one site, two possible occupants. It is the whole reason a dual binder is not simply two specialists in one molecule, and it is the argument of 1.5, Result 3.

And the phenotype that is actually measured:

R / Rcontrol = ( θ2 / θ2,control )β

The model is calibrated on swarming only. It is the one phenotype with a documented mechanistic route from rhl output to the thing that gets measured. Pyocyanin and biofilm are regulated by las, rhl and pqs together, and mapping them directly would mean inventing parameters that no measurement here could fix. 1.6 tests them a different way instead.

The model contains no mechanism by which an aptamer could alter a phenotype that quorum sensing does not control, so for such a phenotype it would predict a ratio of exactly 1 with no fitted parameter. That is a structural property of the model, not a result — nothing here measures such a phenotype, so it is untested, which is the gap 1.2 describes.

One numerical choice is worth naming. Autoinduction makes the system bistable over part of the parameter range, so there is more than one steady state to choose from. Rather than solve for a steady state directly, the model integrates forward from a signal-free start, which picks the branch a freshly inoculated plate actually samples. The integration horizon is not fixed but extended until a drift criterion is met: a sponge buffers the signal and multiplies the relaxation time by roughly (1 + A/Kd), which at a large dose is far beyond any horizon chosen in advance.

1.5 What the model says

Result 1 · The inhibition is partial because the system sits at the switch

At the nominal yield the sponge is weak in pure binding terms. It moves RhlR's half-activation point by only about 9 % (Keff = K2 (1 + Afree/Kd)), which on its own would take about 4 % off the rhl output. The autoinduction loop turns that into the 36 % that was measured — an amplification of about nine.

The same amplification cuts the other way. It puts the system right at the edge of the switch, where a small change in dose produces a large change in outcome — which is the model's explanation both for why the measured effects are partial and for why they are not reproducible to the percent.

Result 2 · A C12-only aptamer is predicted not to do this job

ALS-5 reaches swarming only indirectly, through the Las → rhl gate, and in the model that route saturates: no dose of a 3-oxo-C12-HSL-only aptamer reaches 50 % inhibition of swarming, in 269 of 296 calibrated draws (91 %). That is the flat blue curve in the figure above.

This is a prediction, not a result. The data do not have the resolution to test it: it would be tested by a dose series, and what exists is one dose. The swarming difference between ALS-5 and A16-2 is consistent with it — eight of their nine shared plates run that way (1.2), though the size of the difference is not resolved within plates — but consistency at a single dose does not distinguish “ALS-5 saturates” from “ALS-5 is simply weaker here”. 1.8 says what would settle it.

This one reverses the obvious guess. A dual binder divides one site between two ligands, so dual specificity looks like the thing that should cost it. Decomposing the A16-2 → Cb-2 gap says otherwise, in two steps that point in opposite directions.

Held out again: the other two phenotypes

The model is calibrated on swarming. Pyocyanin and biofilm are not independent of it — all three are supposed to be downstream of the same quorum-sensing suppression — so one set of receptor occupancies ought to account for all three at once. That is a test the model can fail. Each phenotype is written as a power law in the two occupancies:

P / Pcontrol = ( θ1 / θ1,0 )w1 · ( θ2 / θ2,0 )w2

ALS-5 and A16-2 determine the two exponents exactly. A16-2 binds C4-HSL only and las is upstream of rhl, so it leaves θ1 untouched and isolates w2; ALS-5 then gives w1. Cb-2 is left out of all three fits and becomes a held-out prediction in each assay.

None of the three phenotypes needs the las arm. Every w1 interval includes zero. The ordering reversal in 1.2 — A16-2 strongest in swarming, Cb-2 strongest in pyocyanin — looked as though it might mean pyocyanin runs partly through las, which would have made the dual binder's second arm worth something. It does not. This is a negative result and it is reported as one.

Swarming and pyocyanin read the same suppression differently. Swarming is linear in rhl occupancy (w2 = 0.99) and pyocyanin is close to its square root (0.50), and the two intervals do not overlap. Halving the rhl output halves the swarm radius but reduces pyocyanin by only about 30 %. Biofilm is shallower still at 0.30 [0.15, 0.46] — overlapping pyocyanin, well clear of swarming, and resolved away from zero, where under the plate reading of 1.2 the same exponent had spanned almost everything. That makes it a test rather than a formality, and below it is the one the model misses by the most. β was fixed at 1 by assumption in 1.3, and this analysis recovers 0.99 without being told to — not independent evidence, since the same swarming data fix both, but the assumption is at least internally consistent.

The reason the three react so differently to a change in dose is the exponents above. Three readings are left and this design cannot separate them: Cb-2's affinities differ from the specialists'; the model's switch is too abrupt, or more generally its one-compartment structure is wrong; or Cb-2's estimates are displaced by the plates they sit on — five swarming plates it shares with nothing else, and ten of twelve biofilm wells on a single-treatment plate (1.2). The next part takes the second of those apart; the experiment that bears on all three is in 1.8.

One compartment, or a plate of patches?

Everything above rests on a jump. The model treats a swarm plate as a single well-mixed volume, autoinduction makes that volume bistable, and the whole of it crosses the switch at once — which is why no dose lands on the measured 0.820. No real plate can be discontinuous in dose. A swarm is a spreading front, so a plate reading is an aggregate over patches that do not all switch together, and the jump may belong to the assumption rather than to the biology. Nothing in the repository said which, so the whole analysis is run again over a plate of patches: a lognormal spread of local dose, averaged over 32 equal-probability strata, with both free parameters re-fitted at every spread and Cb-2 still held out of all three phenotypes.

The swarming miss is structural, and not a matter of tuning. Every spread from 0.2 to 0.7 puts the held-out swarming prediction inside the measured interval while the spread itself moves over a factor of three and a half; the log residual runs from −0.075 to +0.045 and crosses zero near 0.5. A prediction that landed on the measurement at one spread and nowhere else would be a fitted parameter wearing a structural hat, and this is not that. So the reachability verdict above turns over with it: under one compartment no dose reproduces 0.820; over patches it is reproduced at the nominal dose.

The other two misses are not structural. Pyocyanin reaches 0.857 at the widest spread against a measured 0.716, and biofilm 0.908 against 0.633; both sit outside at every spread, and the residuals shrink by about a third without closing. Their sub-linear exponents had already compressed the jump before any averaging, so smoothing it buys them almost nothing. The model still has no held-out test that it passes in all three phenotypes.

What does not move is worth as much as what does. The fit is nearly indifferent to the structure — θlas goes from 14.8 to 13.6 and the A16-2 affinity stays inside 1.5 % — and so does the headline dose claim: the dose for 50 % swarming inhibition is 1.55× the nominal yield under one compartment and 1.65× at a spread of 0.5, and for 90 % it is 25.8× against 27.1×. Result 1 does not depend on the structure.

What this does not license. Nothing here measures plate heterogeneity, so the spread is an assumption swept over a plausible range, not a parameter with a value. The swarming prediction landing inside its interval under that assumption says what the structure can accommodate; it is not a held-out test the model passed, and the failed test above is not withdrawn. The spread also acts only on the dose, which leaves the untreated reference alone; a spread in cell density would move the control too, and this design cannot tell those apart. Every number published elsewhere in this part is the one-compartment model.

The parameters dominate every output. Re-measuring the swarming effects would barely narrow anything: the data account for about a quarter of the variance in the 50 % dose and essentially none of it elsewhere.

The dose splits the outputs in two. It is almost irrelevant to the two fold requirements, because those are expressed as a fold increase over whatever dose a draw assumed, so the assumption largely cancels. It is the second contributor to both absolute quantities — the held-out Cb-2 ratio and the fitted A16-2 affinity — which is why 1.9 calls that fitted Kd an effective parameter rather than a measurement of the aptamer.

The measured ALS-5 effect puts a floor under the applied dose. 66 of the 200 dose-only draws do not calibrate at all, and every one of them lies below 3.1 ng/µL: with the parameters at their nominal values, no dose that low produces an ALS-5 swarming effect as large as the one measured, at any gating strength. That is a bound the experiment could not give, and it comes from the measurement rather than from the model.

One qualification applies to every number in this section. The quantiles and the correlations below are computed on the draws that calibrated, and whether a draw calibrates is not independent of the dose it assumed: 47 % of the draws in the lowest quarter of the dose range calibrate against 98 % in the highest, by the same mechanism as the floor above. The calibrated draws do still span 1.0 to 30.0 ng/µL, so this is a weighting rather than a truncation, and it runs in the direction that would understate a low-dose effect. Nothing here corrects for it; the dose and the calibration flag are both columns of uncertainty_samples.csv, which is what makes the weighting checkable rather than implicit.

1.8 What it changed

Seven things went back to the wet lab, in the order they can be acted on. None of them had been carried out when this page was written; where one is completed before the wiki freeze, this list should be rewritten to say what happened.

  1. Measure a phenotype that quorum sensing does not control, on the same plates as swarming. A flagellar motility assay is the usual choice. This is the single highest-value measurement missing here: without it nothing in this part excludes the possibility that the aptamers simply slow the bacteria down, and every conclusion inherits that caveat. One plate set carrying both settles it.
  2. Raise the aptamer yield by about 1.6×, and expect to cross 50 % swarming inhibition. The most robust dose prediction here (90 % range 1.0–2.6× across all three sources of uncertainty). Full suppression is a different matter: median 11×, reaching 49×.
  3. Measure β: rhamnolipid quantification alongside swarm radius. It is the most influential parameter in the model (PRCC −0.54) and it is currently assumed, not measured. 1.7 shows that the parameters, not the measurements, are what hold the predictions back, and this is the parameter at the top of that list.
  4. Run a dose series of A16-2 on swarm plates (0, 0.25×, 1×, 4× the current yield, same plate). The model predicts a steep transition between 1× and 2× — a falsifiable shape, not just a number — and it is what would test the claim in Result 2 that a C12-only aptamer saturates.
  5. Record and report the applied aptamer concentration in mol/L, not ng/µL, and compare constructs per mole. Cb-2's predicted deficit against A16-2 is a molar-budget effect of being 93 nt long; per molecule it is the better sponge. This is also the one missing number that would turn the failed Cb-2 prediction into a real test. All the model can currently say is that the dose was above about 3 ng/µL — and a bound is not a record.
  6. Put Cb-2 on the same plates as the other two. Every Cb-2 comparison in swarming is currently across different plates, which costs more statistical power than any extra replicate would buy back — and 1.6 leaves a residual that is either a real difference in Cb-2's affinities or an artefact of exactly that isolation. One shared-plate run separates them.
  7. Biofilm needs its treatments on shared plates, not more replicates. The assay has 24 independent replicates and resolves two of its three treatments against their controls, so replication is not what it is short of. What it cannot do is compare the treatments with each other: ten of Cb-2's twelve replicates sit on a plate carrying nothing else, so both significant biofilm folds are across-plate comparisons, and on bf-P2 — the one plate carrying all three — neither reproduces. That plate also disagrees with bf-P1 about Cb-2 itself (p = 0.002). One run with all three treatments replicated on every plate fixes both, and it is the same recommendation as 6 for the same reason. Source: plate_split.csv, contrasts.csv.

1.9 Assumptions and limits

  • No phenotype outside quorum-sensing control was measured, so a growth effect cannot be excluded. This is the largest limitation here; 1.2 states it in full and 1.8 opens with it.
  • The applied aptamer concentration on a plate was never recorded. The model uses the one measured ssDNA concentration (7.797 ng/µL) as the nominal dose and sweeps it over 1–30 ng/µL. Under equal-mass dosing the Cb-2 prediction fails; under equal-molar dosing it fails in the other direction. For the two synthesised aptamers this is a record-keeping gap; for Cb-2, a crude extract, the quantity was never measured at all.
  • No dose–response data exist, so the A16-2 affinity is inferred from a single effect size rather than measured. Its fitted value (Kd ≈ 5.6 µM) is weak for an aptamer and should be read as an effective parameter that also absorbs any error in the assumed dose.
  • β is assumed, not measured, and is the most influential parameter in the model. It is fixed at 1 purely so that the number of free parameters equals the number of calibration points.
  • Cb-2's affinities are assumed equal to the specialists'. No measurement exists. This is the optimistic case, so the predicted length penalty is a lower bound on the real one.
  • Every Cb-2 comparison in swarming is unpaired. Cb-2 shares no plate with ALS-5 or A16-2, so those contrasts carry plate-to-plate variation that the paired contrasts do not.
  • Biofilm rests on the wet lab's statement that its wells are independent biological replicates (2026-10-15). Read as plates instead, the assay has two units per treatment and resolves nothing (p = 0.13–0.45); every biofilm number in this part would change, and only the biofilm numbers. The reading is recorded in the code with its source so that it can be checked rather than inferred from the output.
  • Biofilm Cb-2's two plates disagree, and its interval is computed as if they did not. bf-P1 gives 40.6 % inhibition on ten wells and bf-P2 13.3 % on two (p = 0.002), after each well was divided by the control on its own plate. The reported 36.7 % [28.8, 43.7] treats the twelve wells as twelve independent experiments; the plate-level standard error of the same rows is 3.5× wider. No other treatment in the data set shows this (1.2).
  • The two biofilm treatments that differ significantly from each other are not plate-controlled, for the same reason, and more generally no contrast in this part survives holding the plate fixed: five of the nine are significant pooled and none of the five is at p < 0.05 when the comparison is restricted to the plates both treatments were on. The honest summary of the aptamer comparisons is that the swarming ordering is consistent in direction and not resolved in size.
  • The control mean a biofilm row divides by carries error that no interval here contains. Every well of a block is normalised to the mean of that block's control wells, so one number divides all of them and its own sampling error cancels out of the spread of the ratios. That is the convention, and it is what the wet lab's one-sample tests do, but it is an approximation: carrying it through widens Cb-2 from [28.8, 43.7] to [25.4, 46.3] and turns over the two equivalence verdicts in 1.2. No verdict against no effect moves. The sensitivity is reported beside the headline columns and feeds nothing downstream.
  • Nineteen of the workbook's own 68 stated percentages are divided by the wrong control, which is why every ratio here is recomputed from the raw readings. Re-pairing each swarming Cb-2 well with the control on its own plate is the difference between the 18.0 % reported here and the 19.3 % the workbook's column gives; the raw millimetres are identical either way (1.2).
  • The two calibration targets are resampled independently. ALS-5 and A16-2 sit on nine of the same plates, so their errors are really positively correlated; pairing independent draws makes the difference between them — which is what pins the fit — more variable than it is. The data uncertainty reported here is therefore an upper bound, and 1.7 shows it is not the dominant source in any case.
  • The reconciling dose in 1.6 sits on a discontinuity. The least-squares optimum lands at 8.6932 ng/µL, the first dose past the switch; at 8.6930 the model predicts 0.991 for swarming instead of 0.662. The fold is the edge of a jump, not an interior optimum, and should not be quoted to more than the two decimals given. Over a plate of patches the gap is not there and the optimum is interior, which is the last part of 1.6.
  • The workbook records no dates, so a plate is identified by its control reading. That recovers which treatments were measured side by side, which is what the paired contrasts rest on, but it cannot tell whether two plates were run on the same day. Two pyocyanin rows quote the same control reading and are read as one plate rather than two.
  • Whether a numbered row is a plate or a replicate is not something the sheet states, and it decides what counts as n. The workbook's own percentages fix how the ratios were formed but say nothing about how the cultures were grown. That came from the wet lab, and until 2026-10-15 this analysis had assumed the other answer (1.2).
  • Two Cb-2 biofilm percentages have no raw reading anywhere in the workbook and are excluded by a traceability rule fixed before they were looked at. They sit above the values that can be traced, so excluding them makes the Cb-2 biofilm result larger, not smaller; in the event it moves the well-level mean only from 64.3 % to 65.6 % of control. It is also the whole of the n = 14 against n = 12 difference from the wet lab's own test (1.2).
  • The swarm plate is treated as one well-mixed compartment. A real swarm is a spreading front with gradients in cell density and signal; the model replaces that with a single effective concentration. 1.6 measures what this costs: averaging over patches of one plate accounts for the failed swarming prediction and for nothing else, leaves the fit and the dose requirement where they were, and turns “no dose reaches it” into a statement that holds for one compartment only. The spread it sweeps is not measured, so the alternative structure is a sensitivity and not a correction.
  • Whether the aptamer acts inside or outside the cell is not resolved. The model places the sponge in the same compartment as the signal. If sequestration happens in the engineered E. coli cytoplasm, the two AHLs would have to cross a membrane first and would be affected differently. That term is absent here.
  • 104 of 400 joint draws failed to calibrate, almost all of them (102) where a wide parameter draw makes the observed ALS-5 effect unreachable at any gating strength; the other two left the fit with more than one root. The single-source modes fail the same way: 30 of 200 for the parameters and 66 of 200 for the dose, the latter all below 3.1 ng/µL. Those draws carry no prediction and are excluded from every quantile above, which is the weighting 1.7 qualifies.
  • Everything here comes from one workbook. A self-check enforces it: source_file in the tidy tables has a single value, because a model assembled from several sources cannot be reproduced from what the repository ships.

Part 2

Imaging model: from photograph to millimetres

2.1 The question

Colony sizes are measured by hand, one colony at a time. The work is slow and it has to be repeated for every plate. This part of the project is a tool built for the wet lab: it takes one photograph of a plate and returns every colony's size in millimetres, with each figure drawn across the colony it belongs to, so a reading can always be checked against what it was taken from.

It is not a model of the biology. What it models is the measurement — how a count of pixels becomes a length in millimetres, what the picture itself has to supply for that conversion to hold, and which of the bright things on a plate are colonies and which are not.

2.2 The model

  1. 1 Find the dish Circle transform on a downscaled copy; if that returns nothing, the largest thresholded shape instead. Gives the scale and the boundary in one step. dish ø ÷ diameter in px → mm/px
  2. 2 Flatten the lighting A heavily blurred copy is taken as the background and subtracted, removing the gradient across the plate.
  3. Which light is this?
    • White Brightness Otsu on the flattened image.
    • UV Colour Green-to-blue ratio: fluorescence is a question about hue, not about being bright.
  4. 3 Keep only what has a core A loose outline and a much stricter core. A region survives only if it holds enough core pixels — a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through.
  5. Convex enough to be one colony?
    • Yes Leave it alone Kept whole and counted as one colony.
    • No Watershed Split apart, and each piece counted as its own colony.
  6. 4 Measure and annotate Longest span, equivalent diameter, area — drawn onto the photograph with a scale bar.
{# 只留流程圖沒帶到的理由，並點名用到的 CV 技術。流程本身不要在這裡重述一次。 #}

The pipeline is OpenCV throughout. A Hough circle transform[2] finds the dish, run on a downscaled copy — the dish is the largest thing in the frame, so the search does not need full resolution — with Otsu thresholding[3] as the fallback. Subtracting a large-σ Gaussian flattens the lighting. White light and UV are then two problems rather than one with a switch: brightness against the agar is a question of intensity, answered by Otsu; fluorescence is a question of hue, answered by the green-to-blue channel ratio, with the choice between them made from the blue-violet fraction of the frame in HSV.

The difficult part was never finding bright things. It was telling a colony from a reflection off the agar or the lid, which can be just as bright. Thresholding twice — a loose outline and a stricter core — and keeping only the connected components that hold enough core pixels settles it: a reflection is bright but thin, a colony is bright all the way through. Solidity and aspect ratio remove what is left. Touching colonies are split only where solidity says they need it, by a marker-controlled watershed seeded from the distance transform, because breaking a clean colony apart is the worse error. The figure drawn on the photograph is the Feret diameter.

Four thresholds worth explaining
ThresholdWhat it decides
A loose outline at 1.1× Otsu, a core at Otsu + 0.6 of the way to white, and 30 core pixels Whether a bright patch is a colony at all. The gap between the two thresholds is the whole reflection test: a reflection clears the loose one and fails the core one, because it is bright without being bright all the way through. Thirty pixels is what stops a few stray bright pixels from passing as a centre.
Solidity 0.80 Whether a region is left alone or sent to the watershed. Everything above it is taken as one colony on sight. The line sits high deliberately: splitting a clean colony invents a result, while failing to split two that touch only merges a real one, so the benefit of the doubt goes to not cutting.

2.6 What it changed

{# 三點都是團隊給的。第 1、3 點描述的是程式實際的行為與架構（標註輸出、 無學習模型），第 2 點指回 2.5 的數字而不另外下形容詞。 「免費方案的主機」是團隊告知的部署方式——站內其他頁目前沒有寫到部署環境， 之後 Software 頁若補上部署說明，這裡要跟它一致。 #}

Three things this changed about how the wet lab measures a plate.

  1. A plate is read in one pass, and the reading comes back drawn on the photograph. Every colony on the plate is measured together rather than one at a time, and the output is the picture itself with each span, its figure and a scale bar marked on it. The number never has to be copied from an instrument into a notebook, and any reading can be checked against the colony it came from without going back to the plate.
  2. The reading is good enough to use. Against hand measurement it agrees to within 0.71 mm on average, every colony inside 2 mm, with no bias the data can separate from zero (2.5). That is finer than the differences the assays are built to compare.
  3. It is small enough to run anywhere. The pipeline is classical computer vision — thresholds, morphology, a watershed, a convex hull — so there is no learned model, no weights to load and no inference step. It needs no GPU and runs on a free hosting tier, which is what let it go to the people doing the measuring rather than staying on one machine in the lab.

2.7 Limits

{# 兩點都是團隊給的。第 2 點末尾那句「同一盤內的比較不受影響」是從 「比例尺是全盤共用的一個係數」推出來的：係數錯了，盤上每顆都錯同樣的 倍率，所以盤內的相對比較不變。這是推論，不是量測結果，寫法上沒有 當成已驗證的結論。 #}
  • A strong enough reflection can still be read as a colony. The two-threshold test rejects the glare we have photographed — a reflection is bright without being bright all the way through, so it clears the loose outline and fails the core (2.4 shows one being dropped). What it cannot reject is a reflection that is both bright and even enough to have a dense centre of its own, which a fluorescent tube close to the plate, or a lid left on, can produce. The plates here were photographed without a lid and away from direct overhead light; that is a handling rule rather than something the program enforces.
  • The plate has to be photographed square-on. Every millimetre on the page comes from one number: the dish diameter the operator enters, divided by the dish diameter the program measures in pixels. That division assumes the dish appears as a circle. Photographed off-axis it projects as an ellipse, the circle fit lands somewhere between its long and short axis, and the scale is wrong by however much the tilt was. The error is systematic rather than random — one scale factor is shared by the whole plate, so every colony on it is out by the same proportion. A comparison made within a single plate, which is how the assays read, should therefore survive a small tilt even though the absolute millimetres would not; we have not measured how large a tilt that holds for.

Reader model: lessons from the first prototype

3.0 The question we met

CAPTURE-Screen was built for our AHL biosensor and rehearsed on GFP cultures: we expected an AHL-induced culture to be about as bright as a GFP one (Hardware). Everything in this part is that rehearsal, and it was run on version 1 of the reader, the build that was later replaced (Hardware). What it describes is that instrument's measurement behaviour — how a count relates to the culture in the cuvette — not the biosensor's response to AHL, which we have not measured.

Within that scope, the reader model had two questions put to it.

Is the reading proportional? Within one session, where the signal is clear of the floor, it is: double the GFP culture and the reading becomes 2.04×.

Why did the same recipe read six times higher on one day than another? Because a volume fraction is not an amount — a volume of thin culture holds less GFP than the same volume of dense culture. Dividing by the density of that day's stock brings the three slopes to within 8 % of each other.

3.5 After the rebuild: what carried over

The models above describe the first reader. Its noise did not come mostly from the detector. In four of seven replicate groups, seating and preparation outweighed the cycle noise (3.1). That was one consideration in the rebuild that followed, alongside the parts that had broken or made loading slow.

The reader was rebuilt on 2026-10-03: five printed parts redrawn around a one-step cuvette seat, and one resistor to bring the LED to its design current. Putting the same tube back now repeats to 0.76 %, against 3.6 to 6.3 % before (Hardware).

Three sessions on the rebuilt reader — a dilution series, a session on AHL plates and one on how dense a culture can be — show what carries over. All three are on the Hardware page, and everything in this section was worked out after their readings were seen.

The inverse models did not carry over

Three models were also fitted on the first reader to read G back from the sensor's channels, none of them needing the stock OD: F4 alone, a ridge regression on four channels, and a random forest. All three were applied unchanged to the twelve tubes of the dilution series, whose share of GFP culture is known. None did better than always guessing the mean of the training tubes: the best was off by 27.1 points on average against 27.8, and that only after the counts were scaled down for the higher LED current. The stock of that session gave more signal for the same share of culture than any stock the models had seen. G, a share of one day's stock, is not a scale that carries from one stock to the next.

A background that rises with scatter

Every tube in 3.1 that held no GFP read exactly zero on the green channel. The plate suspensions were far denser than any of those tubes, and in them the green channel no longer read zero without GFP. That has to be described before any reading from those plates can be used.

What the extra reading follows is scatter. The denser the suspension, the higher the scatter channel F2, and the higher the green channel reads. So the description is the signal from GFP plus one term in proportion to F2, put through the curve R that the dilution series already traced at its faint end:

F4 = R(SGFP + c · F2),   R(S) = S / (1 + (S0 / S)n)

Only one number in it is new. S0 = 6.51 counts and n = 5.20 are the shape of R, taken from the dilution series and not refitted. c, the size of the scatter term, was fitted here and comes to 0.0629. It rests on thirteen captures from the two sessions that held no GFP or were judged not induced, and only three of those thirteen read above zero at all. That is a thin fit, so everything below is a check against readings it was not fitted to.

Those 21 points are the background itself, measured rather than modelled. Joined into a curve — the only assumption being that it never falls as scatter rises — they give the background at any scatter in that range, and the AHL session's readings can be set against them. Why the numbers are weaker than they look

  • The slope s contains both how much GFP each cell makes and how sensitive the instrument is. Without a fluorescence standard the two cannot be separated.
  • ρ rests on very few OD readings: one in the first session, two in the third.
  • Tubes sharing a cuvette, a time slot or a side of a firmware reset are not fully independent, so every interval and p value on this page is nominal.
  • Three sessions, each with a different recipe design, are a small basis for a model. Every analysis here looked at the same three.
  • The cross-session conversion is a post hoc analysis (3.4). How ρ should be read was chosen after the three sessions had been seen, there are only three of them, and no prediction made in advance has tested it yet. The formal comparison agrees with the figures — a shared slope is not rejected after conversion, p = 0.897, and is rejected before it, p < 0.001 — but for the same reason those p values describe the data and validate nothing.
  • Mixing session 1 supports no lack-of-fit test (3.3). It repeated one recipe, which gives two degrees of freedom of pure error — too few for the test to detect anything — so that column is left blank for it rather than filled with p values that say nothing either way.
  • Neither background curve is a calibration (3.5), and neither may be used to subtract a background from other readings. Both were worked out after the readings were seen. The fitted curve rests on one parameter that only three readings above zero could constrain. The measured curve is one culture on one day, and it covers F3 from 5 to 89 and no further. Neither says whether the background is scattered light or the cells' own fluorescence. A reading that sits on the background means the reader read nothing above it there, not that the plate was not induced.
