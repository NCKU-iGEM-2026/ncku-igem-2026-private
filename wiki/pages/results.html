Part 02

Inhibition

Overview

To evaluate the functional effect of our aptamer system, we examined three quorum-sensing-associated phenotypes of Pseudomonas aeruginosa: swarming motility, pyocyanin production, and biofilm formation (Wu et al., 2025). Cb-2 is the aptamer our engineered retron produces, and it is what these assays set out to test. ALS-5 and A16-2 are chemically synthesized aptamers taken from the literature; they are included only as benchmarks, to give Cb-2’s effect something to be measured against.

Across all three assays, Cb-2 reduced the phenotype relative to the untreated control: swarming motility, biofilm biomass and pyocyanin production alike. In two of the three — biofilm and pyocyanin — it was the most effective of the aptamers tested. These results support the functional inhibitory effect of the retron-derived Cb-2 system on P. aeruginosa quorum-sensing-associated behaviors.

Swarming assay

Swarming is a coordinated, flagellum-dependent bacterial motility across a semisolid surface that is regulated by quorum sensing networks (Las and rhl) and promoted by biosurfactants like rhamnolipids. Before optimization, PAO1 stayed at the inoculation site and no dendritic spreading appeared (Figure 1). After optimizing the plate preparation and incubation conditions (see Engineering), PAO1 finally formed the characteristic branched, dendritic swarming pattern (Figure 2A).

Under optimized assay conditions, swarming migration was quantified by measuring swarming radii relative to the untreated control (Figure 2A, B). Every value below is a percentage of the untreated control, so a smaller number means stronger inhibition. Cb-2 reduced the swarming radius to 80.7 ± 5.52% of the control, an inhibition of about 19%. The two reference aptamers reduced it further — A16-2 to approximately 66% and ALS-5 to approximately 77% — but both varied widely from plate to plate, whereas Cb-2 was the most reproducible of the three. All three aptamers therefore disrupt coordinated, QS-mediated surface movement, and Cb-2 does so the most consistently.

Biofilm Inhibition

Since quorum sensing controls biofilm maturation in P. aeruginosa, assessing biofilm formation provided a complementary phenotypic assay for QS interference. Following swarming inhibition experiments, we investigated the inhibitory impact of the aptamers on biofilm formation, as described by Wu et al. (2025).

We then quantified biofilm biomass with a crystal violet assay (O’Toole, 2011) in 96-well plates (see Protocols). Cultures were adjusted to OD600 0.1 and mixed with each extract (see TRIzol) at a final concentration of 10% (v/v). Control wells received the same volume of LB in place of aptamer. After 48 h of static incubation, adherent biomass was stained with crystal violet (Figure 3A) and the dye was solubilized in 30% acetic acid. Absorbance at 550 nm was blank-corrected and expressed relative to the control (Figure 3B).

Among the three aptamers evaluated, Cb-2 exhibited the most promising anti-biofilm activity, reducing P. aeruginosa PAO1 biofilm biomass by 34% in our latest trial. Given the variability observed across earlier optimization runs, we regard this as a promising preliminary observation. To confirm these findings, we plan to validate the assay across at least three independent biological replicates under standardized conditions and incorporate orthogonal QS readouts, such as pyocyanin quantification.

Pyocyanin inhibition

Pyocyanin biosynthesis serves as a direct indicator of active QS signaling in P. aeruginosa due to its strict reliance on the rhl and pqs regulatory networks. Following chloroform extraction and 0.2 M HCl phase separation, pyocyanin levels were quantified by absorbance at 520 nm (OD520). Spectrophotometric analysis demonstrated a clear reduction in pyocyanin output across all aptamer-treated groups relative to the untreated control (Figure 4).

Cb-2 again gave the strongest suppression, decreasing pyocyanin to 73.30% of control (~27% inhibition) against A16-2 at 81.90% and ALS-5 at 81.33%. This agrees with the biofilm assay, where Cb-2 was also the most effective; in the swarming assay the two reference aptamers inhibited more, though far less consistently. Taken together, the three assays show that the aptamer our retron produces suppresses several P. aeruginosa virulence phenotypes at once, which is what an anti-virulence strategy asks of it.
