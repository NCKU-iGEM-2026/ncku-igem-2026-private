{# ===================== Part 01 · Detection ===================== #}
Part 01

Detection

{# ======================== overview ======================== #}

Overview

The detection module reports the presence of Pseudomonas aeruginosa by sensing its quorum-sensing signal, 3-oxo-C12-HSL. LasR is expressed constitutively; once it binds the signal it activates the LasR-responsive promoter pLasRV, which drives sfGFP.

Two readouts are reported here: fluorescence on an agar plate, where PAO1 culture supernatant diffuses out of a central well, and fluorescence in liquid culture followed over successive readings in a plate reader.

Synthetic 3-oxo-C12-HSL has to be dissolved in DMSO, and the amount of DMSO this takes is enough to kill the cells. The plate assay therefore uses PAO1 culture supernatant as its signal source, and the amount of AHL in that supernatant is not known. How the assay was arrived at is described on the Engineering page.

{# ================== plate-based detection ================== #}

Fluorescence on plate

Engineered E. coli was overlaid in soft agar and PAO1 culture supernatant was placed in a well at the centre of the plate. Fluorescence appeared as a halo around that well, which the plate-photo analysis in LasReader outlines and measures. Details of the assay conditions are described on the Engineering page.

{# ====================== time course ======================== #}

Fluorescence in liquid culture

Both constructs were grown with 1 µM synthetic 3-oxo-C12-HSL and read once an hour for five hours, beside a control carrying the empty vector pBBR1MCS-1 — the same resistance marker, but no sfGFP. Fluorescence is reported as RFU divided by OD600, so it is read per unit of growth rather than per well.

Both constructs sat well above the control at every reading, and the J23101 construct above the ProD one throughout. The comparison does not, however, show that the signal is what switched the reporter on: the control carries no sfGFP gene, so it could only ever be dark. Establishing that the response depends on AHL needs the same construct run with and without the signal, which this experiment did not include.

{# ================ what detection supports ================== #}

What the detection module supports

The biosensor was built, it expresses its reporter, and it responds on plate to P. aeruginosa supernatant in a way the empty vector does not. Its output was also made measurable rather than judged by eye.

  • Reporter expression confirmed both constructs fluoresced above the empty-vector control in liquid culture, at every reading (p < 0.001)
  • Plate response halo around PAO1 supernatant, where the empty-vector control stayed dark
  • Readout millimetres LasReader turns the fluorescent area into a measured span, ready for the hardware to read

Two questions remain open, and both are about what the assay compares against rather than about whether the circuit works. The plate assay has to use PAO1 supernatant, because synthetic AHL needs enough DMSO to kill the cells, so the amount of signal reaching the cells is not known and no dose–response could be measured. The liquid-culture control carried the resistance marker without sfGFP, so it establishes that the reporter is expressed rather than that AHL is what switches it on.

Each has a clear experiment attached: a signal source of known concentration, and the same construct run with and without AHL. They are also part of what the hardware is for. A biosensor whose output sits a few fold above background needs a reader that resolves a weak signal the same way every time, and that is the reader we built.

{# ===================== Part 02 · Inhibition ===================== #}
Part 02

Inhibition

{# ======================== overview ======================== #}

Overview

To evaluate the functional effect of our aptamer system, we examined three quorum-sensing-associated phenotypes of Pseudomonas aeruginosa: swarming motility, pyocyanin production, and biofilm formation.[1] The Cb-2 treatment was prepared from our engineered retron-Cb-2 system and was the main treatment we aimed to evaluate. A16-2 and ALS-5 were chemically synthesized reference aptamers from the literature and were included for comparison. However, because the retron-Cb-2 sample was a crude extract with an unknown aptamer concentration, these groups should not be considered as dose-matched comparisons.

We also included a BL21 extract control in all three assays. This sample was prepared from E. coli BL21(DE3) without the retron plasmid and went through the same culture, harvesting, and TRIzol extraction procedures as the Cb-2 sample. By comparing this group with the untreated control, we could examine whether the extraction background itself contributed to the observed effects.

These three phenotypes are all associated with quorum sensing, but they represent different biological outputs. Swarming reflects population-level motility, pyocyanin represents a QS-regulated virulence-associated metabolite, and biofilm formation reflects a clinically relevant surface-associated phenotype.

All values were normalized to the untreated control from the same batch, which was set as 100%. Each treatment was compared with the untreated control using a one-sample t test, and no correction for multiple comparisons was applied.

The charts show each group as a percentage of that control. In the text and the boxes below we report the same numbers as an inhibition rate, 100% minus the normalized value: a positive rate means the treatment lowered the phenotype, and a negative rate means it raised it. The ± SD is the same either way.

{# ===================== swarming assay ===================== #}

Swarming assay

Swarming motility was quantified by measuring the relative swarming radius of PAO1 after treatment.[2] Details of the assay conditions are described on the Engineering page.

  • A16-2 chemically synthesized 33.74 ± 18.44% inhibition rate versus the untreated control
  • ALS-5 chemically synthesized 22.19 ± 14.36% inhibition rate versus the untreated control
  • Cb-2 extract from retron-Cb-2 cells 19.29 ± 5.52% inhibition rate versus the untreated control (p < 0.01)
  • BL21 extract from cells without the retron −2.3 ± 4.9% inhibition rate versus the untreated control — not significant

All three aptamer treatments reduced swarming relative to the untreated control. The extract control did not move, leaving swarming at the untreated level, so what reduced the radius came with the plasmid and not with the extraction.

{# =================== pyocyanin inhibition =================== #}

Pyocyanin inhibition

Pyocyanin production was quantified by chloroform extraction, followed by acidification with 0.2 M HCl and absorbance measurement at 520 nm.

  • A16-2 chemically synthesized 19.84 ± 3.32% inhibition rate versus the untreated control
  • ALS-5 chemically synthesized 18.56 ± 6.94% inhibition rate versus the untreated control
  • Cb-2 extract from retron-Cb-2 cells 26.28 ± 6.14% inhibition rate versus the untreated control (p < 0.01)
  • BL21 extract from cells without the retron 1.4 ± 0.77% inhibition rate versus the untreated control — not significant

The retron-Cb-2 sample reduced pyocyanin production by more than it reduced swarming, and both reference aptamers moved the same way, while the extract control again stayed at the control level. The reduction in pyocyanin provides an additional functional readout consistent with decreased QS-associated virulence output.

{# ==================== biofilm inhibition ==================== #}

Biofilm Inhibition

Biofilm biomass was quantified by crystal violet staining after 48 h of static incubation and normalized to the untreated control.[1][3] The full method is on the Protocols page.

  • A16-2 chemically synthesized 12.21 ± 8.67% inhibition rate versus the untreated control (p < 0.05)
  • ALS-5 chemically synthesized 10.98 ± 10.69% inhibition rate versus the untreated control — not significant (p = 0.053)
  • Cb-2 extract from retron-Cb-2 cells 34.24 ± 10.93% inhibition rate versus the untreated control (p < 0.001)
  • BL21 extract from cells without the retron −6.9 ± 7.2% inhibition rate versus the untreated control — not significant

This is the largest reduction observed in the retron-Cb-2 group in any of the three assays, and the most clearly significant of them. The extract control sits slightly above the untreated control rather than below it (an inhibition rate of −6.9%), and not significantly so.

{# ===================== overall result ===================== #}

Overall result

Across all three functional assays, the retron-Cb-2 sample was associated with reductions in QS-related phenotypes of P. aeruginosa, while the extract control was not significantly different from the untreated control in any of them.

  • Swarming 19.3% swarming radius, the population-level behaviour
  • Pyocyanin 26.3% pyocyanin production, the metabolite closest to the circuit
  • Biofilm 34.2% biofilm biomass, the largest effect of the three

Together, these results are consistent with the expected functional effect of Cb-2 on quorum-sensing-associated behaviors.

The extract control narrows what the results can be explained by, but it does not close the question: it shows that the effect travels with the retron plasmid rather than with the extraction, not that the active species is retron-derived Cb-2 ssDNA. Our molecular verification remains inconclusive, as the Engineering page sets out. Identifying and quantifying the retron-derived ssDNA directly is still required.

Even so, the functional picture is a consistent one. Three assays measuring different things — a surface behavior, a secreted metabolite and an adherent structure — all moved in the direction our hypothesis predicts, and did so in the retron-Cb-2 group as well as in the reference aptamers. We take that as meaningful support for the retron-Cb-2 system working as designed, and as the reason the molecular verification is worth completing rather than treating as a formality.
