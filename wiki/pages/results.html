Overview

Our approach builds on Wu et al. (2025), which showed that aptamers which are able to reduce P. aeruginosa virulence and biofilm formation by disrupting the Las and rhl quorum-sensing systems. Whereas that study applied chemically synthesised aptamers externally, we asked whether the same signal-capture strategy could be delivered biologically, by engineering a retron system to produce aptamer ssDNA inside a bacterial chassis. Before testing that platform, we first needed to establish which aptamer candidates produce measurable effects on QS-regulated behaviour in our own hands.

We therefore treated PAO1 with three aptamers (A16-2, ALS-5 and Cb-2) and measured three QS-regulated phenotypes: motility (swarming), biofilm formation (crystal violet staining) and virulence-factor production (pyocyanin). Each assay reports on a different branch of the QS network, so a consistent pattern across them gives stronger evidence than any single assay.

Swarming assay

Swarming is a coordinated, flagellum-dependent bacterial motility across a semisolid surface that is regulated by quorum sensing networks (Las and rhl) and promoted by biosurfactants like rhamnolipids. After optimising the plate preparation and incubation conditions (see Engineering), PAO1 finally formed the characteristic branched, dendritic swarming pattern (Figure 2A)

Under optimized assay conditions, swarming migration was quantitatively assessed by measuring swarming radii relative to the untreated control (Figure 2A,B). All evaluated aptamers exhibited inhibitory activity against swarming motility. Aptamers A16-2 and ALS-5 decreased the swarming radius to approximately 66% and 77% of control values, respectively. In comparison, Cb-2 achieved an inhibition of ~19% (relative radius: 80.7 ± 5.15%, n = 6), while demonstrating superior reproducibility and consistency compared to A16-2. These findings validate that target aptamers disrupt coordinated, QS-mediated surface movement.

Biofilm Inhibition

Since quorum sensing controls biofilm maturation in P. aeruginosa, assessing biofilm formation provided a complementary phenotypic assay for QS interference. Following swarming inhibition experiments, we investigated the inhibitory impact of the aptamers on biofilm formation, as described by Wu et al. (2025)

We then quantified biofilm biomass with a crystal violet assay (O’Toole, 2011) in 96-well plates (see Protocols). Cultures were adjusted to OD600 0.1 and mixed with each extract (see TRIzol) at a final concentration of 10% (v/v). Our control wells were received with the same volume of LB in place of aptamer. After 48 h of static incubation, adherent biomass was stained with crystal violet and the dye was solubilised in 30% acetic acid. Absorbance at 550 nm was blank-corrected and expressed relative to the control (Figure 3).

Among the three aptamers evaluated, Cb-2 exhibited the most promising anti-biofilm activity, reducing P. aeruginosa PAO1 biofilm biomass by 43% in our latest trial. Given the variability observed across earlier optimization runs, we regard this as a promising preliminary observation. To confirm these findings, we plan to validate the assay across at least three independent biological replicates under standardized conditions and incorporate orthogonal QS readouts, such as pyocyanin quantification

Pyocyanin inhibition

Pyocyanin biosynthesis serves as a direct indicator of active QS signalling in P. aeruginosa due to its strict reliance on the rhl and pqs regulatory networks. Following chloroform extraction and 0.2 M HCl phase separation, pyocyanin levels were quantified by absorbance at 520 nm (OD520). Spectrophotometric analysis demonstrated a clear reduction in pyocyanin output across all aptamer-treated groups relative to the untreated control (Figure 3).

In alignment with our motility and biofilm assays, aptamer Cb-2 yielded the strongest suppression, decreasing pyocyanin concentration to 73.30% of control (~27% inhibition), outperforming A16-2 (81.90% of control) and ALS-5 (81.33% of control). These results complete our phenotypic evaluation, establishing dual-target aptamers as a compelling anti-virulence platform for neutralizing multifaceted P. aeruginosa pathogenicity.
