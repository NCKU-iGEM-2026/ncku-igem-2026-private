Part 01

Detection

Overview

The detection module reports the presence of Pseudomonas aeruginosa by sensing its quorum-sensing signal, 3-oxo-C12-HSL. LasR is expressed constitutively; once it binds the signal it activates the LasR-responsive promoter pLasRV, which drives sfGFP.

Two readouts are reported here: fluorescence on an agar plate, where PAO1 culture supernatant diffuses out of a central well, and fluorescence in liquid culture followed over successive readings in a plate reader.

Synthetic 3-oxo-C12-HSL has to be dissolved in DMSO, and the amount of DMSO this takes is enough to kill the cells. The plate assay therefore uses PAO1 culture supernatant as its signal source, and the amount of AHL in that supernatant is not known. How the assay was arrived at is described on the Engineering page.

Fluorescence on plate

Engineered E. coli was overlaid in soft agar and PAO1 culture supernatant was placed in a well at the centre of the plate. Fluorescence appeared as a halo around that well, which the plate-photo analysis in LasReader outlines and measures. Details of the assay conditions are described on the Engineering page.

Fluorescence in liquid culture

Both constructs were grown with 1 µM synthetic 3-oxo-C12-HSL and read once an hour for five hours, beside a control carrying the empty vector pBBR1MCS-1 — the same resistance marker, but no sfGFP. Fluorescence is reported as RFU divided by OD600, so it is read per unit of growth rather than per well.

Both constructs sat well above the control at every reading, and the J23101 construct above the ProD one throughout. The comparison does not, however, show that the signal is what switched the reporter on: the control carries no sfGFP gene, so it could only ever be dark. Establishing that the response depends on AHL needs the same construct run with and without the signal, which this experiment did not include.

What the detection module supports

The biosensor was built, it expresses its reporter, and it responds on plate to P. aeruginosa supernatant in a way the empty vector does not. Its output was also made measurable rather than judged by eye.

  • Reporter expression confirmed both constructs fluoresced above the empty-vector control in liquid culture, at every reading (p < 0.001)
  • Plate response halo around PAO1 supernatant, where the empty-vector control stayed dark
  • Readout millimetres LasReader turns the fluorescent area into a measured span, ready for the hardware to read

Two questions remain open, and both are about what the assay compares against rather than about whether the circuit works. The plate assay has to use PAO1 supernatant, because synthetic AHL needs enough DMSO to kill the cells, so the amount of signal reaching the cells is not known and no dose–response could be measured. The liquid-culture control carried the resistance marker without sfGFP, so it establishes that the reporter is expressed rather than that AHL is what switches it on.

Each has a clear experiment attached: a signal source of known concentration, and the same construct run with and without AHL. They are also part of what the hardware is for. A biosensor whose output sits a few fold above background needs a reader that resolves a weak signal the same way every time, and that is the reader we built.

Part 02

Inhibition

Overview

To evaluate the functional effect of our aptamer system, we examined three quorum-sensing-associated phenotypes of Pseudomonas aeruginosa: swarming motility, pyocyanin production, and biofilm formation.[1] The Cb-2 treatment was prepared from our engineered retron-Cb-2 system, and it is what these assays set out to test. A16-2 and ALS-5 are chemically synthesized reference aptamers from the literature, run alongside it. They are not a head-to-head comparison: the retron-Cb-2 sample is a crude extract, so the amount of aptamer in it is not known the way it is for a synthesized oligonucleotide.

A fourth group runs through all three assays as an extract control, labelled BL21 in the charts. It is the same TRIzol preparation made from untransformed E. coli BL21(DE3) — the strain that hosts our retron construct, here carrying no retron plasmid at all — grown, harvested and extracted exactly as the Cb-2 sample was. Everything the preparation brings with it other than the aptamer is in that extract too, so it is what separates an effect of the aptamer from an effect of the liquid it arrives in.

All three are controlled by quorum sensing, so each one reports, from a different angle, on whether the treatment was associated with reduced QS-related phenotypes: swarming is the population-level behavior, pyocyanin the metabolic output closest to the circuit, and biofilm the phenotype that matters most clinically.

Values are normalized to the untreated control run in the same batch. Each treatment was compared with that control (100%) by a one-sample t test; no correction for multiple comparisons was applied.

Swarming assay

Swarming motility was quantified by measuring the relative swarming radius of PAO1 after treatment.[2] Details of the assay conditions are described on the Engineering page.

  • A16-2 chemically synthesized 66.26 ± 18.44% of the untreated control
  • ALS-5 chemically synthesized 77.81 ± 14.36% of the untreated control
  • Cb-2 extract from retron-Cb-2 cells 80.71 ± 5.52% of the untreated control — a 19.3% reduction in swarming radius
  • BL21 extract from cells without the retron 102.3 ± 0.049% of the untreated control — untransformed BL21(DE3), extracted the same way; not significant

All three aptamer treatments reduced swarming relative to the untreated control, and the retron-Cb-2 sample showed the narrowest spread among replicates. The extract control did not move. That control is an extract of untransformed E. coli BL21(DE3) — the same strain we use to express the retron, grown and harvested the same way and put through the same TRIzol procedure, but carrying no retron plasmid — and it left swarming at the control level. What reduced the radius therefore came with the plasmid, not with the extraction.

Pyocyanin inhibition

Pyocyanin production was quantified by chloroform extraction, followed by acidification with 0.2 M HCl and absorbance measurement at 520 nm.

  • A16-2 chemically synthesized 80.16 ± 3.32% of the untreated control
  • ALS-5 chemically synthesized 81.44 ± 6.94% of the untreated control
  • Cb-2 extract from retron-Cb-2 cells 73.72 ± 6.14% of the untreated control — a 26.3% reduction in pyocyanin
  • BL21 extract from cells without the retron 98.6 ± 0.77% of the untreated control — untransformed BL21(DE3), extracted the same way; not significant

The retron-Cb-2 sample reduced pyocyanin production by more than it reduced swarming, and both reference aptamers moved the same way, while the retron-free extract control again stayed at the control level. The reduction in pyocyanin provides an additional functional readout consistent with decreased QS-associated virulence output.

Biofilm Inhibition

Biofilm biomass was quantified by crystal violet staining after 48 h of static incubation and normalized to the untreated control.[1][3] The full method is on the Protocols page.

  • A16-2 chemically synthesized 87.79 ± 8.67% of the control — ~12% reduction (p < 0.05)
  • ALS-5 chemically synthesized 89.02 ± 10.69% of the control — ~11%, not significant (p = 0.053)
  • Cb-2 extract from retron-Cb-2 cells 65.76 ± 10.93% of the control — a 34% reduction (p < 0.0001)
  • BL21 extract from cells without the retron 106.9 ± 7.2% of the control — untransformed BL21(DE3), extracted the same way; not significant

This is the largest reduction observed in the retron-Cb-2 group in any of the three assays, and the most clearly significant of them. The retron-free extract control sits slightly above the untreated control rather than below it, and not significantly so, which is the clearest of the three assays on the point: the crystal violet signal does not fall simply because a TRIzol extract was added to the well.

Overall result

Across all three functional assays, the retron-Cb-2 sample was associated with reductions in QS-related phenotypes of P. aeruginosa, while the extract control was not significantly different from the untreated control in any of them. That control is an extract of untransformed E. coli BL21(DE3), prepared from the same culture volume by the same TRIzol procedure as the Cb-2 sample and differing from it only in carrying no retron plasmid.

  • Swarming −19.3% swarming radius, with the narrowest spread between replicates
  • Pyocyanin −26.3% pyocyanin production, the metabolite closest to the circuit
  • Biofilm −34% biofilm biomass, the largest effect of the three

Together, these results are consistent with the expected functional effect of Cb-2 on quorum-sensing-associated behaviors.

The extract control narrows what the results can be explained by, but it does not close the question: it shows that the effect travels with the retron plasmid rather than with the extraction, not that the active species is retron-derived Cb-2 ssDNA. Our molecular verification remains inconclusive, as the Engineering page sets out, and the concentration of aptamer in the retron-derived sample was not quantified. Comparisons with the chemically synthesized reference aptamers should therefore not be read as dose-matched comparisons. Further molecular verification and quantification of retron-derived ssDNA will be required.

Even so, the functional picture is a consistent one. Three assays measuring different things — a surface behavior, a secreted metabolite and an adherent structure — all moved in the direction our hypothesis predicts, and did so in the retron-Cb-2 group as well as in the reference aptamers. We take that as meaningful support for the retron-Cb-2 system working as designed, and as the reason the molecular verification is worth completing rather than treating as a formality.
