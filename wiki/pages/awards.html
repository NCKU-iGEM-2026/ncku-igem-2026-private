Best New Composite Part: our answers to the four questions

These four questions are iGEM’s, for our chosen part BBa_26LDAZW5 on the Registry (opens in a new tab) (Ec‑67–Cb‑2–retron). These are our answers, with the place on this wiki where each one can be checked.

  1. How does the documentation on the Registry compare to BBa_25LG0OCK, BBa_25IWB2F9, and BBa_25LK9WHI?

    • Follows the same four-part shape as the three reference parts: design rationale, construction, verification protocols and characterization data.
    • Adds a folding simulation of the Cb-2–msd region — the aptamer’s predicted fold, and the same sequence shown in place inside the msd it is carried in (Fig. I1–I2).
    • Could not investigate the molecular structure of Cb-2 produced in vitro — we had no access to instruments that visualize ssDNA structure — so the part is documented by what it does: three quorum-sensing phenotypes of P. aeruginosa, all three significantly reduced, each against an untreated control and an extract control.
    • Full protocols for all three assays are linked to our Lab Book, with figures of the quantified data.
    • The whole engineering round behind the part is written up as well, including the gel work that did not settle and the control we added after it, so the documentation shows how the part was arrived at and not only where it ended.

    More details The engineering round behind the part BBa_26LDAZW5 on the Registry (opens in a new tab)

  2. Did the team show the part works as expected (modeling data can be acceptable)?

    • Three quorum-sensing phenotypes of P. aeruginosa assayed against an untreated control, reported as an inhibition rate: the figure is how far the phenotype fell.
    • Swarming radius: inhibition rate 19.29 ± 5.52% (n = 5, p < 0.01).
    • Pyocyanin: inhibition rate 26.28 ± 6.14% (n = 4, p < 0.01).
    • Biofilm biomass: inhibition rate 34.24 ± 10.93% (n = 6, p < 0.001).
    • Extract control: the same preparation from BL21(DE3) without the retron plasmid was not significantly different from the untreated control in any of the three assays.
    • All three readouts moved the way the design predicts, and the two chemically synthesized reference aptamers moved the same way in the same assays — a surface behaviour, a secreted metabolite and an adherent structure, agreeing with each other.
    • Consistent with Cb-2’s expected function — though molecular verification of the retron-derived ssDNA remains inconclusive, so we do not claim the effect is attributable to Cb-2 alone.

    More details Swarming, Fig. 1 Pyocyanin, Fig. 2 Biofilm, Fig. 3

  3. Could it be useful to the community?

    • The cargo sits in the msd region, so another team can produce a different ssDNA by swapping only that sequence, keeping the msr stem and the Ec67 RT intact.
    • Turns a one-off construct into a programmable platform for intracellular ssDNA production.
    • Changes what it costs to keep an aptamer in use. A synthesized oligonucleotide is bought again for every experiment; a culture is grown from a stock, so the amount on hand follows the medium rather than the budget. That is also what widens the range of things a team can afford to try — more conditions, more replicates, a longer time course — since none of them is a separate purchase.
    • The chassis, the growth and the TRIzol extraction are documented too, so a team taking this part gets the route to an extract, not only a sequence.

    More details Ec67-Cb-2 retron, on Parts TRIzol extraction protocol

  4. How well characterized (experimentally measured or modeled) is this Composite Part?

    • Characterized across three independent QS-associated readouts, 3–6 replicates each, with significance testing against an untreated control.
    • Run alongside two chemically synthesized reference aptamers from the literature, so the size of the effect has something to be read against.
    • Every assay reached significance for the retron-Cb-2 group, in three readouts that fail in different ways — a motility behaviour, a secreted metabolite and an adherent biomass.
    • Full protocols are in our Lab Book, and every figure on Results is the data behind one of these numbers.
    • The extract is crude, so aptamer concentration is unknown — the comparison with the synthesized aptamers is not dose-matched.
    • Every assay carried an extract control — BL21(DE3) with no retron plasmid, grown and extracted the same way — and it did not reproduce the effect, so the extraction background alone does not explain it. It does not rule out growth effects specific to the Cb-2 sample (Model).

    More details Swarming protocol Pyocyanin protocol Biofilm protocol All three readouts, on Results

Best Education: our answers to the four questions

These four questions are iGEM’s. These are our answers, with the place on this wiki where each one can be checked.

  1. How well did their work promote mutual learning and/or a dialogue?

    Through 17 school outreach workshops and interactive rural education initiatives, we moved beyond one-way knowledge transfer to actively listen to feedback from students across different age groups and local educators, establishing a dynamic framework for mutual learning and inclusive dialogue.

    More details 17 sessions 31 anonymous questions Oral feedback, 7 groups Handbook §7.2 Feedback from students and teachers (PDF, opens in a new tab)

  2. Is it documented in a way that others can build upon?

    We thoroughly documented our complete lesson plans, rural co-learning handbooks, and outreach SOPs on our Wiki platform as open-source, modular resources—enabling future teams to continuously adapt, refine, and carry forward the legacy of synthetic biology education.

    More details Education Handbook, EN & 中文 §6 Lesson Units, eight of them (PDF, opens in a new tab) §5 A Sample Camp: schedule, roles, run of show (PDF, opens in a new tab) §8 Recommendations for Future Teams (PDF, opens in a new tab) Appendices A–C: safety rules, the questionnaire, a document checklist (PDF, opens in a new tab)

  3. Was it thoughtfully implemented?

    Fully recognizing the geographical and resource limitations faced by rural areas, we thoughtfully tailored customized learning materials and tiered curricula for different age groups. This effectively bridged educational disparities while engaging children through step-by-step interactive experiences that sparked their curiosity and genuine passion for science.

    More details Kindergarten Ten rural camps Junior high Senior high Pre/post results, N = 217

  4. Did the team convince you that their activities would enable more people to shape, contribute to, and/or participate in synthetic biology?

    By bridging the urban-rural educational divide through inclusive outreach, our team successfully demystified genetic engineering for the public and empowered the next generation to envision synthetic biology as an accessible, practical tool for solving real-world clinical and societal challenges.

Best Hardware: our answers to the four questions

The four questions the judges score, with the place on the Hardware page where each answer can be checked. How we got here is told at the end of that page.

  1. Does the hardware address a need or problem in synthetic biology?

    Green fluorescence from a GFP reporter is one of the commonest readings in synthetic biology, and it usually waits for a shared plate reader. CAPTURE-Screen reads 200 µL at the bench for US$54 to US$161, 21 to 65 times less than an open-source plate reader, and runs from one USB cable.

    More details What the assay asked of a reader 72 iGEM teams took the same reading US$161, line by line US$54 for the laser-cut build A laptop, one cable and the reader

  2. Did the team conduct user testing and learn from user feedback?

    Our wet lab used both readers, and each was rebuilt around what they said: on the 3D-printed build, loading fell from 30–40 s to 3–5 s. Two members then ran it from our Run card and found it clear; one met an error and dealt with it from the card.

    More details What they told us, and what followed Version 1 to version 2, card by card The Run card’s remedies Seven problems from the operating log Laser-cut build: two changes

  3. Did the team demonstrate utility and functionality in their hardware proof of concept?

    We believe the proof of concept we set out for was met. With no emission filter, the 3D-printed build’s 90° light path read 86 counts from a GFP culture and 0 from one without, with nothing subtracted. The laser-cut build, cheaper and easier to assemble, removes its background in the data, and its signal rose in a straight line with sfGFP (r² ≈ 0.97). Neither has yet resolved AHL induction.

    More details Four design choices, and what each gives The dilution series Beside a plate reader Every channel of a read A dark read of zero Laser-cut build: how a reading is made Laser-cut build: validation Where it stops

  4. Is the documentation of the hardware system sufficient to enable reproduction by other teams?

    The 3D-printed build’s CAD sources, firmware and parts list are on iGEM GitLab under the MIT licence, with a twelve-step assembly and a Run card for every command and error. The laser-cut build’s parts are redrawn in the browser for your own board, with its assembly in photographs and its current firmware on GitLab too.
