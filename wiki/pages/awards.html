The criteria below are iGEM’s own, quoted from the 2026 Judge Handbook. Under each is the page of this wiki where we document it.

Bronze

“A Bronze medal is awarded to those teams that have participated in iGEM, presented their work, and made a contribution for future teams.”

  1. Competition Deliverables

    Complete the following Competition Deliverables: Wiki, Presentation Video, Judging Form and Judging Session.

  2. Project Attributions

    Describe what work your team members did, as well as what other people did for your project, using the standardized Project Attributions Form.

    Documented on Attributions

  3. Contribution

    Make a useful contribution for future iGEM teams.

    Documented on Contribution

Silver

“A Silver medal is awarded to those teams that have additionally addressed these pillars of an iGEM project: Engineering Success and Human Practices.”

  1. Engineering Success

    Demonstrate engineering success in a technical aspect of your project by going through at least one iteration of the engineering design cycle.

    Documented on Engineering

  2. Human Practices

    Explain how you have determined that your work is responsible and good for the world.

    Documented on Human Practices

Gold

“A Gold medal is awarded to those teams that have shown excellence in three areas beyond the Silver medal in General Biological Engineering and chosen Specialization(s).”

  1. Excellence in Synthetic Biology

    Impress the judges with your work towards three Special Awards of your choice. You must demonstrate excellence in both General Biological Engineering and in at least one Specialization.

The three Special Awards we chose

General Biological Engineering

Best New Composite Part

Judged on the documentation in the Registry for the part named on our Judging Form.

What the judges score

  1. How does the documentation on the Registry compare to BBa_25LG0OCK, BBa_25IWB2F9, and BBa_25LK9WHI?
  2. Did the team show the part works as expected (modeling data can be acceptable)?
  3. Could it be useful to the community?
  4. How well characterized (experimentally measured or modeled) is this Composite Part?

Best Education

Judged on our Education page.

What the judges score

  1. How well did their work promote mutual learning and/or a dialogue?
  2. Is it documented in a way that others can build upon?
  3. Was it thoughtfully implemented?
  4. Did the team convince you that their activities would enable more people to shape, contribute to, and/or participate in synthetic biology?

Best Hardware

Judged on our Hardware page.

What the judges score

  1. Does the hardware address a need or problem in synthetic biology?
  2. Did the team conduct user testing and learn from user feedback?
  3. Did the team demonstrate utility and functionality in their hardware proof of concept?
  4. Is the documentation of the hardware system sufficient to enable reproduction by other teams?

Documented on Hardware

Best Education: our answers to the four questions

The four questions above are iGEM’s. These are our answers, with the place on this wiki where each one can be checked.

  1. How well did their work promote mutual learning and/or a dialogue?

    Through 17 school outreach workshops and interactive rural education initiatives, we moved beyond one-way knowledge transfer to actively listen to feedback from students across different age groups and local educators, establishing a dynamic framework for mutual learning and inclusive dialogue.

    See for yourself 17 sessions 31 anonymous questions Oral feedback, 7 groups Handbook §7.2 Feedback from students and teachers (PDF, opens in a new tab)

  2. Is it documented in a way that others can build upon?

    We thoroughly documented our complete lesson plans, rural co-learning handbooks, and outreach SOPs on our Wiki platform as open-source, modular resources—enabling future teams to continuously adapt, refine, and carry forward the legacy of synthetic biology education.

    See for yourself Education Handbook, EN & 中文 §6 Lesson Units, eight of them (PDF, opens in a new tab) §5 A Sample Camp: schedule, roles, run of show (PDF, opens in a new tab) §8 Recommendations for Future Teams (PDF, opens in a new tab) Appendices A–C: safety rules, the questionnaire, a document checklist (PDF, opens in a new tab)

  3. Was it thoughtfully implemented?

    Fully recognizing the geographical and resource limitations faced by rural areas, we thoughtfully tailored customized learning materials and tiered curricula for different age groups. This effectively bridged educational disparities while engaging children through step-by-step interactive experiences that sparked their curiosity and genuine passion for science.

    See for yourself Kindergarten Ten rural camps Junior high University workshops Pre/post results, N = 217

  4. Did the team convince you that their activities would enable more people to shape, contribute to, and/or participate in synthetic biology?

    By bridging the urban-rural educational divide through inclusive outreach, our team successfully demystified genetic engineering for the public and empowered the next generation to envision synthetic biology as an accessible, practical tool for solving real-world clinical and societal challenges.

    See for yourself Why we started: 37.0% had heard of the infection 1 of 7 could define synthetic biology SDG 11: the urban–rural gap The Green Cabinet: 24 of 33 improved
