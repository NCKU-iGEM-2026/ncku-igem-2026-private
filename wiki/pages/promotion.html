NCKU Pilot Interdisciplinary Sharing Session

"Sharing our science is not merely about presenting localized results; it is an open invitation to refine our vision through critical dialogue and diverse perspectives."

On May 23, 2026, our team participated in the NCKU Pilot Interdisciplinary Sharing Session, taking an important step forward in our Human Practices and outreach journey. Beyond promoting synthetic biology to a broader academic community, this event offered a rare, highly rewarding platform to connect directly with university peers of similar ages. Experiencing firsthand how students from various disciplines leveraged innovative, out-of-the-box thinking to address urgent challenges facing humanity and the planet resonated deeply with us—striking at the very core and founding spirit of iGEM.

Serving as a crucial checkpoint to evaluate our months of hard work, the presentation allowed us to put our preliminary concepts to the test in the real world. We were privileged to engage in thought-provoking discussions with scholars and experts spanning diverse academic fields. Although some panel members were initially unfamiliar with the specific framework of the iGEM competition, their unbiased, sharp questions and unconstrained perspectives provided us with unexpected and profoundly impactful insights.

This rigorous interactive exchange ultimately triggered a pivotal transformation in our project's narrative and scientific scope. After carefully weighing the experts' constructive feedback, we realized the need to sharpen our focus, prompting us to make a key adjustment—pivoting from a broader entry point centered on Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP) toward a more precise, dedicated investigation into Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection mechanisms. Facing the gaps in our original logic was admittedly humbling and unsettling; however, this platform forced us to re-examine our blind spots with complete honesty. Ultimately, we walked away with invaluable guidance, a clearer scientific direction, and a renewed momentum to recalibrate our pace as we steadfastly march forward.

Lund University Podcast

For collaboration in Human Practices, we really wanted to connect with people beyond our usual circles, so we teamed up with the iGEM team from Lund University in Sweden to record a special Chinese-language podcast episode.

It was such a fun and rewarding experience to sit down and just chat about our project, Capture. We took the opportunity to explain the science behind our fight against Pseudomonas aeruginosa, doing our best to break down complex molecular tools like retrons and aptamers into concepts that anyone tuning in could easily grasp.

But we didn't want the episode to be just a boring science lecture, so we also shared a lot about who we are as people. We opened up about what initially sparked our drive to join iGEM and gave listeners a behind-the-scenes look at our daily lives—sharing the reality of our late-night brainstorming sessions, the inevitable wet lab struggles, and the incredibly tight bond we've built as a team.

Ultimately, working with Lund University was about so much more than just communicating our research; it was a chance to bridge continents, break down language barriers in science, and build a genuine friendship across the global iGEM community.
