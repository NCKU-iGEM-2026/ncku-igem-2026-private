Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs) are becoming increasingly prevalent due to their vast potential across various sectors, particularly in medicine and pharmaceuticals. However, significant misconceptions and controversies continue to surround them. While the majority of the scientific community advocates for the advancement of GMOs, public skepticism appears to be growing.

To better understand public perception of GMOs within the context of our Capture project, we designed a survey focusing on their application in treating Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections. We successfully distributed this survey to an international audience, ultimately collecting 376 responses.

Population Sample

To properly contextualize our findings, we first analyzed the demographics of our 376 respondents. The data (Figure 1) reveals a predominantly young demographic, with 83.3% of participants being 25 years old or younger, which includes 26.1% under 18 and 57.2% aged 18 to 25. Furthermore, we were encouraged to see that a vast majority of respondents have attained at least a high school level of education, with 59.8% holding a senior high school diploma and 28.7% possessing a bachelor's degree (Figure 2). Interestingly, despite this high baseline of general education, only 23.9% of participants reported having a scientific background (Figure 3).

Knowledge on Infection

To begin our analysis, we sought to assess the general public's baseline understanding of Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections. We designed specific questions to gauge their familiarity with the pathogen. Surprisingly, only 37% of respondents had ever heard of P. aeruginosa (see Figure 4), and a mere 0.8% considered themselves to be well-informed about the infections it causes (see Figure 5).

These findings underscored a significant knowledge gap regarding Pseudomonas aeruginosa among the general public. Recognizing this lack of awareness directly inspired our team to take action. Consequently, we launched interactive workshops and community outreach programs designed to educate the public about these infections through an engaging, hands-on approach.

Living Therapeutic Acceptance

Following our evaluation of public awareness, we sought to assess the respondents' acceptance of "living therapeutics" — specifically, the use of engineered bacteria as medical treatments. To achieve this, we posed several questions regarding their stance on genetic modification and gene-edited drugs.

The survey revealed a cautious public attitude. Only 48.4% of the participants expressed support for genetic engineering or modification in general (combining level 4 and 5 support), while a significant portion, 39.9%, remained entirely neutral (see Figure 6). Interestingly, when asked specifically about the development of gene-edited bacterial drugs (see Figure 7), support increased slightly to 63.6% (combining level 4 and 5 support). However, when it came to the actual use of these bacterial drugs on themselves (see Figure 8), the acceptance rate dropped to 58.5% (combining level 4 and 5 acceptance).

From these responses, we deduced that a substantial portion of the population remains hesitant or neutral toward gene editing, particularly when these technologies are applied as direct medical treatments.

What It Told Us

Overall, the results of this survey provided critical insights for our Capture project. The data clearly highlighted a dual challenge: a profound lack of public knowledge regarding Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections, coupled with persistent hesitancy toward genetically engineered living therapeutics. These findings proved that developing a great scientific solution is only half the battle. To ensure the future success and acceptance of our project, we realized we must actively engage with the community. This realization became the driving force behind our subsequent educational workshops and outreach programs, which aimed not only to inform the public about P. aeruginosa but also to demystify genetic engineering, thereby fostering a more informed and receptive society.
