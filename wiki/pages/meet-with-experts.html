Academic expert

Designing for the clinic: priorities, DNA stability and sensor choice

Prof. Tsunglin Liu

Chair of the Department of Biotechnology and Bioindustry Sciences, National Cheng Kung University

Prof. Liu began by asking us what we value most in designing and developing our hardware. Weighing the current needs of medical testing, we settled on three priorities: low cost, speed and convenience, with the aim of a testing method that is both accessible and efficient.

Prof. Liu also suggested that we collect and analyze the relevant DNA sequences, so that we can anticipate how enzymes present in patient samples might degrade them. Doing so would help us choose suitable sequences and make our system more stable and reliable for future clinical use.

On the hardware design itself, Prof. Liu encouraged us to explore a wider range of sensors and to make use of their different detection thresholds and characteristics. Experimenting with different sensing mechanisms may reveal new ways to improve the performance of our detection system.

In short

  1. Know your priorities. Low cost, speed and convenience, for a test that is accessible and efficient.
  2. Plan for the sample. Analyze the DNA sequences against the enzymes in patient samples, and choose ones that hold up.
  3. Widen the sensors. Try sensors with different detection thresholds and characteristics.
Industry · fabrication

Ai Wei Li Laser Engraving Studio: the hardware shell

Ai Wei Li Laser Engraving Studio

Date September 10, 2026

While developing the hardware, thought was put into using cost-effective and reusable materials. Therefore, 3D printing and laser cutting were considered as methods of hardware fabrication. Within the period between July and August, a number of materials and designs were used for the hardware case. Nevertheless, most prototypes could not meet the requirements with respect to strength and efficiency.

In order to improve the design, professional consultation was obtained through working with Ai Wei Li (Laser Engraving Studio). The design files were discussed at the studio, and valuable advice was received concerning material choice and fabrication process. Firstly, it should be pointed out that each substance has its own properties and they should be taken into account during the device construction, including light reflection. The importance of the thickness of the material was stressed, especially at the places where different elements are connected, because the incorrect connection may cause light leakage.

Furthermore, assembly and adjustment of the hardware were performed at the studio under the on-site support of the manufacturer.

In short

  1. Every material behaves differently. Account for each one's properties, light reflection included.
  2. Mind the thickness at the joints. A poor connection between parts lets light leak in.
  3. Build it together. The case was assembled and adjusted at the studio, with the maker on hand.
