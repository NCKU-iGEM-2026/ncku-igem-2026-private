3

SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being

Closest targets: 3.3 — combat communicable diseases; 3.d — strengthen early warning and the management of health risks.

Capture

Pseudomonas aeruginosa does its worst where medicine cannot avoid creating an opening: ventilators, catheters and open wounds. It is consistently among the top pathogens behind healthcare-associated infections, and in the ICU the cost of a late diagnosis is counted in days a patient does not have.

Capture works on the signal the bacteria use to coordinate. One system detects that signal and reports the infection early, before anything has grown on a plate; the other captures it, so the coordination it carries never arrives. The full case — the burden, the gap in today’s diagnosis, and how both systems work — is on Project Description.

Health in the classroom

Our science camps with STEAM from Seed also count SDG 3 among their aims: a medical professional was present at every activity, and the lessons were built around life science children meet in daily life. See Education → STEAM from Seed.

SDG 4: Quality Education

Closest targets: 4.1 — quality primary and secondary education; 4.7 — education for sustainable development.

3.36 → 4.33self-rated understanding of the SDGs, before and after playing (1–5, n = 33)

Most of our human practices is teaching, and we took it to the schools that see the least of it. With the student volunteer group STEAM from Seed we brought life science into their STEAM camps in rural elementary schools; at Puyan Elementary School in Changhua we ran “The Ultimate Code of Life” on equal access and hands-on exploration; and at our two-day NCKU Workshops high-school students went from an enzyme lab to playing our SDG board game. Every session is written up on Education.

Target 4.7 is where the board game comes in: at the NCKU Workshops, students’ own rating of how well they understood the SDGs rose from 3.36 to 4.33 out of 5 after one session. The figures and what the students wrote are on Board Game.

SDG 5 and SDG 10: Gender Equality and Reduced Inequalities

Where a child goes to school should not decide whether they ever see a lab. The STEAM from Seed camps go to rural elementary schools on purpose, and they take every child who comes: no one is selected by background, and every child is treated equally. That is why we count SDG 10 and SDG 5 among the camps’ aims alongside SDG 3 and SDG 4. The camps were also run to the four principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, set out on Education → STEAM from Seed.

SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production

Running ten camps with children means a lot of consumables. Most of the experiments followed the 3R principle — Replace, Recycle, Reduce: we reused equipment wherever we could and sorted the waste properly after each session.

The same thinking shaped our hardware. Cost-effective and reusable materials were the brief for the reader’s case from the start, which is why 3D printing and laser cutting were the methods we tried; how that went, and what a laser engraving studio taught us about choosing materials, is on Human Practices and Hardware.

17

SDG 17: Partnerships for the Goals

None of the above was done alone. The camps exist because STEAM from Seed let us into their curriculum. Within the iGEM community we mentored the Mingdao High School team, met the other Taiwanese teams at the 2026 iGEMers Meetup, and kept working with Team WiST long after it; across continents, we recorded a Chinese-language podcast with the iGEM team from Lund University. With Mingdao and WiST especially, what we passed on was the practical part: how to run outreach, how to reach underserved schools, how to work with outside organisations.

All of it is on Collaboration and Promotion.

All seventeen: The Green Cabinet

The goals above are the ones our work reaches. The Green Cabinet, the board game we designed from scratch, is how we put all seventeen in front of other people. Players lead rival factions in an international council, each racing to bring their own SDGs to the finish line, and every event card is a real world event that pushes some goals forward and others back.

That is the point it was built to make: sustainable development has no single path, and a policy that helps one goal can cost another. Students who played it told us they “learned about some events that had a real impact on the world.” Read how it was designed, see the playtest results, or play it online on Board Game.
