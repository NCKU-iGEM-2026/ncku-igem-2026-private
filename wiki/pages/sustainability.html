Goals we work on

3

SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being

Closest targets: 3.3 — combat communicable diseases; 3.d — strengthen early warning and the management of health risks.

34.2%less P. aeruginosa biofilm, using the aptamer our engineered cells make — measured at our own bench
37.0%of 376 people surveyed had ever heard of P. aeruginosa infections
4 / 7people we interviewed had been on antibiotics that did not work

Capture

Pseudomonas aeruginosa does its worst where medicine cannot avoid creating an opening: ventilators, catheters and open wounds. It is consistently among the top pathogens behind healthcare-associated infections, and in the ICU the cost of a late diagnosis is counted in days a patient does not have.

Capture works on the signal the bacteria use to coordinate. One system detects that signal and reports the infection early, before anything has grown on a plate; the other captures it, so the coordination it carries never arrives. The full case — the burden, the gap in today’s diagnosis, and how both systems work — is on Project Description.

Target 3.3 asks for work against communicable disease, so the fair test is whether any of this does anything measurable. It does. The aptamer our engineered cells make cut P. aeruginosa biofilm biomass by 34.2% — and biofilm was not the only thing that moved. We measured three separate signs of coordinated infection — biofilm, swarming and pyocyanin — and all three fell, every one of them significantly. These are early results on laboratory strains, not a treatment, and the assay conditions and error bars are on Results.

What that buys is bigger than the numbers. An antibiotic kills, and in killing it selects for whatever survives — which is the cycle that creates resistant infections in the first place. Capture does not kill. It takes away the signal the bacteria use to organise, so there is nothing for resistance to be selected for. And because cells inside a biofilm can be 10 to 1000 times harder to treat than the same cells growing free, weakening the biofilm is a way of making the drugs hospitals already have work again rather than demanding new ones. For a health system facing infections its antibiotics no longer clear, that is the point of the whole project. The full argument, with sources, is on Project Description.

Target 3.d is about early warning, and warning only works if people know what they are being warned about. Of the 376 people who answered our survey, only 37.0% had ever heard of these infections; of the seven we interviewed face to face, four had been on a course of antibiotics that did not work. Both are on Survey.

Health in the classroom

Our science camps with STEAM from Seed also count SDG 3 among their aims: a medical professional was present at every activity, and the lessons were built around life science children meet in daily life. See Education → STEAM from Seed.

SDG 4: Quality Education

Closest targets: 4.1 — quality primary and secondary education; 4.5 — eliminate all discrimination in education; 4.7 — education for sustainable development.

10science camps in rural Tainan elementary schools
266children reached by those camps
4.06 → 4.80rural pupils agreeing “science is interesting”, before and after a camp (1–5, N = 217)

Most of our human practices is teaching, and we took it to the schools that see the least of it. With the student volunteer group STEAM from Seed we brought life science into their STEAM camps in rural elementary schools; at Puyan Elementary School in Changhua we ran “The Ultimate Code of Life” on equal access and hands-on exploration; and at our two-day NCKU Workshops high-school students went from an enzyme lab to playing our SDG board game. Every session is written up on Education.

What we most wanted to grow in those rural classrooms was a liking for science, and the children’s own answers say it did. Before and after each camp they rated the same statements from 1 to 5: “science is interesting” rose from 4.06 to 4.80, “I enjoy hands-on experiments” from 4.03 to 4.74, and “science knowledge is too difficult” fell from 2.53 to 1.73 (N = 217, every change p < 0.001). The full analysis is on Education → STEAM from Seed.

SDG 5 and SDG 10: Gender Equality and Reduced Inequalities

Closest targets: 5.b — promote the empowerment of women through technology; 10.3 — ensure equal opportunity and end discrimination

Where a child goes to school should not decide whether they ever see a lab. The STEAM from Seed camps go to rural elementary schools on purpose, and they take every child who comes: no one is selected by background, and every child is treated equally. That is why we count SDG 10 and SDG 5 among the camps’ aims alongside SDG 3 and SDG 4. The camps were also run to the four principles of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, set out on Education → STEAM from Seed.

Under SDG 5 we also went to the girls. At the Medical Research Club of National Tainan Girls’ Senior High School we ran both an education session and a promotion talk, giving its members a first-hand look at synthetic biology and the biotechnology behind Capture — techniques that rarely reach a high-school classroom. See Education and Promotion.

And no activity of ours was closed to anyone: we set no limit on gender or on background for any event we ran. Where we have the figures, the turnout bears that out — at National Xihu Senior High School, with no gender quota in registration, 26 of the 39 participants were girls. We did not record gender at every activity, so that is the one event we can give a number for, and we will not generalise from it. See Education → Xihu.

SDG 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

Closest targets: 9.1 — develop sustainable, resilient and inclusive infrastructure; 9.4 — upgrade all industries and infrastructure for sustainability; 9.5 — enhance scientific research and upgrade technological capabilities.

US$54to build the laser-cut reader, by our own costing
US$161to build the 3D-printed reader; about US$38 with a printer of your own
53STL files, every printed part of both versions, free to download

Reading our biosensor’s fluorescence would normally take a plate reader: a shared benchtop instrument that is booked, scheduled and kept in one building. We built our own instead. CAPTURE-Screen is a single-cuvette fluorescence reader: one blue LED lights the sample, and a multi-channel spectral sensor reads the green light it gives off at 90°. There are two builds on that principle, one 3D-printed and one laser-cut in its own enclosure, and either can sit next to the incubator.

Target 9.5 is about research capability, and the point of the reader is who can have one. By our own costing the two builds come to about US$54 and US$161, against under US$3,500 for a published open-source plate reader — a comparison of price only, since that instrument reads a whole plate and has been validated, and ours reads one cuvette and has not been compared with it. And it is open: every printed part is published as a file, the laser-cut parts are generated in the browser from the measured thickness of the board, and the Hardware page sets out how to print, wire and run it, so another team can build one.

SDG 17: Partnerships for the Goals

Closest targets: 17.6 — knowledge sharing and cooperation for access to science, technology and innovation; 17.16 — enhance the global partnership for sustainable development

None of the above was done alone. The camps exist because STEAM from Seed let us into their curriculum. Within the iGEM community we mentored the Mingdao High School team, met the other Taiwanese teams at the 2026 iGEMers Meetup, and kept working with Team WIST long after it; across continents, we recorded a Chinese-language podcast with the iGEM team from Lund University. With Mingdao and WIST especially, what we passed on was the practical part: how to run outreach, how to reach underserved schools, how to work with outside organisations.

All of it is on Collaboration and Promotion.

All seventeen: The Green Cabinet

The goals above are the ones our work reaches. The Green Cabinet, the board game we designed from scratch, is how we put all seventeen in front of other people. Players lead rival factions in an international council, each racing to bring their own SDGs to the finish line, and every event card is a real world event that pushes some goals forward and others back.

That is the point it was built to make: sustainable development has no single path, and a policy that helps one goal can cost another. We playtested it with 33 high-school students aged 15–18, and 24 of the 33 scored higher on the SDGs afterwards than they had before; one of them told us they “learned about some events that had a real impact on the world.” Read how it was designed, see the playtest results in full, or play it online on Board Game.

In closing

Living the SDGs was never about proving that everything we did fits a UN target. It is what we owe the world as its citizens — doing our part, however small, to leave it a little better than we found it.
