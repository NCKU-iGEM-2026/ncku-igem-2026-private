A fluorescent biosensor reports its target as light, but a team needs a concentration. In between sit standards, blanks, replicates, a curve, and a decision about which part of that curve can be used, usually worked out by hand in a spreadsheet.

LasReader turns that whole process, from reading fluorescence to an AHL concentration, into one clear workflow in the browser, with nothing to install. With CAPTURE-Screen, our low-cost reader (Hardware), the instrument is part of the workflow: LasReader checks the reader, shows which tube to read next, reads each one in a click, and gives every sample's concentration with a 95% interval. Data from any plate reader goes through the same steps.

We built it for our AHL sensor, but the analysis never uses the target's name: any biosensor whose fluorescence rises with concentration works.

1.3The workflow

Seven steps, in the order they happen at the bench. Choose one to see what you do and what LasReader does.

1Plan the standards

You do

Prepare standards of known concentration, and blanks with none.

LasReader does

  • Asks for at least 4 concentrations and 2 blanks, the minimum for a four-parameter curve and a limit of detection.
  • On CAPTURE-Screen, sets the reading order so instrument drift falls on every concentration equally.

In Plate Reader Assay and CAPTURE-Screen → Calibrate

2Read

You do

Read the plate on any plate reader, or the tubes on CAPTURE-Screen.

LasReader does

  • Takes the plate as the reader exports it (Figure 2), with fluorescence divided by OD600 if you choose.
  • On CAPTURE-Screen, reads each tube in one click and subtracts the dark for you (Figure 4).

In Plate Reader Assay and CAPTURE-Screen

3Check the data

You do

Make sure the instrument behaves and no reading is off.

LasReader does

  • Self-checks CAPTURE-Screen with a buffer-only cuvette, and flags any tube that saturated, scattered more than the blank, or was read under other settings.
  • Excludes a reading only with a written reason, and keeps it in the export.

In CAPTURE-Screen → Instrument, and every fit

4Fit the curve

You do

Draw a standard curve through the standards.

LasReader does

  • Fits a weighted four-parameter logistic curve and works out the limits of detection and quantification (§2.1).
  • Marks the usable range: the only part of the curve that returns a number.

In Plate Reader Assay and CAPTURE-Screen

5Convert the samples

You do

Read each sample's concentration off the curve.

LasReader does

  • Averages the replicates, converts once, scales by the dilution, and gives a 95% confidence interval.
  • Outside the usable range, says “below LOD” or “above range” instead of a number.

In Plate Reader Assay and CAPTURE-Screen → Measure

6Compare instruments

You do

Measure the same samples on a commercial plate reader and on CAPTURE-Screen.

LasReader does

  • Pairs the results by sample name and shows how well they agree: a log-log plot, the geometric mean ratio and Bland–Altman limits of agreement (§2.1).

In Cross-Validation

7Export and reproduce

You do

Record the results so that someone else can check them.

LasReader does

  • Exports a CSV with every reading, every exclusion and its reason, and every result, plus the curve as a PNG. CAPTURE-Screen backs everything up to one JSON file.
  • Because every reading and exclusion is in the file, anyone can enter them again and get the same curve.

Part 2

How it decides

How a reading becomes a concentration, and how sure that number is: a weighted four-parameter fit, limits set from the blanks, and a 95% interval on every result. Every equation below is what the code computes; the full derivation is in the repository's model document.

{# 方法依資料流排成五步：模型 → 加權擬合 → 極限 → 反推與區間 → 儀器比較。 每條式子都對過 LasReader 的 frontend/js/curve_fit.js（1–9）與 js/compare.js（10）， 符號在每條式子下方的 where 說明裡定義。id="key-ideas" 保留（本頁其他地方連過來）； 其他地方用「§2.1, step N」引用，步驟順序改了要一起改。 #}

2.1The method, step by step

The same five steps run in both tools, in the order the data flows through them. Each step gives its equations, defines every symbol, and names the section of the model document that derives it.

  1. Model the response
  2. Weight and fit
  3. Set the limits
  4. Invert, with an interval
  5. Compare instruments

1Model the response

A biosensor saturates, so its signal is S-shaped on a log concentration axis. LasReader uses the four-parameter logistic (4PL), whose parameters each mean something at the bench.

F(c; θ) = B + {{ frac('T − B', '1 + (EC50 / c)h') }}, θ = (T, B, ln EC50, h) (1) where B is the signal with no target, T the saturated signal, EC50 the concentration at half response and h the Hill slope. Blanks enter at c = 0, where F = B, so they fix the baseline directly. Fitting ln EC50 keeps EC50 positive; h is held between 0.05 and 20.

Model document §7

2Weight and fit

Replicates scatter more at high signal than at low, so an unweighted fit would let the top standards dominate. LasReader measures how the scatter grows from the replicates themselves and weights each reading by the inverse of its variance.

σ2(F) = a + bF2 (2) An additive part a and a proportional part b, found by least squares from each concentration's replicate variance against its squared mean, with a, b ≥ 0.
θ̂ = arg minθ Σi {{ frac('(yi − F(ci; θ))2', 'σi2') }}, σi2 = max(si2, a + byi2) (3) where yi is reading i at concentration ci, and si its own read-noise SD (CAPTURE-Screen measures it on every read; it is 0 for a plate reader). The sum is minimised by Levenberg–Marquardt.
Σθ = {{ frac('χ2min', 'N − 4') }} (J⊤WJ)−1 (4) The uncertainty of the fitted curve: J is the N × 4 matrix of derivatives ∂F/∂θ at θ̂, W = diag(1/σi2), N the number of readings and χ2min the minimised sum in (3). With one reading per concentration and no read-noise estimate, the fit is unweighted and a is taken from the residuals.

Model document §8, §9

3Set the limits

A curve can only be read where it is both above the noise of the blanks and still rising. LasReader works out that stretch from the data and never extrapolates beyond it.

LOD = F−1(F̄0 + 3s0), LOQ = F−1(F̄0 + 10s0) (5) where F̄0 is the larger of the blanks' mean and B, s0 the blanks' standard deviation, and F−1 the inverse curve of (7).
cmin = max(LOD, clowest standard), cmax = min(chighest standard, EC50·191/h) (6) EC50·191/h is where the curve reaches 95% of its span: setting F = B + 0.95(T − B) in (1) gives (EC50/c)h = 1/19. Above it, a change in signal smaller than the noise moves the concentration by a large factor. A sample outside [cmin, cmax] is reported as “below LOD” or “above range”, with no number.

Model document §10

4Invert, with an interval

A sample's replicates are averaged in signal first, then converted once: the curve is non-linear, so averaging converted concentrations would be biased. The interval is worked out on the log scale, because concentration errors are multiplicative.

ln ĉ = ln EC50 + {{ frac('1', 'h') }} ln {{ frac('F̄ − B', 'T − F̄') }} (7) where F̄ is the mean of the sample's n replicate readings.
Var(ln ĉ) ≈ gF2 {{ frac('a + bF̄2', 'n') }} + gθ⊤ Σθ gθ, 95% CI = exp(ln ĉ ± 1.96√Var) (8) The first term is the noise of the reading, which shrinks as n grows; the second is the uncertainty of the curve, from (4), which every sample shares and more replicates cannot reduce. This is the delta method: a first-order expansion of (7).
gF = {{ frac('1', 'h') }} ( {{ frac('1', 'F̄ − B') }} + {{ frac('1', 'T − F̄') }} ), gθ = ( −{{ frac('1', 'h(T − F̄)') }}, −{{ frac('1', 'h(F̄ − B)') }}, 1, −{{ frac('1', 'h2') }} ln {{ frac('F̄ − B', 'T − F̄') }} ) (9) The derivatives of (7) with respect to the signal and to the four parameters in θ, in the order of (1).

Model document §11, §12

5Compare instruments

Cross-Validation asks whether CAPTURE-Screen agrees with a commercial plate reader on the same samples. Both tools run the same fitting code, curve_fit.js, so any difference comes from the instruments, not from two pieces of arithmetic. Agreement is measured on the log scale, as a ratio:

di = log10 {{ frac('ĉiCAPTURE', 'ĉiplate') }}, GMR = 10d, LoA95 = [10d − 1.96sd, 10d + 1.96sd] (10) where d and sd are the mean and standard deviation of di over the paired samples. The geometric mean ratio (GMR) is the typical bias; the Bland–Altman limits of agreement (LoA) are the range 95% of single comparisons fall in. The tool also counts the pairs whose 95% CIs overlap.

Assumptions. Replicate readings are independent, with roughly normal errors; the response rises with concentration (T > B); and the interval in (8) is a first-order approximation with 1.96, which runs somewhat narrow when there are few tubes. These are listed with the other limits in Table 5.

Steps 1–4 can be tried on your own readings in §3.2, which runs the same code.

2.2Rules it keeps

§2.1 says how a number is computed. These rules say when LasReader refuses to give one, so a result is never more certain than the data behind it. Each is enforced by the code, not left to the person at the bench.

Before the fit

R1No curve from too little data
With fewer than 4 standard concentrations or 2 blanks, the fit will not run. Plate Reader Assay, CAPTURE-Screen
R2Nothing disappears silently
Quality flags only warn. A reading is excluded only with a written reason, and stays on the plot and in the export. Plate Reader Assay, CAPTURE-Screen

When it gives a number

R3No number outside the usable range
Outside cmin to cmax (§2.1, step 3), only the bound is reported, never an extrapolated value. Plate Reader Assay, CAPTURE-Screen
R4A curve converts only what it was made for
Each curve is bound to the reader's configuration fingerprint (e4c7f2 on the step bar, Figure 3). Readings taken any other way are not converted with it. CAPTURE-Screen

Over time

R5A saved curve never changes
Refitting makes a new curve, so every result can be traced to the curve that produced it. CAPTURE-Screen
R6No demo data
With CAPTURE-Screen off, the pages say it is offline. They never show made-up readings. All tools

2.3How the pieces connect

LasReader has three parts: the reader, a small backend, and the pages in your browser. All of the fluorescence analysis runs in the browser; the backend only relays the reader and analyses plate photos.

Part 3

For other fluorescent biosensors

The analysis does not depend on AHL. A team whose fluorescent biosensor gets brighter as its target rises, and who can make standards of known concentration, can use the Plate Reader Assay today. Table 1 lists what other setups would change.

3.1Who can use it

LasReader was built for our AHL biosensor, so its pages say "AHL" and its labels and export columns say nM. The analysis never uses either: it fits standards of known concentration to their readings and converts samples through that curve. Any unit works, as long as standards and samples share it; keep a note of yours, since the labels still say nM.

What it does need is at least 4 standard concentrations and 2 blank readings, and a signal that rises with the target.

3.3Standards it builds on

Where a common format or an established method already exists, LasReader uses it, so its inputs come straight from a lab's instruments and its outputs open in the tools a lab already has. Table 2 lists them, and the one standard it does not use.

Table 2. Standards and established methods in LasReader
Standard or methodWhere LasReader uses it
The 96-well plate layout, wells A1–H12[3]The Plate Reader Assay takes the 8 × 12 block any plate reader can export or copy, with or without its row letters and column numbers, and keeps each reading's well in the export.
CSV (RFC 4180[4]), UTF-8The exports are CSV files that Excel, R and Python open as is; the Plate Reader Assay's has one row per reading. Cross-Validation reads the exports back with an RFC 4180 parser.
Calibrated fluorescence units, such as the InterLab's MEFL[5]The signal can be in any unit, so readings a team has already converted with a fluorescein standard go in unchanged and the curve's signal axis is in those units.
The four-parameter logistic[6]The reference model for a saturating standard curve in binding assays, best fitted with weights (§2.1, steps 1–2), with parameters a biologist can read.
LOD and LOQ from the blanks, 3 and 10 standard deviationsThe blank mean plus a multiple of the blank SD is the usual way to set both limits[7]; 10 is IUPAC's default multiplier for quantification[8]. Both are carried through the curve into concentrations (§2.1, step 3).
Bland–Altman limits of agreement[9]Cross-Validation compares two instruments the way method-comparison studies do, on the log scale (§2.1, step 5).
OpenAPI 3.1[10], WebSocket[11], JSON[12]The backend describes its own API at /docs, and CAPTURE-Screen speaks JSON over WebSocket (§3.4).
SBOL[13]Not used. LasReader handles measurements, not genetic designs; the biosensor strain is recorded as free text in every export.

3.5Run it, build on it

To use LasReader you need only a browser: open the live app. To run it yourself you need Python 3.14 and Git; the frontend has no build step and needs no Node.js. 

git clone https://gitlab.igem.org/2026/software/ncku-tainan/lasreader.git
cd lasreader
bash scripts/setup.sh      # once
bash scripts/dev.sh        # backend on :8000, frontend on :5500

On Windows PowerShell, run scripts\setup.ps1 and scripts\dev.ps1 instead. cd backend; pytest runs the 46 backend tests, and the API documents itself at /docs.

Also in LasReader

Plate Imaging · Agar Vision

A separate tool for plate phenotypes: the size and spread of colonies, such as a swarming colony, measured from photos of the plates.

Input
Plate photos taken from above, up to 24 per batch, each with its strain, condition and replicate.
Scale
The plate is found in the photo, and its known diameter (93 mm by default, adjustable) turns pixels into millimetres.
Detection
The background is corrected, colonies are thresholded, and a region that looks like more than one colony is split. Photos on a purple UV background are detected and handled separately.
Output
Each colony's longest span, equivalent diameter and area, in one CSV for the batch, and every photo returned annotated so you can check the plate edge was found.
Reproducible
The analysis is deterministic: the same photos and plate diameter give the same numbers. The CSV records both on every row.

The analysis is Tzu-Chiao Chou's colony-measurement code, run unchanged on the backend in Python with OpenCV. The backend keeps neither the photos nor the results.
