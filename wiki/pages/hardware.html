    CAPTURE-Screen is a single-cuvette fluorescence reader. It is built to read the green fluorescence of our biosensor culture at the bench, without a plate reader: one blue LED lights the sample, and a multi-channel spectral sensor reads what it gives off at 90°.

    There are two readers on this principle, a 3D-printed build and a laser-cut build in its own enclosure. This page sets out what the two share, then each build in turn.

    Why build a reader

    A plate reader can already make this measurement. The case for building a reader is about who can make it, where, and at what cost.

    Cost and access
    A shared benchtop reader is booked, scheduled and located in one building. An instrument that costs a few thousand NT dollars in parts can sit next to the incubator, and can be rebuilt by anyone with the source files.
    A measurement we can defend
    We can state our own optical geometry, drive current, integration time and dark baseline, because we chose them. That makes a result traceable in a way that a black-box instrument setting is not.
    An engineering object to test
    Building it forces every assumption into the open: stray light, alignment, repeatability. Most of this page exists because building the thing produced questions that a plate reader would have hidden.

    The plate reader stays our benchmark: excitation 485 nm, emission 510 nm, 200 µL.

    The assay it is built for

    The reader has to fit an assay the wet lab defines: our biosensor makes GFP in response to AHL, the quorum-sensing signal. This is what we are designing against.

    Reporter
    sfGFP. Construct and strain not yet specified.
    AHL series
    10 µM, 1 µM, 100 nM, 10 nM, 1 nM, as planned.
    Volume
    200 µL per reaction.
    Incubation
    37 °C, read every hour for six hours.
    Benchmark
    Plate reader at excitation 485 nm, emission 510 nm, 200 µL.

    No reading on this page comes from an AHL-induced culture. AHL induction has not been run yet, and there is no plate-reader dataset to compare against; our 470 nm excitation also differs from the plate reader's 485 nm. What the reader has shown is that it tells a culture reported to carry the sfGFP plasmid from one without it, and from medium.

    How it measures

    Both readers measure the same way. Blue excitation crosses the sample along one axis, and the detector looks at it from 90° to that axis; the reporter is superfolder GFP2.

    The drawing shows this layout to scale, as it is built in the 3D-printed build. There, excitation that passes through the sample ends in a light trap on the far side, so it does not reach the detector, and the sample sits in a UVette, a disposable cuvette with a 10 mm and a 2 mm light path4.

    Ten channels instead of one filter

    A conventional fluorimeter puts one narrow filter in front of one photodiode. The AS73411 has eight filtered channels across the visible range plus a clear and a near-infrared channel, so one read returns the shape of the light and not a single number. That makes a reading checkable: at the 3D-printed build's first light the excitation showed up concentrated in F2 and F3, the two channels either side of 470 nm.

    AS7341 channel map

    Hover or focus a channel for its role in this instrument

    Eight filtered channels at nominal centres of 415, 445, 480, 515, 555, 590, 630 and 680 nm, plus an unfiltered Clear channel and a near-infrared channel. Excitation is nominally 470 nm and sits between F2 and F3; sfGFP emission is read on F4 at 515 nm.

    Working settings on the 3D-printed build: gain 512×, ATIME 255, ASTEP 280, about 200 ms per integration. Centre wavelengths are the sensor's nominal channel centres.

    What the builds have shown

    The 3D-printed build

    Across three sessions version 1 of this build read zero on its emission channel for every sample without GFP, and above zero for 35 of the 38 tubes that held GFP culture. In the two sessions with enough signal, the reading rose in proportion to the GFP culture. Rebuilt as version 2, it read one batch of culture on 2026-10-05: zero again for every sample without GFP, and a signal on every cycle from 12.5 µL of GFP culture in a 200 µL sample upward. It has not yet read an AHL-induced culture, and it has not been calibrated against a fluorescence standard.
