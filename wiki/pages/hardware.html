    CAPTURE-Screen is a single-cuvette fluorescence reader: a 3D-printed optical core, one blue LED, a multi-channel spectral sensor and a microcontroller. It is built to read the green fluorescence of our biosensor culture at the bench, without a plate reader.

    Across three sessions it read zero on its emission channel for every sample without GFP, and above zero for 35 of the 38 tubes that held GFP culture. Within a session the reading rose in proportion to the GFP culture. It has not yet read an AHL-induced culture, and it has not been calibrated against a fluorescence standard.

    Why build a reader

    A plate reader can already make this measurement. The case for building a reader is about who can make it, where, and at what cost.

    Cost and access
    A shared benchtop reader is booked, scheduled and located in one building. An instrument that costs a few thousand NT dollars in parts can sit next to the incubator, and can be rebuilt by anyone with the source files.
    A measurement we can defend
    We can state our own optical geometry, drive current, integration time and dark baseline, because we chose them. That makes a result traceable in a way that a black-box instrument setting is not.
    An engineering object to test
    Building it forces every assumption into the open: stray light, alignment, repeatability. Most of this page exists because building the thing produced questions that a plate reader would have hidden.

    The plate reader stays our benchmark: excitation 485 nm, emission 510 nm, 200 µL.

    The instrument

    Blue excitation crosses the sample along one axis, and the detector looks at it from 90° to that axis. Excitation that passes through the sample ends in a light trap on the far side, so it does not reach the detector. The sample sits in a UVette, a disposable cuvette with a 10 mm and a 2 mm light path4; the reporter is superfolder GFP2.

    Ten channels instead of one filter

    A conventional fluorimeter puts one narrow filter in front of one photodiode. The AS73411 has eight filtered channels across the visible range plus a clear and a near-infrared channel, so one read returns the shape of the light and not a single number. That makes a reading checkable: at first light the excitation showed up concentrated in F2 and F3, the two channels either side of 470 nm.

    AS7341 channel map

    Hover or focus a channel for its role in this instrument

    Eight filtered channels at nominal centres of 415, 445, 480, 515, 555, 590, 630 and 680 nm, plus an unfiltered Clear channel and a near-infrared channel. Excitation is nominally 470 nm and sits between F2 and F3; sfGFP emission is read on F4 at 515 nm.

    Working settings: gain 512×, ATIME 255, ASTEP 280, about 200 ms per integration. Centre wavelengths are the sensor's nominal channel centres.

    It has to be shielded

    In a darkened room, or under an opaque box, every channel reads zero with the LED off, out to 25× the working integration time. In ordinary room light it does not: an empty UVette reads F4 = 11. Four of those counts come in through the mechanism and seven down the cuvette itself, which is transparent and stands in an open slot. The leak does not grow in proportion to integration time, so a dark read cannot simply be subtracted.

    The operating rule. Measure in a darkened room or under an opaque shield. Because a shielded dark read is exactly zero, a dark read that is not zero marks that cycle as invalid: it is dropped and its raw row kept. The firmware has flagged one such cycle on its own.

    The circuit

    The excitation branch turns one LED3 on and off at a current that cannot damage it: a resistor-limited LED with an N-channel MOSFET on the low side, switched from one pin of the controller. It is a breadboard circuit that was measured, not a product design. The gate pulldown, R3, is the part that is not optional: without it the LED can emit while the controller boots, during what the firmware takes for a dark baseline.

    How it got here

    Each step in the log below was a test whose result changed what we did next.

    Development log

    Eight steps, 2026-08-14 to 2026-10-03

    1. 2026-08-14 to 08-17

      The printed parts arrive

      The printer flagged walls too thin to print on one part, which was redrawn first. Of 59 pieces, 57 arrived; two were missing, and two broke as their supports were removed.

      What it changed We built with what we had, taped the two breaks, and wrote down where they broke for the next revision.

    2. 2026-08-25

      First light

      With the LED at 5.553 mA, a paper scatterer at the sample position read F2 = 87 and F3 = 81 against dark reads of zero. With nothing in the beam, stray excitation stayed under one count.

      What it changed The light path works, taped joints included. The UVette's orientation was fixed from here on.

    3. 2026-08-27

      Room light gets in

      An empty UVette under ordinary room lighting read F4 = 11 with the LED off. In a darkened room, or under an opaque box, every channel read zero.

      What it changed A shield became mandatory, and “the dark read must be zero” became the rule that voids a cycle.

    4. 2026-09-02 to 09-04

      First fluorescence

      Running the project firmware, a culture reported to carry sfGFP read F4 = 19.58 ± 0.79 and its control read 0, over twelve cycles each. Twelve cycles are not twelve samples.

      What it changed The emission channel is usable. We had called that culture “induced”; no AHL had been added, and the word was withdrawn.

    5. 2026-09-09 and 09-14

      Five days apart

      A GFP culture read 40.50 ± 1.23 over four insertions, with its control at zero. Five days later the corresponding culture read 32.67 ± 1.83, lower by 19.3 %. The instrument had been moved in between and the state of the culture was not known.

      What it changed A difference across days cannot be explained afterwards, so every comparison now carries its reference in the same session.

    6. 2026-09-16, 09-21 and 09-29

      Three mixing sessions

      GFP stock, non-GFP stock and medium, mixed at set fractions. Samples without GFP read zero every time, and within a session the reading rose in proportion to the GFP culture. A thin stock on 09-21 left three tubes at zero.

      What it changed The stock has to be dense enough to clear the floor, and one session's slope cannot be carried to another as it stands. The Model page takes it from there.

    7. 2026-09-23 to 10-02

      The weak parts, redrawn

      The cuvette seat and the excitation end were redrawn: a pocket the cuvette drops into and lands in, an LED holder that grips the LED without a clip, and a baffle that prints on its side. With the cap that never arrived, that makes five parts to print. Two fit coupons were printed first, and the pocket and the socket that fitted by hand set the two dimensions a drawing cannot settle.

      What it changed The fractures became a check: each printed layer's cross-section against the fullest section within 3 mm of it. Where the parts broke that ratio was 0.63 and 0.32; the five new parts keep at least 0.82, against a threshold of 0.75. It is a geometric screen tuned on those two breaks, not a strength calculation.

    8. 2026-10-03

      Version 2 on the bench

      New printed parts were fitted, and one resistor was changed to take the LED from 5.55 mA to about 10 mA. With the same empty cuvette the rebuilt reader read F2 = 7.75 and F3 = 6.71 after an adjustment, inside the range version 1 had read, and about twice that once the current was raised. The emission channel stayed at zero throughout.

      What it changed The reader is no longer the instrument that produced the bench and culture readings on this page. Its fit, its alignment and its response to a culture have to be measured again before the two can be put side by side.

    The week-by-week record is in the Notebook.

    Decisions that narrowed the first prototype 5 decisions Show Hide

    Recorded project decisions from 2026-08-09. The marked ones removed capability from the prototype on purpose.

    Turbidity QC is out of P1 acceptance narrows the scope
    Red-light transmission and turbidity QC are removed. The far side of the excitation axis is occupied by the light trap, so there is no transmission path and the measurement is not physically implementable in this geometry.
    470 nm excitation for the first round
    Three independent design documents agree on the nominal 470 nm Cree LED, and the LED holder was dimensioned from that part's datasheet.
    The UVette is a benchmark, not the product vessel
    The reaction cartridge is a joint wet–dry decision that has not been made. Future containers will be handled with adapters rather than by modifying approved optical modules.
    PlateDock is out of P1 scope narrows the scope
    Petri-dish imaging and swarming analysis are held as separate future work. No CAD for it exists in any revision, and P1's resources belong on validating one reader properly.
    Angled-wedge light trap selected for manufacture
    The stepped-baffle variant is deferred, not rejected. Both validate digitally and no bench evidence separates them, so the selection is a manufacturing choice and explicitly not a claim that the alternative is worse.

    Running a measurement

    The 3D-printed build is driven over the serial port, one command per line, under its shield. One run reads three cycles of dark, lit, dark.

    Operating protocol 7 steps Show Hide
    1. Connect, power up, and put the opaque box over the instrument.
    2. id: go on only if the sensor reports ready and its settings read back as written.
    3. Empty cuvette, LED not armed, run: all twelve channels must read zero in all three phases. This is the check that the shield is on.
    4. Empty cuvette again, arm and run: the day's empty-cuvette reading. It is not used as a reference across days.
    5. A medium blank, 200 µL.
    6. Each sample: check the cuvette against the light for bubbles, the beam passing 8.5 mm above its floor; name it with sample; then run, which reads three cycles of dark, lit, dark.
    7. disarm when done. A successful run does not disarm by itself.
    How to read one row of data
    The row saysIt meansDo
    flag=OK, fault=NONEA valid reading, including when F4 is 0.Keep it.
    AMBIENT_PRESENTThe dark read before the flash was not zero.Void the cycle, fix the shield, read again. Do not subtract.
    SATURATEDA channel reached full scale, 65 535.Dilute the sample.
    NOT_ARMEDThe LED was not armed, so there is no signal.Arm and run again.

    Two rules sit above the steps. The controller is reset once, at the start, and not again: a sensor initialisation fault has followed a reset, for a reason we have not found, and in that state the firmware refuses to run. And a voided row stays in the file. It is left out of the statistics, not deleted.
