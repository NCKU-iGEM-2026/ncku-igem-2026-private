Our wet lab is building the two things this project has to read: a reporter designed to answer an acyl-homoserine lactone (AHL) signal by making sfGFP, and a swarming assay that shows what a given condition does to how the bacteria move.

What neither of them has yet is a way of being read that is the same on Tuesday as it was on Monday. CAPTURE-Reader is that reading step. It is not a second attempt at the biology — it is a measurement interface: two modules that turn what the wet lab produces into numbers and images that can be repeated, paired with each other, and handed to someone else.

What the hardware has to do

Two parts of our project need reading, and they are read in different states of matter.

System 1 — the reporter
Designed to answer AHL by expressing sfGFP. It is a liquid sample, and its answer is a fluorescence level.
System 2 — the aptamer construct
Whatever effect it has shows up on solid medium as a change in swarming. That answer is an image, not a number, until something measures it.

The decision that shapes the whole instrument is this: we do not build a second detector for System 2. Liquid samples taken from different System 2 conditions are given to one fixed System 1 reporter, and that reporter's fluorescence becomes the liquid readout. The plate keeps its own, separate readout.

  1. System 2 conditions
  2. a fixed System 1 reporter
  3. Liquid Reader + PlateDock

This is also the cheapest honest answer to a hardware problem. Building one well-registered light path and using it for everything is far more likely to give us numbers we trust than building two mediocre ones.

Two modules, and which one is the main one

Primary

Liquid Reader

A fixed-geometry fluorimeter that reads one sample at a time.

Takes
a sealed tube, or a 10 mm square cuvette
Measures
relative sfGFP fluorescence, plus a red-light transmission QC
Built from
a 470 nm LED, a long-pass filter cutting on near 500 nm, an AS7341 spectral sensor, an ESP32
Gives back
F_corrected, a leakage index, T_red, a QC flag, and a CSV row
Supporting

PlateDock

A jig that photographs a plate the same way every time, and the image analysis that follows it.

Takes
a swarming plate, which stays sealed throughout
Measures
area, equivalent diameter, coverage, and an overlay showing what was counted
Built from
a fixed phone height, fixed diffuse lighting, a dish locator and a fiducial
Gives back
image QC and a phenotype that can be matched to a liquid reading

What we deliberately left out of v0

Every hour spent on a mechanism that does not change the answer is an hour not spent on the repeatability that does. These are the trades we made, and why.

Liquid Reader: how the light path works

One AS7341 does two jobs. Its F4 channel, near 515 nm, reads the green light sfGFP gives off; its F7 channel, near 630 nm, reads how much red light gets through the sample. The two LEDs that make those readings possible are never on at the same time.

Three things that have to be right

Liquid Reader: holding the sample still

A fluorimeter with a good light path and a sloppy holder is still a bad fluorimeter. If the tube can sit a millimetre lower, or turn a few degrees, the reading changes for reasons that have nothing to do with biology. The mechanical job is therefore registration first and appearance second.

Two cartridges, for two different jobs

Liquid Reader: electronics, timing, and what comes out

An ESP32 runs the measurement at a fixed gain and integration time, sequences the LEDs, averages the readings, and writes them out over USB as CSV. A lid interlock refuses to record a valid reading while the lid is open. You enter the sample ID first; after that it is one button.

  1. 1 Dark Both LEDs off. Measure the sensor's own offset.
  2. 2 Blue 470 nm on. Read F4 ≈ 515 nm: the sfGFP signal.
  3. 3 Off Both off again, so the two sources never overlap.
  4. 4 Red 630 nm on. Read F7 ≈ 630 nm: the transmission QC.

One press averages several readings. That average describes how steady the instrument is. It does not stand in for biological replicates, which still have to be separate cultures.

Three numbers, and what each one is not

PlateDock: photographing a plate the same way twice

Swarming is already visible by eye. The problem is that a photograph taken by hand carries the angle, the height and the room lighting into the measurement along with the phenotype. PlateDock removes those three variables and nothing else — it is a jig and an analysis, not a second instrument.

No motorised stage, and no model trained on a handful of plates. The overlay matters more than either: it shows exactly which pixels were counted, so a reader can disagree with us.

Pairing the liquid and the solid readings

Two measurements only mean something together if they came from the same thing. Liquid and solid records share a batch and a sample ID, and each group needs n ≥ 3 independent biological replicates before any of it is worth comparing.

G0

PAO1 only

The baseline phenotype and the baseline signal.

G1

PAO1 + chassis or empty vector, or a non-binding aptamer

Rules out the chassis, the vector, and the act of co-culturing itself.

G2

PAO1 + the active System 2 construct

What we are actually asking about.

Liquid branch

The matched liquid sample stimulates the fixed System 1 reporter, and the Liquid Reader returns F_corrected with its QC.

Solid branch

The matched plate, still sealed, goes to PlateDock, which returns area, diameter and an overlay with its QC.

Both branches write into one CSV, keyed by sample ID, so the comparison that matters — G2 against G1, in liquid and on solid — can be read as a direction rather than as two unrelated charts.

If we can only run G0 and G2, the instrument can still be demonstrated — but then the most we can say is that the complete System 2 condition made the difference. Attributing it to the aptamer specifically is what G1 buys, and without G1 we do not get to claim it.

How we will know it works

Planned — pass conditions set in advance

The conditions below were fixed before any measurement was taken. That is the point of writing them here: a threshold chosen after seeing the data is not a threshold.

If the biology runs late, the hardware still has something to submit: the built instrument, its reference validation, the CAD and bill of materials, the firmware, the data schema, a PlateDock demonstration, and this validation plan — written before the data rather than after it.
