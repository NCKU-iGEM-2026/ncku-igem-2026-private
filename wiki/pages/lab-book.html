Ligation with T4 DNA Ligase (NEB #M0202)

Introduction

Experimental summary

The T4 DNA Ligation protocol catalyses the formation of a phosphodiester bond between a free 5′-phosphate and an adjacent 3′-hydroxyl group of double-stranded DNA, using ATP as a cofactor. In practical terms, it is the enzyme that seals a digested insert into a digested vector and converts the two linear fragments into one covalently closed circular plasmid that can be replicated by a bacterial host.

In our workflow, ligation is the joining step of a conventional restriction-cloning route: the vector and the insert are first cut with restriction enzymes to generate compatible cohesive (sticky) ends, purified to remove enzymes, buffer and small fragments, and then brought together with T4 DNA Ligase. The ligated product is subsequently introduced into competent E. coli by transformation, and correct assembly is confirmed by antibiotic selection and colony PCR.

Experimental objective

This technique allows:

  • Covalently join the EcoRI/PstI-digested, column-purified Ec67 insert to the linearised pET28a backbone, generating the circular recombinant plasmid pET28a–Ec67.
  • Carry out the reaction at a 1:3 vector-to-insert molar ratio — 50 ng (0.020 pmol) pET28a to 37.5 ng (0.060 pmol) Ec67.
  • Produce product transformable into competent E. coli, recoverable by kanamycin selection (pET28a marker).
  • Retain a pre-ligation (no-ligase) aliquot as a gel reference.
  • Deliver a transformable product to the downstream transformation/electroporation and colony-PCR steps.

Materials

  • 10X T4 DNA Ligase Reaction Buffer (0.020 pmol, 1 ng/µL)
  • T4 DNA Ligase (400 U, 8 U/µL)
  • Vector DNA (4 kb) (0.020 pmol, 1 ng/µL)
  • Insert DNA (1 kb) (37.5 ng = 0.060 pmol, 0.75 ng/µL, 1:3 ratio)
  • Nuclease-free water, MilliQ

Procedure

Set up the T4 DNA Ligase Reaction

Note: T4 DNA Ligase should be added last. The table below shows a ligation using a molar ratio of 1:3 vector to insert for the indicated DNA sizes. Use the NEB calculator to calculate molar ratios.

  1. Thaw the 10X T4 DNA Ligase Buffer and resuspend it at room temperature. Mix until the solution is completely clear, then keep it on ice.
    Tip: Aliquot the 10X buffer less concentrated so when thawing, the DTT gets soluble more easily.
  2. Set up the following reaction in a microcentrifuge tube on ice:
    ComponentVolume (µl)
    10X T4 DNA Ligase Buffer5
    pET28a DNA: 50 ng (0.020 pmol)20
    Ec67 DNA: 37.5 ng (0.060 pmol)20
    Nuclease-free water4
    T4 DNA Ligase1
    Total50
  3. Gently mix the reaction by pipetting up and down and microfuge briefly to collect the contents at the bottom of the tube. Do not vortex vigorously.
  4. For cohesive (sticky) ends, incubate at 16°C overnight or room temperature for 10 minutes. For blunt ends or single base overhangs, incubate at 16°C overnight or room temperature for 2 hours.
  5. Heat inactivate at 65°C for 10 minutes to stop the reaction.
  6. Chill on ice and/or store it at −20°C.
  7. ENDPOINT: The ligation product is now ready for transformation, which is documented in a separate protocol.
    For reference: NEB specifies 1–5 µL of the reaction into 50 µL of competent cells; our laboratory transforms 2 µL into 25 µL of chemically competent E. coli DH5α.
