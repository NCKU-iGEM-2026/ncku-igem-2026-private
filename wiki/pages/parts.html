The retron system mainly consists of three components: two non-coding RNA elements, msr and msd, and a reverse transcriptase (RT), which is able to reverse transcribe the RNA sequence of the msd to ssDNA. The msr is a conserved sequence, which forms a stem structure for the reverse transcriptase to bind to the RNA. In addition, the msd is the sequence that the RT will reverse transcribe.

By changing the msd sequence, this part could be used as the system to produce different kinds of ssDNA aptamers.

E7 is a lysis protein. Its expression lyses the cell, releasing the Cb-2 aptamer that our retron system produces inside it. The plasmid was constructed, but the lysis module has not yet been validated.

E7 is a lysis protein, used to release a product made inside E. coli. We place it downstream of the LasR-responsive promoter pLasRV, so that detecting AHL triggers release rather than fluorescence.

It is 47 residues, beginning with a hydrophobic signal sequence that ends in the cysteine of a lipobox.
