Many iGEM teams build a fluorescent biosensor, and then have to measure it: find a plate reader, turn its readings into concentrations, and work out what went wrong when the numbers look odd. We built our own way through all three for our AHL sensor, and this page hands it over: a reader you can build for US$54 to US$161, the software that turns its readings, or a plate reader’s, into concentrations, and the troubleshooting we wrote down along the way. The files are on iGEM’s GitLab under the MIT licence: free to use, change and pass on, as long as the copyright notice goes with them.

A fluorescence reader you can build

CAPTURE-Screen reads one cuvette at the bench: a 470 nm LED lights the sample, and a ten-channel spectral sensor reads it at 90°. We built it twice, and either build can be made from what we publish.

A shared plate reader is booked, scheduled and in one building; a reader of your own can sit next to the incubator. Everything below is written up in full on the Hardware page; each line links to the section that holds it.

3D-printed build

Print it, wire it, run it

  • 38 printed pieces in matte black PLA, each one saved as an STL file from the page’s 3D view. On a printer other than ours, print the two fit coupons first.
  • 12 assembly steps, in the order the reader’s builder checked.
  • The circuit: an ESP32, the AS7341 detector on its I²C bus, and a 2N7000 MOSFET switching the LED at about 10 mA through 217 Ω.
  • Run from any serial terminal at 115 200 baud, one command per line, with a script in the repository that stamps and keeps every line of the log.
  • About US$161, most of it printing bought from a shop; with a printer of your own, about US$38 and 180 g of PLA.

Laser-cut build

Cut it to your board

  • Parts generated in the browser from the measured thickness of your board. The optical layout stays fixed and thicker board makes the walls grow outward; the generator was checked across 2.80–3.20 mm and refuses to make files at 3.25 mm.
  • One 450 × 300 mm sheet for every jointed part, with 8 mm tabs and slots made 0.1 mm smaller a side for the laser’s kerf.
  • 10 assembly steps and 6 operating steps, with an OLED display and a button on the reader itself.
  • Run from LasReader, or from a serial monitor at 115 200 baud.
  • US$53.54 as costed, the enclosure included. The LED, its resistor and the cuvette were not priced, and we have not guessed at them.

Software that turns readings into concentrations

LasReader is the wet-lab workflow of a fluorescent biosensor in the browser: standards, blanks, replicates and a curve, with nothing to install.

We built it for our AHL sensor and our own reader, but the analysis uses neither: it fits standards of known concentration to their readings and converts samples through that curve. Any unit works, as long as standards and samples share it. It keeps rules a team can rely on: no concentration is given outside the range the curve can support, and a reading left out of a fit stays in the run, on the plot and in the export, with the reason.

Troubleshooting we wrote down

What cost us time, so that it need not cost yours. Each problem is written up on the Hardware page, in the section where it happened.

Table 3. Problems we met, and what fixed them
What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
A dark read is not zero (AMBIENT_PRESENT) Light is getting in. Void the cycle, fix the shield and read again. Do not subtract it, and leave the voided row in the file.
The reader refuses: ENABLE is not in a defined acquisition state The detector lost power or was reset. Reset the controller once. If that fails, unplug the USB for five seconds and check the detector’s wires: SDA on GPIO21, SCL on GPIO22, and 3V3.
The LED lights while the controller boots Without a pulldown on the MOSFET’s gate, the LED can light before the firmware takes charge of it. Keep the gate pulldown in the circuit.
The LED never lights (NO_SIGNAL;LED_DISABLED) It was not armed, or the sketch was built with its LED gate off, as it ships. Arm and run again; build the sketch once with CAPTURE_LED_HARDWARE_ENABLE=1.
A channel reads 65 535 (SATURATED) It has reached full scale. Dilute the sample.
The same tube reads differently each time it is put back The cuvette’s height and position were set by hand, with a collar, in four steps. A seat that sets both by itself. Ours went from 3.6–6.3% of the tube’s own mean to 0.76%.
Counts do not match from one session to the next The reader was moved between sessions: an empty chamber read about 310 in one and 160 to 190 in another. Read a tube with no sfGFP in every session and reference that session to it.
Tubes below about 20% sfGFP culture scatter by tens of percent They are the most sensitive to how they were mixed. Mixing them by serial dilution should reduce this; we have not tested it yet.
Culture density cannot be read off the fluorescence channels A 90° geometry suits fluorescence and does not track turbidity; no choice of channels recovers it. Measure cell density separately, with a spectrophotometer.
The reading rises with how dense the suspension is, even with no reporter in it In a suspension scraped from a plate, the emission channel picks up a background that rises with scatter. A cuvette without the reporter read 1.78. Compare samples of matched density, read one without the reporter beside them, and record the OD. We have not yet set a ceiling on density.

What is not done yet

So that nobody builds on more than is there.

  • Nobody has yet built a second reader from our instructions; the assembly order was checked by the reader’s own builder.
  • Neither build has been compared with a plate reader, and the 3D-printed build has not been calibrated against a fluorescence standard. Its one session on AHL plates did not resolve induction, and the laser-cut build has not read an AHL-induced culture.
  • A light-path check, reading a fixed reference such as the same blank tube at intervals, is not yet part of the firmware.
  • LasReader’s Plate Reader Assay is yet to be checked against a full real dataset.
  • LasReader fits rising responses only: a sensor whose fluorescence falls as its target rises gives no curve.

The full lists are the Software page’s Limitations and next steps and the Hardware page’s Limits and next steps. If you build a reader from these files, run LasReader on your data, or meet a problem that is not here, the contact links at the foot of every page reach us.
