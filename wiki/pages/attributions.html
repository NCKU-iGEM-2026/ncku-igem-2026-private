Use of generative AI

We used three AI assistants for the tasks listed below: Claude (Anthropic), Codex (OpenAI) and Gemini (Google). This is the use we declared in our Project Attributions Form; this section gives the context behind it. They were tools we worked with. None of them is a member of our team, and none is credited as a contributor anywhere on this page.

Where we used it

  • Claude (Anthropic) — analysis, hardware, wiki coding and visualization.
    • Analysis. Assisting with data analysis and with developing the models on our Model page.
    • Hardware. Assisting with 3D modeling and with the design of our Hardware.
    • Wiki coding and visualization. Implementing the wiki's layout and styling, its figures and visualizations, the SDG board game on our Board Game page, its cross-device multiplayer, and the interactive Taiwan map on our Education page.
    • Fixing what was broken. Finding and correcting layout and interaction faults, including a navigation menu that could not be opened on phones, and map pins that could not be clicked.
    • Testing. Writing automated browser tests that check the pages and the board game across screen sizes and devices.
    • Transcription. Converting our own data — our card database and reference list among it — into the formats the website and the board game read.
    • Translation. Translating our Chinese text into English for this wiki. We checked every translation ourselves.
  • Codex (OpenAI) — hardware and analysis.
    • Data processing. Automating the input and output of our own data, so that readings moved between files without being retyped.
    • Design verification. Checking the CAD files behind our Hardware builds.
  • Gemini (Google) — visualization and wiki coding.
    • One decorative illustration. The illustration behind the “Global Burden of Healthcare-Associated Infections” panel on our Home page, there for visual presentation (hais-without-bg.avif). It is the only AI-generated image anywhere on this wiki. It is decorative by construction — a background image, hidden from screen readers — it carries no data, and no claim we make rests on it.

What stayed ours

These tools assisted us; they did not do the work for us and they did not decide anything. Every decision — what to investigate, how to design it, what the results mean — was made by our team members. Every AI-assisted output was reviewed, corrected where it needed correcting, and validated before it was committed: the members responsible for each part understood the work in it and can explain it. Everything published under our name remains our responsibility.

Three things we want to state plainly, because they are the ones that decide whether a scientific record can be trusted:

  • No AI-generated data. Every number, plot, gel image and model output on this wiki comes from an experiment we ran or a computation we have documented. No figure offered as evidence was generated, altered or cleaned up by an AI tool.
  • No AI-written science. The reasoning — what the project did, why it was designed that way, and what each result does and does not support — is ours. Where these tools touched the prose, it was to translate or tidy sentences we had already written, as listed above.
  • References checked by us. Every citation on this wiki was taken from the source and checked against it by the team member who used it, and every link resolves to the work cited.

Because every one of you gave something, Capture could be completed — and could stand on the world stage.

Thank you. — NCKU-Tainan 2026
