Part 1

Inhibition model

A conditional swarming model: testing Cb-2 and estimating an A16-2 dose series.

Our inhibition module is designed to supply single-stranded DNA aptamers that bind the acyl-homoserine lactone (AHL) signals of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. We modelled signal sequestration and synthesis feedback, calibrated two parameters on ALS-5 and A16-2 swarming measurements, and compared the prediction with Cb-2, which was excluded from calibration. The baseline prediction misses Cb-2; broader parameter uncertainty accommodates its measured effect but gives little predictive precision. The model also suggests a relative A16-2 dose to investigate.

  • Cb-2 Baseline mismatch; uncertain generalisation The nominal swarming prediction is 0.992 versus a measured ratio of 0.820. The joint scenario range is much wider and overlaps the measurement. See the Cb-2 comparison.
  • 1.6× A16-2 dose hypothesis Median relative dose for 50 % swarming inhibition; central 90 % range 1.0–2.6× among 296 successful calibrations out of 400 draws. This is an unvalidated scenario estimate. See the dose analysis.

Code and exported results: inhibition-model, revision {{ model_revision[:8] }} (MIT). Source links below refer to that revision. The measurements and assay photographs are reported on the Results page.

1.1 The question

Three designs were tested: ALS-5 (35 nt, targeting 3-oxo-C12-HSL), A16-2 (44 nt, targeting C4-HSL), and Cb-2 (93 nt, targeting both signals).[1] ALS-5 and A16-2 were chemically synthesised reference oligonucleotides; the project's retron-Cb-2 sample was applied as a TRIzol extract rather than a purified oligonucleotide. Their active concentrations were not established on a common scale.

The assays show partial reductions in QS-associated phenotypes. These observations do not directly measure free AHL, receptor occupancy, or QS shutdown. We asked:

  1. Can a model calibrated on ALS-5 and A16-2 account for the held-out Cb-2 swarming measurement?
  2. Under its stated assumptions, what relative A16-2 dose would be useful to investigate next?

The Cb-2 comparison precedes the dose extrapolation so that its limitations are clear before the proposed dose is interpreted.

1.2 What the measurements show

Read alongside the Results page, the distinction is between an observed phenotype reduction and an explanation of that reduction. The Cb-2 preparation reduced the measured phenotypes; the baseline model's failure to reproduce those effects limits its explanatory and predictive use. It does not overturn the observations or establish that AHL sequestration caused them.

A16-2 has the largest estimated swarming reduction; Cb-2 has the largest estimated pyocyanin and biofilm reductions. These are point-estimate rankings, not tests of superiority between treatments. Each ratio is relative to an untreated control on the same plate; 1 means no change.

Replicate counts, control sharing and alternative analyses

Swarming has 10, 11 and 10 readings from 10, 11 and 5 plates for ALS-5, A16-2 and Cb-2 respectively. Pyocyanin has 4, 3 and 4 readings from 4, 3 and 3 plates. Biofilm has 6, 6 and 12 biological replicates, each treatment spanning two plates; there are three plates overall. Repeated readings on one plate are not additional independent plates.

The biofilm analysis follows the wet lab's identification of the wells as separately grown biological replicates. Common plate conditions and shared controls can still correlate their ratios. With only two plates per treatment and limited overlap between treatments, these intervals should not be read as resolving plate effects. A plate-unit analysis does not resolve the treatment effects (reported p values 0.13–0.45).

The Results page uses arithmetic summaries and one-sample t tests; this page uses log ratios. Two Cb-2 biofilm wells lacking traceable controls are excluded here. The cell-level control choices and crosschecks are recorded in {{ model_file('results/prism_crosscheck.csv', 'prism_crosscheck.csv') }}. The largest reported inhibition difference between the two analyses is 2.5 percentage points for Cb-2 biofilm.

The figure captions on Results report n = 5 per group for swarming and n = 6 per group for biofilm. Those counts do not fully match the analysis units listed here. The log transformation alone cannot explain a difference in sample counts, and the excluded biofilm wells do not reconcile all of them. Until the source data and plotted subsets are reconciled, the two sets of counts should be treated as an unresolved reporting discrepancy, not as interchangeable descriptions of the same analysis sample.

A replicate-unit bootstrap gives a useful alternative summary. It places ALS-5 biofilm just below a ratio of 1, unlike the primary interval above; we therefore retain the description “borderline” rather than select a significance label from the more favourable method. The bootstrap swarming point estimates, 0.766 for ALS-5 and 0.639 for A16-2, are the calibration targets in section 1.3. 1.3 Model and calibration

The mechanistic core tracks two AHL signals, their sequestration by aptamers, and feedback on synthesis. Its output is a long-time receptor occupancy ratio, mapped to relative swarm radius. This describes a proposed mechanism; it does not establish the mechanism from phenotype data.

R/Rcontrol = (θ2/θ2,control)β

R is swarm radius and θ2 is rhl receptor occupancy. The nominal model fixes β = 1; the joint uncertainty analysis varies it from 0.5 to 2. Neither occupancy nor this mapping was measured in the project.

Calibration uses two swarming observations to fit two parameters: the las-to-rhl gate parameter and an effective A16-2 affinity. The ALS-5 and A16-2 targets are 0.766 and 0.639 from the plate-unit bootstrap. Cb-2 is excluded. Agreement at the two calibration points is expected from fitting and is not independent validation.

Main assumptions: one well-mixed compartment; rapid binding; one competing binding site per aptamer; a power-law occupancy-to-radius mapping; and Cb-2 affinities equal to the specialists' values. The single-site model does not establish Cb-2's physical binding architecture, and equal affinities are not a guaranteed upper or lower bound on its performance.

Full equations and the long-time solution

L1 is 3-oxo-C12-HSL; L2 is C4-HSL. The free concentrations determine occupancy:

θi = {{ frac('Li,free', 'Ki + Li,free') }}

The differential equations track total ligand. The implemented removal term acts on this total pool:

dL1,total/dt = v1[e1 + (1 − e1)θ1h] − d1L1,total
dL2,total/dt = v2[e2 + (1 − e2)θ2h]g(θ1) − d2L2,total
g(θ1) = (θlas + θ1)/(1 + θlas)

ei is the basal synthesis fraction, h is the feedback exponent, vi is the maximum synthesis rate, and di is the removal rate. θlas is a non-negative gate parameter, not a receptor occupancy: larger values make the gate less dependent on las.

For aptamer j at total concentration Aj, the competitive-binding balance is:

Dj = 1 + L1,free/Kd,j1 + L2,free/Kd,j2
Li,total = Li,free + ΣjAj(Li,free/Kd,ji)/Dj

Both ligands compete for one site in this model. A specialist's non-target affinity is set to infinity. Independent binding domains, aptamer turnover and spatial transport are not explicitly represented.

The code integrates from low initial total signal (0.001 nM for each ligand) and extends the integration until a drift criterion is met. It selects the long-time state accessible from that initial condition; it is not a simulation of the assay's measured endpoint time. Synthesis rates are back-calculated from assumed untreated steady-state signal levels. Source: {{ model_file('src/sponge_model.py', 'sponge_model.py') }}.

The 2019 C4-HSL aptamer study does report affinity measurements.[3] Their direct applicability to this project's 44 nt A16-2 sequence and assay conditions has not been established here. The fitted affinity absorbs errors in dose, mapping and other inputs; it is not a replacement measurement of molecular binding. Likewise, the 2013 ALSAP-5 study and the 2025 design study can describe different stages of an aptamer's provenance. Transferring a Kd requires matching the actual sequence and conditions.

The nominal mass concentration, 7.797 ng/µL, is a Nanodrop reading of the retron extract recorded on 2026-07-31. It is not a quantified active Cb-2 concentration or a recorded applied plate dose. Applying it to all three treatments is a modelling convention. With 330 g/mol per nucleotide, the assumed pure-aptamer amounts would be 675, 537 and 254 nM for 35, 44 and 93 nt respectively. Equal-molar scenarios instead match each construct to A16-2's nominal molar amount.

Joint sampling combines plate-unit bootstrap calibration targets with a Latin-hypercube design over model inputs and nominal dose. β is varied over 0.5–2, the ALS-5 affinity over 20–35 nM, and dose over 1–30 ng/µL. Untreated signal levels span 60–2600 nM for 3-oxo-C12-HSL and 56–5000 nM for C4-HSL. These are the exported analysis's scenario bounds, not established concentration limits for our plates; the exact literature attribution for their upper endpoints has not been verified. The full design, including activation constants, basal synthesis and feedback, is in {{ model_file('params/parameters.csv', 'parameters.csv') }} and {{ model_file('src/inference.py', 'inference.py') }}.[5]

Published signal-transport measurements provide context for the untreated AHL pools;[4] earlier QS models motivate feedback terms.[6] LasR and RhlR studies inform activation assumptions without making reporter activation thresholds interchangeable with equilibrium binding constants.[7][8] Nominal removal rates are 0.014 and 0.087 h−1, motivated by AHL lactonolysis;[9] they affect relaxation but cancel from the steady-state equations when synthesis is back-calculated using those same rates.

296 of 400 joint draws calibrate; 104 do not. In 102 draws the ALS-5 target cannot be reached within the calibration search, and two report multiple roots. All joint quantiles on this page are conditional on the successful subset. They are scenario percentiles, not confidence intervals for an experimentally validated prediction; excluding failed calibrations also limits their coverage. Sources: {{ model_file('results/calibration_draws.csv', 'calibration_draws.csv') }} and {{ model_file('results/uncertainty_summary.csv', 'uncertainty_summary.csv') }}.

Exploratory extensions to pyocyanin and biofilm

The mechanistic core is calibrated on swarming. An additional empirical mapping is fitted separately for each phenotype:

P/Pcontrol = (θ1/θ1,control)w1 (θ2/θ2,control)w2

With the core at its nominal fit, the two ALS-5 and A16-2 effects in each assay determine that assay's two exponents. Thus pyocyanin and biofilm measurements do enter their own mapping fits. Cb-2 is excluded from all these fits. The mapping omits explicit pqs regulation and should be read as an empirical extension.

1.4 Cb-2: prediction and uncertainty

The nominal equal-mass prediction underestimates Cb-2 swarming inhibition. It predicts a radius ratio of 0.992, above the primary measured 95 % interval, 0.697–0.964. An equal-molar scenario predicts 0.490, below that interval. Neither nominal point reproduces the observation. These are alternative assumptions about an unrecorded dose, not an experimental comparison of two dosing protocols.

The full joint scenario range changes the interpretation. For Cb-2 swarming its median is 0.669, with a central 90 % range of 0.064–0.989. This overlaps the measured effect. The nominal mismatch therefore does not reject every sampled parameterisation; the broad range also does not provide a precise, independently validated prediction.

Cb-2 is one held-out treatment, applied as a crude extract on five swarming plates without either specialist. Active dose, affinity, plate differences and components of the extract remain confounded. The exploratory pyocyanin and biofilm extensions also miss their nominal comparisons; their additional fitted mappings and narrower uncertainty scope are described in the phenotype extension methods.

Figure 3 · Why the dose convention changes the prediction

At the same mass, a 93 nt construct supplies about 2.1 times fewer molecules than a 44 nt construct. In the nominal model this reduction moves Cb-2 across a feedback threshold. Report both mass and active molar dose when comparing constructs; length alone does not determine their experimental value.

Post hoc checks of spatial structure, dose and affinity

Spatial structure can accommodate the swarming observation. The nominal one-compartment response has a sharp transition: near 8.69–8.70 ng/µL its ratio changes from about 0.991 to 0.658. Averaging patches with an assumed lognormal local-dose spread (log standard deviation 0.2–0.7) puts the swarming prediction inside the measured interval. This is a post hoc sensitivity check; heterogeneity was not measured, so it does not establish the cause of the mismatch. The same change is insufficient for the two empirical phenotype extensions.

At a spread of 0.5, the nominal A16-2 half-inhibition dose changes from 1.55× to 1.65×. This is local robustness to one structural change, not a joint uncertainty analysis over alternative structures. Source: {{ model_file('results/structure_comparison.csv', 'structure_comparison.csv') }}.

A dose-only refit does not reconcile all three nominal phenotype mappings. At the fitted 1.11× Cb-2 dose, predicted ratios are 0.662, 0.784 and 0.864 for swarming, pyocyanin and biofilm. Only pyocyanin enters its measured interval. This conclusion is conditional on the baseline structure and mappings; it does not rule out dose differences under other model choices. Source: {{ model_file('results/cross_phenotype_dose.csv', 'cross_phenotype_dose.csv') }}.

Inverting the nominal swarming model instead gives a Cb-2 C4-HSL affinity near 158 nM. This is another parameter value that can reproduce one observation, not evidence of a measured affinity. Dose, binding architecture, spatial effects and extract activity remain alternative explanations. Source: {{ model_file('results/cb2_inversion.csv', 'cb2_inversion.csv') }}.

1.5 A relative dose hypothesis

Sweeping A16-2 dose produces a candidate range for a follow-up experiment. Each dose multiplier is relative to that draw's assumed baseline dose. It is not a multiple of a measured A16-2 production yield or of a recorded plate concentration.

Relative A16-2 doses from the nominal fit and successful joint scenarios.
Target reduction in swarm radiusNominal fitJoint medianCentral 90 % scenario range
50 %1.55×1.6×1.0–2.6×
90 %25.8×11×1.0–49×

The median half-inhibition estimate suggests investigating doses around the assumed baseline and the 1–3× region. In contrast, the broad 90 % range and the disagreement between nominal fit and joint median do not support a precise high-inhibition target. Discontinuous model responses may cross a target without attaining exactly that radius ratio.

Figure 5 · Sensitivity and the ALS-5 plateau prediction

β is varied in the joint analysis and has the largest absolute partial rank correlation for the 90 % dose requirement. The untreated C4-HSL level is more influential for the 50 % target in this analysis. Neither is directly measured here.

The nominal ALS-5 curve plateaus above 50 % of control. In 269 of 296 calibrated joint draws, the model does not reach half inhibition within its searched dose range (0.001–100 000 ng/µL). This is a model prediction, not evidence that ALS-5 saturates on a real plate. Testing it requires an ALS-5 dose series, distinct from the A16-2 series.

At the nominal 7.797 ng/µL baseline, A16-2's point-fit doses are 12.1 and 201 ng/µL for the two targets. These absolute values inherit the unverified dose convention and should not be interpreted as measured yield requirements.

The dose hypothesis is unvalidated. The reported range includes β and other input uncertainty but is conditional on successful calibration, the chosen scenario bounds, and the model structure. A recorded dose-response series is needed before using it as a design requirement.

These are recommendations for follow-up work. None has a reported result in this analysis.

For biofilm, distributing treatments and controls across shared plates addresses confounding that additional wells on isolated plates cannot resolve. For the retron route, active aptamer molecules produced per cell would complement the applied molar dose; total extract nucleic acid does not supply that quantity.

1.7 Assumptions and limits

  • Mechanism is unresolved. The phenotypes and non-significant BL21 extract comparisons do not establish growth independence, aptamer-specific activity or direct AHL sequestration.
  • Active dose is unknown. A crude-extract Nanodrop reading cannot establish the active dose for Cb-2 or either synthetic reference. The scenario scan does not recover that missing measurement.
  • Calibration is sparse. Two swarming observations determine two core parameters; two anchor observations per additional phenotype determine its two empirical exponents. Fitted affinities are effective parameters, not binding measurements.
  • Parameter transfer is conditional. Sequence identity, assay conditions, activation constants and untreated signal levels need direct verification. The exported scenario endpoints are not all established primary-literature bounds for these constructs or plates.
  • Structure is simplified. One-site competitive binding, total-pool ligand removal, a long-time state, and a well-mixed compartment omit possible independent binding domains, transient behaviour and transport. Post hoc patch averaging tests one alternative.
  • Uncertainty is conditional. Joint ranges exclude 104 failed calibrations and retain the chosen sampling design. The exploratory cross-phenotype ranges hold core occupancies fixed and are not full joint prediction ranges.
  • Replication remains uneven. Cb-2 swarming plates are separate from the specialists'; biofilm has two plates per treatment with shared-control and clustering concerns. Treatment rankings do not establish between-treatment differences.
  • Generalisation is unconfirmed. The nominal Cb-2 prediction misses; the joint range is broad enough to overlap the observation. No prospective dose-response test establishes predictive accuracy.
