  1. Dark read. The LED is off and the detector reads: under the shield every channel must be zero, or the cycle is void.
  2. LED on. Blue 470 nm light passes the baffle and crosses the sample's 2 mm path.
  3. A sample with sfGFP gives off green. The detector collects it at 90°, along the 10 mm path.
  4. The blue that passes straight through enters the light trap. Its end wall slopes at about 41° to the beam and turns the light up into the roof, not back at the sample.
  5. LED off after about 0.55 s, then a second dark read. A run is three such cycles.

Design advantages

The light is sorted without a filter, the seat places the cuvette, every read checks itself, and the parts change one at a time.

  • No emission filter 86 vs 0

    The detector looks at the sample from 90°, and its own colour bands keep the blue apart from the green. The two cultures read alike beside the source, 21.4 and 21.7 counts on F2; only the green differs. Dense suspensions are where this stops.

    Measured, one session · every channel of both reads
  • A seat that places the cuvette 1 step, 3–5 s

    The cuvette drops between eight ribs onto four corner tabs, which set its position and height. One tube, put back five times: 0.76% between insertions.

    By design; timed and measured on version 2
  • A read that checks itself dark, lit, dark
    Shielded 0560 kept
    Leaking 1 cycle void

    Every flash sits between two reads with the LED off. Shielded, those are exactly zero, so one that is not voids its cycle: eight in version 1's logs, one in the dilution series.

    In the firmware; counts from our logs
  • Parts that change one at a time 5 parts, 1 resistor

    That is all version 2 changed. The base plate, the detection side, the light trap and the firmware stayed as they were.

Functional testing

It tells a GFP culture from one without, and the reading rises with the amount of GFP culture; a plate reader beside it ranked the same tubes the same way. Without GFP it read zero in liquid culture, and a few counts in dense suspensions. It has not yet resolved AHL induction.

The GFP cultures read in this section are a test strain: E. coli TOP10 with a plasmid that makes sfGFP without induction. They stood in for our AHL biosensor, which we expected to be about as bright once induced. That induction has not been shown conclusively: in liquid culture both of its constructs fluoresced above an empty-vector control, with no uninduced culture beside them to compare (Engineering).

The dilution series, tube by tube What the wet lab said

Beside a plate reader (version 2)

Read beside a plate reader on the same tubes, it ranked them the same way, and over the three denser tubes its reading per unit of OD held within 10%.

We diluted one batch of the sfGFP test culture to five strengths, from 6.25% to 100%, and read every tube on our reader and on a SpectraMax M3 plate reader, which also measured its OD600. As the culture got stronger, our green reading rose from 2.44 to 86.44 counts, keeping close step with the M3’s OD600 (r2 = 0.993 across the five tubes). A culture without GFP, at an OD600 of 0.72, read zero on ours and close to background on the M3. Even the thinnest tube, at an OD600 of 0.07, gave a reading on every cycle; we did not try anything thinner.

Not yet This was one culture on one day, and it is not a calibration. Whether the two instruments’ fluorescence readings are in proportion is still open.

Light shielding

Under a shield the dark read is exactly zero, so the zeros above are readings, not corrections.

Under the shield, every channel reads zero while the LED is off, even at 25 times the usual integration time. In room light the leak does not scale with integration time, so subtracting a dark reading would not remove it.

With the LED on, plain medium has read zero on the emission channel every time on version 2: eight blank readings across four sessions, 60 valid cycles in all.

The operating rule: Measure in a darkened room or under an opaque shield. Because a shielded dark read is exactly zero, a dark read that is not zero marks that cycle as invalid: it is dropped and its raw row kept. The firmware applies this rule itself: it voided eight cycles in version 1's logs and one in version 2's dilution series.

Where it stops: dense suspensions (version 2)

In a dense suspension the reader gives a few counts with no GFP there. A culture without the reporter read zero while the scatter channel stayed under about 33 counts, and no longer did from 46.

We met this on suspensions scraped from AHL plates, the samples we were given for an AHL test, in the same E. coli TOP10. The wet lab grew culture on plates containing AHL at five concentrations, 0.22 to 1.1 µM by its own estimate, scraped each plate into suspension and gave us 200 µL of it. By eye it judged every plate induced but the 1.1 µM one. The reading followed how much culture was in the cuvette, not that judgement.

Two days later we took a culture without the reporter gene, scraped from one plate, diluted it in LB and read each dilution: the emission channel for what the culture itself gives, and the scatter channel of the same read beside it. LB alone read zero. Reading zero means below what this reader can register, not that nothing is there.

Where the two days overlap, the readings from plates judged induced lie within about a tenth of a count of a curve drawn through the readings without the reporter. sfGFP added to a dense suspension was still read: 23.56 counts, against 5.22 for that suspension alone.

Not yet This was one culture on one day, so the scatter figures are not a specification.

Construction and operation

Print it, wire it, and run it under a shield. Build version 2.

  1. Print

    Version 2 is 38 printed pieces, specified in matte black PLA; the full set of 52 adds loose shims, filter inserts, aperture disks and two fit coupons. Each can be saved as an STL file from the 3D view. The five new parts print without supports; on another printer, print the two fit coupons first.

    Parts and files Assembly, step by step

  2. Wire

    A 2N7000 MOSFET switches the 470 nm LED at about 10 mA through a 217 Ω resistor, and the AS7341 detector sits on the ESP32's I²C bus. Keep the gate pulldown: without it the LED can light while the controller boots.

    The circuit

  3. Run

    Any serial terminal at 115 200 baud, one command per line, under an opaque shield. This is one session from start to finish; the reader's answers are from the log of the dilution series on version 2, 2026-10-05.

    Once, before the first session

    The sketch ships with its LED gate off, and built that way the LED never lights. Build it with the gate on, then upload it. 

    arduino-cli compile --fqbn esp32:esp32:esp32doit-devkit-v1 --build-property "compiler.cpp.extra_flags=-DCAPTURE_LED_HARDWARE_ENABLE=1" firmware/esp32_fluorescence_reader
    On the benchYou typeThe reader answers
    1. Shield on Box over the reader. Reset the controller once, now, and not again.
      id
      # hw_enable(compile)1
# sensor_ready      1
# settings_readback 1

      Go on only if all three read 1.

    2. Empty cuvette, LED not armed The check that the shield is on.
      run

      dark 0LED off 0dark 0NO_SIGNAL;LED_DISABLED

      It stops after one cycle, as it should. All 36 values must be 0.

    3. Same cuvette, LED armed The day's empty reading, not a reference across days.
      arm ARM-LED-P1run

    4. Medium blank, 200 µL The day's zero.
      sample LOW_BLK_1run

      disarm

    dark is all twelve values of a read, before and after each flash: the sensor's ten channels, with Clear and NIR read twice. F4 is the three cycles with the LED on. Each cycle prints one CSV row of 66 fields: keep the terminal's whole log. Ours was kept by a script that stamps and stores every line; it is in the repository as firmware/host/serial_read_tool.ps1.

    When the reader says something else

    AMBIENT_PRESENT
    A dark read was not zero: light is getting in. Void the cycle, fix the shield, read again. Do not subtract it, and leave the voided row in the file. Seen 2026-10-05, at 6.25 µL: one channel read 1 before one flash of nine. Read again, all nine cycles were clean.
    # REFUSED: ENABLE is not in a defined acquisition state
    The detector lost power or was reset, and id shows settings_readback 0. Reset the controller once. If that fails, unplug the USB for five seconds, then check the detector's wires: SDA on GPIO21, SCL on GPIO22, and 3V3. Seen three times on version 2, on 2026-10-03 and 2026-10-09. A reset cleared it each time.
    NO_SIGNAL;LED_DISABLED
    The LED did not light. fault=NOT_ARMED: arm and run again. fault=HW_ENABLE_COMPILED_OFF: the sketch was built with its gate off.
    # REJECTED sample_id: longer than 24 chars
    A name takes up to 24 letters, digits and . _ - +. A rejected name leaves the last one in force: read the echo before run.
    SATURATED
    A channel reached full scale, 65 535. Dilute the sample.
    flag=OK with F4 at 0
    A valid reading. Keep it: medium has read zero every time.

Version 2: the dilution series, tube by tube 12 tubes, 4 controls Show Hide

200 µL to a tube. The sensor settings were version 1's. The LED ran through the new resistor, and its current was not measured that day.

Every tube without GFP read zero on the emission channel, the non-GFP culture included, as on version 1. From 25 to 200 µL, a range picked after the readings were seen, the seven tubes lie within −8.8 % and +14.5 % of one straight line through the origin.

Below that the reading falls short of the line: 12.5 µL read about half of what the line gives and 6.25 µL about a tenth, with a count of one or more on only 4 of its 18 cycles. That is the shape a floor in the reading would give, but one session does not tell a floor from other causes.

Version 1 had read zero for three of the four tubes at or below 0.19 on our concentration index, the stock's displayed OD scaled by its dilution in the tube, all in mixing session 2; 12.5 µL here is that same 0.19. The index leans on the displayed OD, and this session's stock read 0.77 at 1:4, above the 0.13 to 0.71 of the earlier sessions.

The 12.5 µL figure rests on the redone tube: with the first capture kept in its place, the planned analysis puts that level at 0.82 of proportional instead of 0.48, and the pooled form of that analysis no longer resolves a low-end shortfall. The 6.25 µL readings do not depend on it.

One 100 µL tube was taken out and put back five times, by wet-lab members. The five means ran from 49.33 to 50.33 counts, with a standard deviation of 0.38 counts between them: about the third of a count that a three-cycle mean can resolve. With the seat empty, every channel read zero.

Version 2: beside the plate reader, tube by tube 7 tubes, 2026-10-12 Show Hide

The sfGFP test culture, E. coli TOP10 with a plasmid that makes sfGFP without induction, diluted in medium. On our reader, 200 µL to a cuvette and nine cycles each, at the sensor settings of the dilution series; no tube was read twice. On the SpectraMax M3, OD600 and fluorescence at 485 / 515 nm.

One question was registered before the session: is our F4 in proportion to the M3's fluorescence, each tube's ratio within 20% of their mean? It could not be judged. The M3 could not read the denser tubes undiluted, so only 6.25%, 12.5% and 25% have a fluorescence reading of the registered kind. Across those three the ratio ran 0.298, 0.562 and 1.316 (×10−3): not in proportion, the thinner tubes reading low on ours, as the dilution series had led us to write down beforehand.

Worked out afterwards: F4 against the M3's OD600 gives r2 = 0.993 over five tubes, against its diluted fluorescence 0.982 over four, against its undiluted fluorescence 0.981 over three, and the tubes rank the same each time. These are a few points of one dilution series, where r2 runs high: they show one trend, not accuracy.

The M3's values were copied down by the operator; there is no export file, the number of wells and the gain were not recorded, and some values have two significant figures. The two instruments excite at different wavelengths, 470 and 485 nm, so only the trend can be compared, not the size of the numbers.

Version 2: the AHL plate session, cup by cup 12 readings, 2026-10-09 Show Hide

200 µL to a cuvette, nine cycles each, at the sensor settings of the dilution series. Sorted by F3. There was one cuvette to a condition and no plate without AHL, and how much was scraped was not measured: the session gives no dose–response and cannot say whether the plates were induced.

Medium read zero on the emission channel in all 18 cycles. The culture without the reporter did not: 1.78, at a density no earlier session had reached. Whether its cuvette had held culture before was not recorded.

Set side by side after the readings were seen: among the five readings with F3 from 94 to 104, the plate judged not induced read 5.22 and those judged induced 4.44 to 5.22. Among the three with F3 from 57 to 64, induced read 1.11, not induced 1.00, and the culture without the reporter 1.78.

The sfGFP culture, at a quarter of that scatter, read 66.56. Twenty microlitres of it in 180 µL of a dense suspension read 23.56, against 5.22 for that suspension alone: the dense culture did not hide it.

One test was registered before the session: that F4 rises in the order of the AHL concentrations. Read as registered, it does not hold (p = 0.64). Our script first reported that it did (p = 0.05), having used two repeat readings the registration rules out: both were asked for after the first reading was seen, and the 1.1 µM one was a second, denser scrape. Neither outcome bears on AHL. The same test on F3 gives the same p values, so what it ranks is how much was scraped.

Version 2: the density session, reading by reading 21 readings, 2026-10-11 Show Hide

200 µL to a cuvette, nine cycles each, at the sensor settings of the dilution series. A culture without the reporter gene, diluted in LB; per cent is the share of suspension. Sorted by F3.

The first suspension ran out after its 30 per cent tube, and the wet lab scraped a second from the same plate. The two do not line up: 30 per cent of the first scattered 14 to 15 counts, 40 per cent of the second 42 to 51. That is why the series as planned has no tube between F3 15 and 42.

The rule was registered before the session: take the densest tube whose cycles are all zero in both passes, and the thinnest with any cycle above zero. That gives 30 per cent of the first suspension, F3 14.50 over 18 cycles, and 40 per cent of the second, F3 46.28: zero at 41.78 in its first pass, 5 of 9 cycles above zero at 50.78 in its second. The six readings marked added were made after that result was seen, to fill the gap, and are not part of it. In the second pass the cups were told apart by eye, and the 40 and 50 per cent ones may have been exchanged; the table places each reading by its own F3.

Joined into a curve that is only assumed never to fall as scatter rises, these readings give the background of a culture without the reporter at any scatter up to F3 89, and the AHL plate session can be set against it. Its two readings without GFP that fall in that range lie +0.04 and +0.10 counts from the curve; the two from plates judged induced, −0.11 and −0.10. The four densest induced readings, F3 94 to 104, lie beyond the curve: if the background does not fall there, at most 0.56 to 1.33 counts are left. The cuvette with sfGFP added stands 19.76 above it. This was worked out after both sessions, with the two days' counts taken as they are; the curve is one culture on one day, not a correction to subtract. More on the Model page.

One plate, one day, one set of detector settings and no OD: the F3 figures are not a specification and do not carry to another reader or another day. How much GFP signal is left in a sample that thin was not measured.

The firmware stopped three captures partway, once on a detector time-out and twice on a bus error, each before a reading had been shown. The same cuvette was read again after a reset.

Version 1 against version 2: putting one tube back 6 series Show Hide

Version 1 held the cuvette against one spring leaf where the design called for two, which left it free across about 0.6 mm on one axis. Version 2's eight ribs leave 0.05 mm a side, as drawn. Taking one tube out and putting it back is the test of that. On version 2 the five insertions were made by wet-lab members, not by the member who built the reader.

In counts, putting a tube back on version 1 moved the reading five to seven times as much as leaving it alone. On version 2 it moved it little more than leaving it alone had: 0.38 counts on a mean of 49.87, or 0.76 %.

Test 4, in the AHL plate session, put an sfGFP culture back ten times: 2.7 %, against 1.8 % for the same tube left alone. That culture settles. Left alone its reading fell, and handling stirred it up again, so the wider spread cannot be put down to the seat. A first resting series, which the operator may have bumped, spread 3.3 %.

Version 1 against version 2: the LED current 2 tables Show Hide

Version 1 ran the LED at 5.553 mA, under the 8 to 12 mA the design aims for. On 2026-10-03 one 220 Ω resistor replaced the series pair, which brings it to about 10 mA. With an empty cuvette, the two channels beside the source then read about twice what they had, and the emission channel still read zero. Two days later the same held with samples in the beam: medium, a non-GFP culture and the empty seat all read zero on the emission channel, while that culture scattered up to 40 counts into the channel next to the source. The scatter off an empty cuvette roughly doubled and the emission channel stayed at zero in every one of these reads, which is what the 90° layout and the light trap are for.

Limits It is scattered excitation on an empty cuvette, not a fluorescence result, and it does not show that 10 mA is the right current. Readings at the two resistances cannot be pooled: the empty-cuvette ratio is not a conversion for fluorescence. The meter readings were taken during 0.55 s pulses, so they are a lower estimate.

Assembly, step by step 12 steps Show Hide

This is the order the design gives, and the reader's builder has checked it against how ours was put together. In this order a screwdriver can reach each of the 26 screws on the base, which the CAD record checks one by one. Pull the 3D view apart to see each part named here.

Every screw is an M3. Most cut their own thread in a printed pilot hole 2.7 mm wide: stop when the screw is snug, because PLA strips. Eight pass through clearance holes in both the part and the plate, and take a nut underneath.

  1. Clean the prints. Clear strings from the pilot holes, the sockets and the slots. The open-sided sockets on the detector frame, the slots in the baffle and the arrow engraved on the cuvette holder are meant to be there: do not fill them.
  2. Feet. One under each corner of the base plate, held by an M3×8 from above through the corner slot.
  3. Pins and keys. Into the plate's blind sockets: four round pins along the excitation axis, two for each riser; two narrow pins for the detector frame; two rectangular keys for the cradle.
  4. Risers. Seat the cuvette seat riser and the light-trap riser on their pins, and fix each with two M3×12 from under the plate.
  5. Excitation cradle. Onto its two keys, which set it across the beam and let it slide along it. Four screws through its slots, two in front and two behind, with nuts under the plate, only clamp it.
  6. Baffle and LED holder. Lay the baffle body and the LED holder body in the cradle's two saddles, facing as the fitting notes say, with a cap over each and two screws to a cap.
  7. Cuvette holder. Onto the seat riser, against its two edge stops. Four clips hold it down, two on the LED side and two on the side away from the detector, each with an M3×12.
  8. Detector frame. Onto its two pins, with a screw and nut through each of its two ears. Its rail and rear stop are what locate the detector holder.
  9. Sensor board. Into the carrier, sensor facing the sample, under the U-shaped retainer: two screws, the open side to the connector, only tight enough to keep the board in. The carrier goes into the back of the detector holder on four screws with large washers through its oversize holes. Leave them loose, slide the carrier to where the reading is highest, then tighten crosswise.
  10. Filter cassette. Against the front of the detector holder, its drawer opening towards the LED end, held by a clamp bar along its top edge and another along its bottom, two screws to a bar. Slide in an insert: ours holds no filter.
  11. Detector holder. Stand it against the frame's rail and rear stop and close the two side clamps on it, a screw and nut to each.
  12. Light trap. Onto its riser, with a D-shaped clamp plate over each of its two keyholes and a screw through each. Then the LED, its wires and the cuvette, as in the fitting notes, and the circuit.
Fitting the version 2 parts 5 steps Show Hide

These are the notes that went with the parts. They were written at design time and are not yet a record of an assembly that was checked step by step.

There are no photographs of the assembly: the reader was already put together when this page was written. The 3D view can be pulled apart to show where each part goes.

  1. Cuvette. Line up the arrow marking the UVette's 10 mm path with the double-headed arrow on the holder's top face, which runs along the detector axis. Lower it in vertically and push it to the bottom. Keep one orientation through a series.
  2. If the cuvette is tight. Sand the ribs in the pocket lightly, eight of them and each 1.6 mm wide. Do not sand the cuvette. A tight pocket can be eased; a loose one cannot.
  3. LED holder. The straight groove on its outer wall faces up, on the centre line of the baffle's flat top, and the wire notch at the back faces down. Push the LED in from behind until its flange meets the shoulder.
  4. Wires. Out through the notch, tied between the two flanges with a cable tie no wider than 2.5 mm.
  5. Cap. Two M3 screws into the cradle, holding the LED holder down.
The circuit, with its measured values 434 Ω and 217 Ω Show Hide

The excitation branch turns one LED3 on and off at a current that cannot damage it: a resistor-limited LED with an N-channel MOSFET on the low side, switched from one pin of the controller. It is a breadboard circuit that was measured, not a product design. The gate pulldown, R3, is the part that is not optional: without it the LED can emit while the controller boots, during what the firmware takes for a dark baseline.

Development log

Twelve steps, 2026-08-14 to 2026-10-12

  1. 2026-08-14 to 08-17

    The printed parts arrive

    The printer flagged walls too thin to print on one part, which was redrawn first. Of 59 pieces, 57 arrived; two were missing, and two broke as their supports were removed.

    What it changed We built with what we had, taped the two breaks, and wrote down where they broke for the next revision.

  2. 2026-08-25

    First light

    With the LED at 5.553 mA, a paper scatterer at the sample position read F2 = 87 and F3 = 81 against dark reads of zero. With nothing in the beam, stray excitation stayed under one count.

    What it changed The light path works, taped joints included. The UVette's orientation was fixed from here on.

  3. 2026-08-27

    The room-light test: room light gets in

    An empty UVette under ordinary room lighting read F4 = 11 with the LED off. In a darkened room, or under an opaque box, every channel read zero.

    What it changed A shield became mandatory, and “the dark read must be zero” became the rule that voids a cycle.

  4. 2026-09-03 to 09-04

    First fluorescence

    Running the project firmware, a culture reported to carry sfGFP read F4 = 19.58 ± 0.79 and its control read 0, over twelve cycles each. Twelve cycles are not twelve samples.

    What it changed The emission channel is usable. We had called that culture “induced”; no AHL had been added, and the word was withdrawn.

  5. 2026-09-09 and 09-14

    Two re-insertion tests, five days apart

    A GFP culture read 40.50 ± 1.23 over four insertions, with its control at zero. Five days later the corresponding culture read 32.67 ± 1.83, lower by 19.3 %. The instrument had been moved in between and the state of the culture was not known.

    What it changed A difference across days cannot be explained afterwards, so every comparison now carries its reference in the same session.

  6. 2026-09-16, 09-21 and 09-29

    Three mixing sessions

    GFP stock, non-GFP stock and medium, mixed at set fractions. Samples without GFP read zero every time, and in the two sessions with enough signal the reading rose in proportion to the GFP culture. A thin stock in session 2 left three tubes at zero.

    What it changed The stock has to be dense enough to clear the floor, and one session's slope cannot be carried to another as it stands. The Model page takes it from there.

  7. 2026-09-23 to 10-02

    The weak parts, redrawn

    The cuvette seat and the excitation end were redrawn: a pocket the cuvette drops into and lands in, an LED holder that grips the LED without a clip, and a baffle that prints on its side. With the cap that never arrived, that makes five parts to print. Two fit coupons were printed first, and the pocket and the socket that fitted by hand set the two dimensions a drawing cannot settle.

    What it changed The fractures became a check: each printed layer's cross-section against the fullest section within 3 mm of it. Where the parts broke that ratio was 0.63 and 0.32; the five new parts keep at least 0.82, against a threshold of 0.75. It is a geometric screen tuned on those two breaks, not a strength calculation.

  8. 2026-10-03

    Version 2 on the bench

    New printed parts were fitted, and one resistor was changed to take the LED from 5.55 mA to about 10 mA. With the same empty cuvette the rebuilt reader read F2 = 7.75 and F3 = 6.71 after an adjustment, inside the range version 1 had read, and about twice that once the current was raised. The emission channel stayed at zero throughout.

    What it changed The reader is no longer the instrument that produced version 1's bench and culture readings. Its fit, its alignment and its response to a culture have to be measured again before the two can be put side by side.

  9. 2026-10-05

    The dilution series: version 2 reads a culture

    One batch of GFP culture, undiluted and at five dilutions, with medium and a non-GFP culture as controls. The controls read zero on the emission channel. Looked at afterwards, the reading stayed close to one straight line from 25 to 200 µL of culture and fell short of it below that.

    What it changed Version 2 has culture readings of its own. They are one session with one batch, so they stand beside version 1's and are not yet a comparison with them.

  10. 2026-10-09

    The AHL plate session: the reading follows the culture

    Suspensions scraped from plates at five AHL concentrations, with medium, a culture without the reporter and an sfGFP culture as controls. The reading rose with how much culture was in the cuvette, whether or not the wet lab had seen the plate glow, and sfGFP added to a dense suspension was still read.

    What it changed Zero without GFP now carries a condition: it holds in liquid culture, not in a suspension this dense. Reading induction needs samples of matched density and one without AHL.

  11. 2026-10-11

    The density session: where zero ends

    A culture without the reporter, diluted in LB and read from thin to dense. It read zero while the scatter channel stayed under about 33 counts, and no longer did from 46.

    What it changed A few counts on the emission channel can be set against the scatter channel of the same read. An emission filter remains untested.

  12. 2026-10-12

    Beside a plate reader

    Five dilutions of the sfGFP test culture, read on our reader and on a SpectraMax M3. The two ranked the tubes alike, and over the three denser tubes our reading per unit of OD held within 10%.

    What it changed The reader has now been set beside a plate reader once. Whether the two fluorescence readings are in proportion is still open: the plate reader needs every tube read undiluted.

Decisions that narrowed the first prototype 5 decisions Show Hide

Recorded project decisions from 2026-08-09. The marked ones removed capability from the prototype on purpose.

The turbidity check is out of this prototype's scope narrows the scope
Red-light transmission and the turbidity check are removed. The far side of the excitation axis is occupied by the light trap, so there is no transmission path and the measurement is not physically implementable in this geometry.
470 nm excitation for the first round
Three independent design documents agree on the nominal 470 nm Cree LED, and the LED holder was dimensioned from that part's datasheet.
The UVette is a benchmark, not the product vessel
The reaction cartridge is a joint wet–dry decision that has not been made. Future containers will be handled with adapters rather than by modifying approved optical modules.
PlateDock is out of this prototype's scope narrows the scope
Petri-dish imaging and swarming analysis are held as separate future work. No CAD for it exists in any revision, and the effort belongs on validating one reader properly.
Angled-wedge light trap selected for manufacture
The stepped-baffle variant is deferred, not rejected. Both validate digitally and no bench evidence separates them, so the selection is a manufacturing choice and explicitly not a claim that the alternative is worse.
